Chapter 1: The Rollback
Mark sat up.

Or rather, he popped up, springing off the mossy ground without his right knee cracking like a dry twig or his lower lumbar screaming in protest.

He froze, sitting on his heels. No dull ache in his neck. No stiffness in his shoulder from that rotator cuff tear he’d earned in a kayak back in 2018. He took a deep, clear breath through his nose—no chronic sinus pressure, no wheeze.

He looked down at his hands. Smooth skin. Small knuckles. No arthritis. No calluses from forty years of typing or twenty years of chalking up at the climbing gym.

"Aha," Mark murmured. His voice was too high, crisp and light, missing the gravelly 60-year-old baritone he’d used to argue with product managers for the last three decades. "Massive stroke. Probable left-hemisphere bleed. Brain is dumping endorphins and running a childhood nostalgia sequence while the ICU team calls my time of death."

He flexed his fingers, making a tight fist, then stood up and bounced twice on his toes. He felt ridiculously light—maybe 110 pounds instead of his usual 185.

He picked up a nearby fist-sized river rock, feeling its weight, texture, and grain. "Basalt. Igneous. High cooling rate," he noted automatically, his general geology knowledge surfacing effortlessly. He looked at the nearby river—a roaring class-IV rapid churning white foam around exposed granite boulders. He instantly identified the pillow waves, the undercut rocks, and the safe eddy line on the far bank.

"Fascinating render," Mark said to the empty air, smiling faintly. "If I’m going out, at least my subconscious built a nice river for it. Way better than dying with Slack open."

A wild, horned beast burst through the thicket across the bank, roaring with real, damp heat that blew across his face.

Mark didn't flinch. He just crossed his small, teenage arms and watched it with the casual detachment of a veteran software architect watching a junior dev break the staging server.

"Go ahead," Mark told the monster. "Let's see what kind of combat routine my dying neurons cooked up."
