## Chapter 14: Peer Review & The Supply Chain

<br/>

A sharp, rhythmic knock rattled the heavy oak door of Mark's shed mid-morning, cutting through the quiet rasp of his needle-file.

<br/>

Mark set his magnifying lens down on the workbench. That knock was too crisp for Torren, who generally knocked the way other people kicked, and too confident for Pip, who usually just appeared.

<br/>

He unbolted the door.

<br/>

Vespera stood in the sunlight, her indigo cloak pulled tidy around her shoulders. But she wasn't alone. Beside her stood a girl who looked roughly Mark's age—fourteen or fifteen—wearing a canvas work apron smudged with silver polish and red jeweler's rouge. A leather tool roll bristling with fine engraving chisels, gravers, and calipers hung slung across her chest like a bandolier.

<br/>

She had sharp grey eyes, dark hair tied back in a messy knot, and an expression that made it abundantly clear she did not want to be standing behind a coal pit in the armory yard.

<br/>

"Mark," Vespera said, stepping inside without waiting for an invitation and gesturing to her companion. "This is Lyra. She is a journeyman engraver at the Guild-sanctioned Chapterhouse workshop. The youngest in the workshop's history."

<br/>

"By two full years," Lyra added flatly. "People tend to forget that part. I don't."

<br/>

Mark nodded politely, stepping back to give them room in the small shed. "Mark. Nice to meet you."

<br/>

Lyra didn't return the greeting. She crossed her arms, her grey eyes sweeping over Mark's cramped workspace—the dirt floor, the simple oak bench, the pile of salvaged bronze regulators, and the small, leather-bound notebook written in an indecipherable cursive.

<br/>

<br/>

Her gaze traveled up to the corner of the doorframe and stopped.

<br/>

"There is a spider," she said, "the size of my fist."

<br/>

"That's Legacy," Mark said. "She's senior to me. Please don't make eye contact. She's had a rough week."

<br/>

Lyra stared at him for a long moment, then turned to Vespera. "This is the 'genius' you were telling me about? He's a fourteen-year-old scrap boy who talks to spiders. He doesn't even have an engraver's vise."

<br/>

"I have a rock," Mark offered.

<br/>

"He has a rock," Lyra repeated to Vespera, as if submitting it into evidence.

<br/>

"I asked Lyra to come because she actually cuts the rune plates for our Chapterhouse," Vespera said, giving Mark a subtle, apologetic look. "If anyone in Oakhaven understands the physical casting process of Aether regulators, it's her."

<br/>

"I understand how to carve them *correctly*," Lyra corrected firmly, stepping up to the workbench. She pointed a finger at one of the repaired blower discs Mark had set aside for Torren. "Vespera told me you were messing with Guild-stamped fittings. I thought she was exaggerating, but this is a disaster."

<br/>

Mark leaned against the edge of his bench, adopting the calm, dry composure he'd used during code reviews for four decades. Every new job, without fail, came with one person who showed up in the first week to tell you everything you'd done was wrong. Usually they were right about at least a third of it. The trick was figuring out which third.

<br/>

"What's wrong with it?"

<br/>

Lyra picked up the silver-plated brass disc, holding it close to her eyes and tracing the grooves with a practiced thumb.

<br/>

"Where do I start?" she scoffed, pulling a brass caliper from her tool roll and snapping it open with the speed of a gunslinger. "First, the depth of this secondary trace is off by at least two-tenths of a millimeter. You filed away the original silver plating past Guild specification! Second, what is this dried gunk wedged between the parallel channels?"

<br/>

"Pine resin and dried parchment," Mark answered. "It acts as a physical insulator."

<br/>

"It's an un-sanctioned impurity!" Lyra shot back, her cheeks flushing with irritation. "High Imperial doctrine dictates that all rune channels must be open to the air to allow the Aether to breathe!"

<br/>

"Does it also need a lunch break?" Mark asked. "Paid time off?"

<br/>

Lyra ignored this with tremendous effort. "And worst of all..." She turned the disc over, glaring at the small lead solder point where Mark's copper wire was attached. "...you drilled a hole straight through the outer retention rim and attached a piece of common blacksmith's wire! That violates four separate manufacturing safety protocols!"

<br/>

"Only four?" Mark said. "I must be getting rusty."

<br/>

She set the disc back down on the table with a loud *clack*, looking at Vespera. "He's not an engineer, Vespera. He's a vandal. If a Chapterhouse Inspector saw this, they'd break his tools and revoke our workshop's license for even looking at it."

<br/>

"He has a rock," Vespera reminded her mildly. "They'd have to break the rock."

<br/>

Lyra gave her a look of profound betrayal.

<br/>

Mark let out a slow, quiet breath, picking up his magnifying lens and handing it to her.

<br/>

"Look through the glass, Lyra," Mark said.

<br/>

Lyra blinked, thrown off by his complete lack of anger. "What?"

<br/>

"Look at the center of the disc under the lens," Mark repeated, pointing to a discarded, burnt disc sitting in his scrap bin. "That's an un-modified Guild-standard disc that blew out on the timber mill blowers last week."

<br/>

Lyra hesitated, then grunted and pressed the magnifying lens to her eye, angling the burnt disc toward the sunlight.

<br/>

"Tell me what you see at the junction of the third and fourth traces," Mark directed.

<br/>

"It's melted," Lyra muttered. "The silver plate fused across the gap. It happens all the time. Aether flux spikes, the metal overheats, and the logic fails. That's why craftsmen buy replacement discs."

<br/>

"Why does it melt?" Mark asked.

<br/>

"Because Aether is volatile," Lyra replied automatically, quoting her workshop masters.

<br/>

Behind her, Vespera closed her eyes. "He's going to say something about printers."

<br/>

"I was going to say something about printers," Mark admitted.

<br/>

"What is a *printer*?" Lyra demanded.

<br/>

"You don't want to know," Vespera and Mark said, in perfect unison.

<br/>

Lyra looked between the two of them with deep suspicion, like a cat that had walked into a room and sensed that a joke had happened without it.

<br/>

"Aether isn't volatile; your trace layout is a bottleneck," Mark said gently, taking a piece of charcoal and drawing a clean schematic on the wooden workbench between them. "Look at the physics. Your Guild specification cuts the primary silver trace wide, but narrows the return channel by forty percent to create 'sacred resistance.' That resistance creates a massive thermal buildup right at the junction."

<br/>

He tapped the drawing with the charcoal stick.

<br/>

"The brass casing can't absorb heat fast enough. So the heat stays in the silver plate until the metal hits its melting point, jumps the gap, and locks the circuit in a short. Your 'specification' is literally engineered to destroy itself."

<br/>

Lyra stared at the diagram. Her brow furrowed, her grey eyes moving back and forth between Mark's drawing and the burnt disc under her lens. She knew the math of the traces—she spent ten hours a day carving them into brass—but no one in the Chapterhouse had ever explained *why* the lines were laid out in those specific ratios. They were just told to follow the master templates handed down from the Academies. Asking "why" had once earned her a week of polishing doorknobs.

<br/>

"The pine resin parchment stops the voltage from jumping the gap when it gets hot," Mark continued. "And the 'common blacksmith's wire' gives the excess thermal energy a path of least resistance—a drain pipe straight into the ground. That disc isn't a vandalized ruin, Lyra. It's a patch that keeps your work from melting."

<br/>

Lyra opened her mouth to argue, but the logic was suffocatingly clear. She looked at the modified disc sitting on the bench. The copper wire wasn't random clutter; it was positioned at the exact geometric point where thermal accumulation was highest.

<br/>

She pulled her calipers out again and measured the wire's position. Then she measured it a second time, as if hoping the first measurement had been lying.

<br/>

"It... it really is just a heat trap," she whispered, her prickly posture softening into genuine confusion. "The master templates... they're designed with a bottleneck."

<br/>

"Planned obsolescence," Mark said. "Good for Guild repair fees. Bad for hardware longevity."

<br/>

"We throw away three out of every ten discs we carve during testing because the silver bridges during the initial attunement," Lyra murmured, her eyes wide. "If we added a grounding channel during the initial casting phase... we wouldn't lose a single plate."

<br/>

"Exactly," Mark smiled faintly.

<br/>

"But that's not even the worst waste in your pipeline," Mark added, reaching into his tunic pocket.

<br/>

He pulled out one of the dull, slate-grey mana-crystal shards Torren had told him to throw in the slag pit, and set it on the workbench in front of her. A linen-wrapped sausage came out with it. He put the sausage back.

<br/>

Lyra eyed the pocket. Then she eyed the shard. She scoffed instinctively. "A dead power shard? What does that have to do with anything? Once the light goes out, the stone is drained. I throw a sackful of those down the workshop waste chute every evening. It empties straight into the river."

<br/>

"Watch," Mark said.

<br/>

He picked up his wearable leather wristband—Prototype 1.1—and buckled it around his forearm. He wrapped a thin silver wire around the grey stone, connected it to the copper plate on his wrist, and dropped into his rooted stance.

<br/>

He closed his eyes, initiated his diaphragmatic breathwork, and passed a smooth, low-amperage trickle charge through the wire.

<br/>

Lyra leaned forward, her mouth opening in sheer shock.

<br/>

Right before her eyes, the dull grey gravel began to pulse. A brilliant, needle-thin point of azure blue light flickered at the center of the stone, expanding rapidly through the crystalline facets until the entire shard was glowing with a vibrant, cool blue light.

<br/>

Mark unwrapped the wire and picked up the fully recharged mana-crystal, tossing it lightly in the air before catching it and setting it in Lyra's numb, open hand.

<br/>

The stone was warm, vibrating with clean, fully charged Aether.

<br/>

"It's not dead, Lyra," Mark said. "It was just at zero percent charge. Your Guild is throwing away perfectly good rechargeable cells because nobody taught you how to plug them back into the wall."

<br/>

Then he took the sausage back out of his pocket, unwrapped it, and took a large bite.

<br/>

Lyra stared at him. "Is *that* part of the ritual?"

<br/>

"It's part of the power budget," Mark said, chewing.

<br/>

Lyra looked at the glowing blue crystal in her palm, her breathing fast and shallow. She looked from the stone to Mark's quiet, sausage-eating face, then at Vespera, who was watching the exchange with a knowing, subtle smile.

<br/>

"By the Ancestors," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling. "You didn't just fix a blower. You... you completely broke the Chapterhouse supply logic. And the river. We've been throwing *this* into the river for *years*."

<br/>

"The fish are probably extremely well-lit," Mark said.

<br/>

She looked up at him, the skepticism in her eyes replaced by an intense, burning curiosity—the undeniable spark of a fellow builder recognizing someone who knew how the machine actually worked.

<br/>

"How did you figure this out?" Lyra demanded, stepping closer to the bench. "Who taught you this?"

<br/>

"Forty years of troubleshooting bad architecture," Mark replied with a dry grin.

<br/>

Lyra narrowed her eyes. "You're fourteen."

<br/>

"It was a very busy childhood."

<br/>

Lyra set the glowing crystal down carefully, her mind racing through the possibilities. "If my workshop master saw this... he'd either execute you for heresy or make you the Chief Artificer of the province."

<br/>

"Let's go with neither," Mark said. "I like my shed, and I like keeping a low profile. Which brings me to a business proposition."

<br/>

Lyra narrowed her eyes, leaning against the bench. "What kind of proposition?"

<br/>

"You work inside the Guild's primary production facility," Mark said, pacing his words carefully. "You have access to their scrap bins—discarded silver wire offcuts, burnt brass plates, and sacks of 'dead' grey crystals that your master thinks are trash."

<br/>

Lyra nodded slowly. "And the fish."

<br/>

"Leave the fish out of it," Mark said. "Don't throw the crystals down the chute. Bring the 'trash' to my shed. I will teach you how to read circuit schematics, how to calculate thermal resistance, and how to recharge depleted stones. In exchange, you give me first pick of the raw scrap materials before they hit the dump."

<br/>

Lyra looked at the modified disc, the recharged crystal, and the neat English diagrams in Mark's notebook. She had spent four years being told to carve lines without ever asking why. Mark was offering her the keys to the entire underlying machine.

<br/>

She reached out her hand, smudged with silver polish, and looked Mark dead in the eye.

<br/>

"Deal," Lyra said firmly. "On three conditions."

<br/>

Mark raised an eyebrow. "Three?"

<br/>

"One. You teach me how to make one of those wearable wristbands," she said, nodding toward Prototype 1.1 on his arm. "I want to see what it feels like to cast without a heavy staff."

<br/>

"Reasonable."

<br/>

"Two. My name goes on every schematic I help with. *Credited.*"

<br/>

"Attribution," Mark said approvingly. "You'll go far."

<br/>

"Three." She pointed at the corner of the doorframe without looking at it. "That spider stays on *that* side of the room."

<br/>

Mark glanced up at Legacy, who had not moved in an hour and gave no sign of having opinions about anything.

<br/>

"I can't make promises on her behalf," he said. "She's an independent contractor."

<br/>

Lyra stared at him. Mark stared back. Somewhere behind them, Vespera made a small noise that might have been a cough and might have been a laugh.

<br/>

"Fine," Lyra said, and shook his hand with a solid, workmanlike grip. "Two out of three."

<br/>

Mark laughed—a genuine sound that echoed off the stone walls of the shed.

<br/>

"Done," Mark said.

<br/>

He flipped open his ledger right then and wrote a line at the top of a fresh page. Lyra leaned over to look, squinting at the looping, alien script.

<br/>

"What does that say?"

<br/>

"It says 'Lyra, Journeyman Engraver, Credited Collaborator.'"

<br/>

She squinted harder. "That could say anything."

<br/>

"It could," Mark agreed. "It could also say 'Lyra, Journeyman Engraver, extremely rude about my rock.'"

<br/>

"Good," Lyra said, unbothered. "Both are accurate."

---

On the way out, Lyra paused in the doorway, looking back at the shed with an expression caught somewhere between disbelief and the beginnings of grudging respect. Vespera waited beside her.

<br/>

"I told you," Vespera murmured.

<br/>

"You told me he was strange," Lyra said.

<br/>

"And?"

<br/>

Lyra considered the shed. The rock. The spider. The boy eating a sausage over a glowing crystal at his workbench as if none of it were remotely unusual.

<br/>

"He's *extremely* strange," she said. "I'll be back tomorrow."

---

Late that night, after Vespera and Lyra had slipped back out into the quiet avenues of Oakhaven, Mark sat alone at his oak bench under the cool blue light of his recharged capacitor.

<br/>

He opened his leather ledger, picked up his charcoal stylus, and wrote in clean, encrypted English cursive:

<br/>

> *System Architecture Notes — Mark Vance*
> *Log 07: Supply Chain Integration & Stakeholder Onboarding*
> *Key Events:*
> *1. Peer review conducted with Lyra (Journeyman Engraver, Mages' Chapterhouse Workshop). Reviewer was thorough, hostile, and correct about roughly one-third of her findings. Excellent hire.*
> *2. Demonstrated thermal feedback mechanics and crystal re-charging protocol. Resistance from traditional dogma successfully overcome through empirical proof (and one sausage).*
> *3. Strategic Partnership Established: Secured direct access to Guild scrap streams (silver offcuts, discarded logic plates, discharged mana-crystals) in exchange for technical instruction in circuit design. Terms: wristband instruction, full attribution, spider containment (rejected).*
> *System Status:*
> *Resource pipeline fully secured. Material costs for Prototype 2.0 reduced to zero.*
> *Stakeholder Note: Lyra carries calipers at all times and is not afraid to use them.*
> *Next Development Phase:*
> *Begin design on dual-crystal logic arrays to enable multi-target kinetic subroutines.*

<br/>

Mark closed the ledger, unbuckled his wrist cuff, and set it neatly beside his tools. He lay back on his cot in the dark, listening to the soft rain pattering against the slate roof, completely satisfied with his new development pipeline.

<br/>

In the corner of the doorframe, Legacy shifted one leg, very deliberately, about half an inch toward Lyra's side of the room.
