## Chapter 9: Security Patches and Retaliation

<br/>

Mark sat on a wooden bench in the rear courtyard of *The Pine & Anvil*, his palm-sized ledger resting flat on his knee. The evening air was cool, smelling of damp timber, pine resin, and—faintly—chicken.

<br/>

The chicken smell was a fair cop. For the past hour, Mark had been drilling in the corner of the courtyard: sink into stance, drop the heel, push a pulse of Aether into the ground. Sink, drop, pulse. Over and over, two hundred repetitions, until the motion started to feel automatic.

<br/>

His audience had consisted of four chickens and a stable boy. The chickens had watched with deep suspicion. On his hundred-and-twelfth heel-drop, the pulse had come out a little stronger than intended, the dirt had given a faint electric tickle, and every chicken in the courtyard had leaped two feet straight into the air, squawking as if the apocalypse had come early.

<br/>

"What are you doing?" the stable boy had asked. "Stomping bugs?"

<br/>

"Unit testing," Mark had said.

<br/>

The stable boy had nodded slowly and gone to find somewhere else to be. The chickens were still glaring at him from the far side of the yard.

<br/>

<br/>

Mark tapped his charcoal stick against the paper, filling the page with neat, compact English cursive:

<br/>

> *System Architecture Notes — Mark Vance*
> *Log 02: Macro Compilation & Cognitive Caching*
> *Theory: Directing raw Aether in real time requires too much conscious calculation. Solution: Compile physical movements and breath patterns into pre-built subroutines. Like a Kung Fu form, a specific physical trigger (e.g., heel-drop + stance sink) should automatically fire a grounding pass without requiring manual mental evaluation.*
> *Test Status: 200 reps complete. Trigger firing reliably. One false positive (chickens). No casualties.*

<br/>

A soft scuffle of footsteps made him close the notebook and tuck it into his tunic.

<br/>

Pip, the skinny twelve-year-old errand boy Mark had saved in the alleyway, slipped through the rear gate. He was breathing heavily, his face pale beneath a smattering of freckles. He skidded to a stop in front of the bench, drew himself up, and announced—with the air of someone who had been rehearsing the line for the entire run over—

<br/>

"Message bus *delivering*."

<br/>

Mark bit down on a smile. "Calm down, Pip. Report. What's the incident?"

<br/>

"Jax went straight to his older brother, Garek," Pip said in a rush. "Garek's an enforcer for the Ironhead Syndicate down in the timber yards. Jax told him a foreign noble's kid humiliated him and broke his wrist."

<br/>

"I didn't break his wrist," Mark said mildly. "I barely configured it."

<br/>

Pip blinked. "Well, *he* says it's broken. He's got it wrapped up and everything. Garek's furious. He's waiting on the south road between the armory and the inn with two of his men. They know you work the coal pit!"

<br/>

Mark nodded slowly. It was a classic escalation loop—a minor failure in the field, reported up the management chain with the details embellished to save face, resulting in a gross over-allocation of resources. He'd seen whole departments reorganized on less.

<br/>

"How big is Garek?" Mark asked.

<br/>

Pip held his hand up well above his own head. He thought about it, then raised it higher. Then he stood on his tiptoes.

<br/>

"Got it," Mark said.

<br/>

"They're going to jump you after dark," Pip warned, clutching Mark's sleeve. "You can't go back to the forge tomorrow! You have to stay inside!"

<br/>

"Hiding doesn't resolve a security vulnerability, Pip," Mark said calmly. "It just leaves the exploit open until someone takes advantage of it. I appreciate the intelligence report, though. Go on home before your master misses you."

<br/>

"You talk really weird," Pip informed him.

<br/>

"I get that a lot."

<br/>

Pip stared at him like he had lost his mind, but Mark gave him a reassuring pat on the shoulder, sending the boy back out into the dusk.

<br/>

Mark stood up, flexing his young fingers. He didn't buy a sword—he had no interest in wielding a heavy piece of steel he wasn't trained for. Also, swords cost money, and his entire net worth currently consisted of four copper coins, a notebook, and a stick of charcoal.

<br/>

Instead, he walked over to a stack of firewood in the corner of the yard. He selected a thick, dense piece of hardwood offcut about a foot long, heavy as iron and completely dry, and slid it into the inner lining of his sleeve.

<br/>

From his pocket, he pulled a small linen pouch filled with fine iron dust he'd swept off Smith Master Torren's anvil earlier that day. Torren had watched him do it, asked what in the name of the Ancestors he wanted with anvil sweepings, and received the answer "science." The smith had considered this, shrugged, and said, "Fine. Don't eat it."

<br/>

*"Hardware prepared,"* Mark thought. *"Time to deploy the patch."*

---

Twilight had settled completely over Oakhaven by the time Mark walked down the narrow gravel lane bordering the timber yards. High wooden fences flanked the road, casting long shadows under the pale green light of the larger moon. A quarter-mile ahead, the blue glow of an Aether streetlamp marked the entrance to the main thoroughfare.

<br/>

Halfway down the lane, three figures stepped out from the shadows of a lumber stack, blocking the road.

<br/>

In the center stood a towering, broad-shouldered man in a studded leather jerkin. He had a thick, unkempt beard, a broken nose, and carried a heavy, iron-shod wooden truncheon rested lazily against his shoulder. Pip's tiptoe estimate, Mark noted, had been conservative.

<br/>

Jax hovered behind him, his "broken" wrist wrapped in enough dirty cloth to mummify a small cat, a smug, vengeful sneer plastered across his face. The third man was lean and wiry, tossing a heavy iron rivet from hand to hand.

<br/>

Mark stopped twenty feet away, hands resting in the pockets of his linen trousers. In his right pocket, his fingers found the mouth of the linen pouch and pinched up a generous dose of iron filings.

<br/>

"That's him, Garek!" Jax spat, pointing a finger. "That's the little bastard!"

<br/>

Garek took a slow step forward, his boots crunching on the gravel. He squinted at Mark, clearly unimpressed by the skinny, unarmed fourteen-year-old before him.

<br/>

"You've got a big mouth for a stray, kid," Garek rumbled, his voice like grinding millstones. "Jax says you put hands on him. In Oakhaven, when you mess with an Ironhead, you pay in copper... or blood. Since you don't look like you have two coins to rub together, we're taking it out of your knees."

<br/>

"I have four coins, actually," Mark said. "But they're earmarked for parchment."

<br/>

Garek stared at him. The wiry man stopped tossing his rivet. There was a brief, awkward pause, like a meeting where someone had just asked a question that wasn't on the agenda.

<br/>

Mark used it. He stepped into a low, relaxed horse stance, sinking his weight into his thighs and dropping his center of gravity, initiating his diaphragmatic breathwork.

<br/>

*Inhale: four counts. Pull ambient Aether into the chest.*
*Exhale: four counts. Route energy down through the legs, establishing a passive root into the damp earth.*

<br/>

"Garek," Mark said, his voice ringing with the quiet, cold authority of an auditor reviewing a failing project. "Your brother attempted to extort a child, lost a leverage match due to terrible balance, and ran to you to fix his mistake. You're deploying three men to handle a single teenager. It's an inefficient use of labor, and it reflects poorly on your syndicate's leadership."

<br/>

"Is he calling you a bad boss?" the wiry man asked.

<br/>

"Shut up," Garek said. His eyes flashed with anger. "Get him!"

<br/>

The wiry man lunged forward with a wild, telegraphed right hook, putting all his momentum behind the punch.

<br/>

Mark didn't flinch. As the fist shot forward, Mark executed a classic Kung Fu slapping deflection—a sweeping *Pak Sau*—catching the thug's forearm from the outside and guiding the strike harmlessly past his ear. But as he redirected the blow, Mark dropped his heel and fired his freshly compiled macro: a sharp, silent pulse of grounded Aether directly into the damp earth beneath the thug's lead boot.

<br/>

Somewhere far behind him, back in the inn courtyard, Mark was fairly sure a chicken flinched.

<br/>

A tiny, localized burst of thermal energy flash-boiled the moisture in the dirt beneath the man's heel.

<br/>

*Pop.*

<br/>

A small pocket of mud liquefied instantly. Coupled with his off-balance punch, the wiry thug's feet shot out from under him as if he had stepped on a buttered skillet, and he landed heavily on his hip in the rutted dirt.

<br/>

Garek blinked, startled by the sudden slip, but his instincts kicked in. He charged forward, bringing his heavy iron-shod truncheon down in a savage overhead arc aimed straight for Mark's shoulder.

<br/>

The wooden club came down fast, backed by two hundred pounds of muscle and at least one bruised family ego.

<br/>

Mark didn't try to dodge backward. Instead, he stepped *forward* into a low Bow stance, entering the space inside the weapon's effective reach. He brought both forearms up in a high, stacked cross-block, reinforced by the hardwood stake tucked inside his right sleeve.

<br/>

*Thwack!*

<br/>

The impact shook the air, but Mark didn't absorb the force in his arms. The moment the wooden club struck his defense, Mark dropped his rear heel hard into the dirt, sinking deeper into his stance to execute his grounding subroutine.

<br/>

The kinetic force of Garek's blow surged down through the hardwood stake, through Mark's aligned skeletal structure, and straight down his legs into the earth. The dirt beneath Mark's boots cracked faintly, absorbing the shock wave without bending his frame.

<br/>

Garek's eyes went wide as his heavy strike bounced off the boy's rigid, rooted frame. It was, from his perspective, like swinging a club at a fence post and having the fence post look mildly disappointed in him.

<br/>

"Root," Mark whispered.

<br/>

Before Garek could pull the truncheon back, Mark swept his hands downward, snapping his palms across Garek's lead wrist in a sharp, double-handed redirection. He caught Garek's wrist with his left hand and slapped his right palm—still coated with the pinch of iron filings from his pocket—flat against the bare skin of the enforcer's joint.

<br/>

Now came the second macro: *Thermal Output.*

<br/>

Instead of an open flame, Mark executed his heat subroutine through the conductive iron dust, targeting the sensitive nerve clusters running along Garek's forearm.

<br/>

*Zap!*

<br/>

A sharp, searing spike of localized heat shot straight into Garek's wrist tendon.

<br/>

"Aagh!" Garek roared, his fingers reflexively snapping open. The heavy iron-shod truncheon thudded uselessly onto the gravel.

<br/>

On the ground, the wiry man saw his opening. He hurled his iron rivet at Mark's head with all his strength.

<br/>

Mark tilted his head two inches to the left.

<br/>

The rivet sailed past his ear and struck Jax squarely in the middle of the forehead with a crisp, resonant *tonk*.

<br/>

"OW!" Jax howled, clutching his face with his good hand. "Whose side are you on?!"

<br/>

Without breaking motion, Mark stepped past Garek's lead foot, using a swift Kung Fu foot-sweep behind the enforcer's heel while thrusting his palm lightly against Garek's chest. Deprived of his weapon and offset by the thermal shock, Garek's heavy frame tipped backward, crashing onto his back in the dirt.

<br/>

In three fluid, economy-of-motion seconds, the enforcer was on the ground and his crew was neutralized—one of them, Mark noted, by friendly fire.

<br/>

Behind them, Jax turned white as a sheet, a red welt blooming on his forehead, backing away until his heels hit the timber fence.

<br/>

Mark stood over the syndicate enforcer, settling back into a calm, upright stance. His breathing was steady, his posture relaxed, his hands held loosely at his sides.

<br/>

"Here is the post-mortem on this encounter," Mark said, his voice cool and measured in the evening quiet. "Your strike was telegraphed, your root was weak, and your escalation path failed completely. Your team's communication was poor, and your ranged support"—he glanced at the rivet lying at Jax's feet—"has a targeting problem. I am a laborer trying to earn enough copper to buy parchment. I am not a threat to your syndicate unless you make me one."

<br/>

Mark stepped back two paces, hands returning casually to his pockets.

<br/>

Garek sat up slowly, cradling his scorched, aching wrist and staring at the dirt where Mark's boots had rooted. There was a tiny, faint scorch mark in the mud where the grounded Aether had discharged.

<br/>

"What are you?" Garek rasped, his voice trembling slightly. "You're no farm boy. You move like a Master from the East... or a battle-mage."

<br/>

"I'm an engineer," Mark replied dryly. "And my billable hours are way too high for this kind of nonsense. Take your brother and go."

<br/>

"What's an engineer?" the wiry man whispered from the ground.

<br/>

"Some kind of priest, I think," Garek muttered.

<br/>

Garek pushed himself up, glaring at Jax with sudden, furious irritation. He grabbed his younger brother by the collar of his shirt and dragged him down the lane toward the main road without looking back. Halfway there, Mark heard a distinct smack—the sound of an open palm meeting the back of a head.

<br/>

"*A noble's kid*, you said!"

<br/>

"He *talks* like one!" Jax protested.

<br/>

Mark watched them vanish into the blue light of the streetlamps. The wiry man limped after them, pausing only to retrieve his rivet, which he regarded with an expression of deep betrayal.

<br/>

Mark looked down at his hands, wiping the remaining iron dust off his palm onto his trousers. There was a faint, satisfying tingle in his forearm, but no headache, no burning nerves, and no cognitive hangover. Two hundred reps in front of a hostile chicken audience had paid off.

<br/>

He pulled his leather notebook from his tunic, flipped to his latest entry, and added a line in neat English cursive:

<br/>

> *Log 02 (Update): Macro execution successful. Root-and-Deflect subroutine deployed with minimal cognitive overhead. System performance: optimal.*

<br/>

He considered for a moment, then added a second line:

<br/>

> *Known Issue: Horse stance has exposed a structural weakness in the inseam of these trousers. Priority: medium.*

<br/>

Mark closed the book, tucked it away, and started his walk back to *The Pine & Anvil*, a faint, dry smile playing on his lips.

<br/>

*"No Slack,"* Mark thought happily as the cool night wind blew through his hair. *"No Brad. And my runtime environment is getting smoother by the day."*

<br/>

When he stepped back into the inn's courtyard, four chickens turned in unison to glare at him.

<br/>

*"Mostly smoother,"* he amended.
