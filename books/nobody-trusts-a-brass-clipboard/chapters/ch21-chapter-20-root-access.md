## Chapter 20: Root Access

<br/>

Vespera arrived at the Academy forty minutes after dawn, and Mark heard her coming from two corridors away.

<br/>

It wasn't her footsteps. It was the people getting out of her way. A steady wave of startled exclamations, doors banging, and one student loudly apologizing to no one in particular rolled down the West Wing toward the small dining alcove where Mark and Lyra were eating breakfast.

<br/>

Mark buttered his bread. "That'll be Vespera."

<br/>

"How do you know?" Lyra asked.

<br/>

"The Anchor fired again last night," Mark said. "There's exactly one person in this province who knows whose fault that is."

<br/>

Vespera swept into the alcove with her indigo cloak billowing, her staff in one fist, her face pale with a fury so pure it was almost serene. She stopped at their table and looked down at them.

<br/>

At Mark's forearms, wrapped wrist to elbow in strips of torn linen, bruised black and purple where the wrappings didn't reach.

<br/>

At Lyra's right palm, bandaged and spotted through with dried blood.

<br/>

At the bread, the butter, the porridge, and the two of them, sitting there eating breakfast as if nothing whatsoever had happened.

<br/>

For a long moment, Vespera didn't speak. When she did, her voice was very quiet.

<br/>

"You," she said, "are a *menace*."

<br/>

"You wrote that in your note," Mark said.

<br/>

"I am saying it to your *face*. It is *worse* to your face. I want you to *feel* how much worse it is."

<br/>

"I feel it," Mark said honestly. "Would you like some porridge?"

<br/>

Vespera sat down very abruptly on the bench across from them, set her staff against the wall, and put her face in her hands.

<br/>

---

<br/>

"Start," she said, through her fingers, "from the beginning."

<br/>

Mark gave her the calm, structured version. He'd written enough incident reports to do it in his sleep: timeline, root cause, impact, resolution.

<br/>

"At approximately twenty-one fifteen, the archive door was barred from the outside by Julian Thorne—"

<br/>

"—who *knew what was down there*," Lyra put in.

<br/>

"—who had prior knowledge of the risk," Mark agreed. "At approximately twenty-one twenty-five, an automated sanitation construct ascended from below the third landing—"

<br/>

"It was *seven feet tall*," Lyra said. "It *crushed* First Era plates like they were *biscuits*."

<br/>

"—and began destroying source material. I engaged it with a kinetic macro, which was ineffective—"

<br/>

"It threw him over a *table*."

<br/>

"—and we transitioned to an evasion strategy, during which Lyra identified a critical detection weakness—"

<br/>

"It can't see Guild magic," Lyra told Vespera, her eyes shining. "Only clean magic. All this time, the Restriction's been *hiding* us."

<br/>

Vespera lowered her hands slowly from her face.

<br/>

"—after which we deployed a honeypot, which failed," Mark went on, "followed by a coordinated takedown in which Lyra dropped a tower of eleven thousand plates onto the construct—"

<br/>

"From a *shelf*," Lyra said.

<br/>

"—and threaded a silver lead onto its core through a two-tenths-of-a-millimeter seam in its armor, while I performed a reverse-polarity drain in Beta mode—"

<br/>

"While its hand," Lyra said, holding up her bandaged palm and miming fingers closing slowly, "was *reaching for my face*. It got *this close*." She held her thumb and forefinger an inch apart.

<br/>

Vespera was gripping the edge of the table with both hands now.

<br/>

"Resolution," Mark concluded. "Core drained to zero. Construct offline. Two minor injuries. Approximately three First Era plates lost." He paused. "And Lyra named it Gerald."

<br/>

There was a very long silence.

<br/>

"Why," Vespera said at last, in the faint voice of a woman who no longer had the energy to be surprised by anything, "is it named *Gerald*?"

<br/>

"Gerald's dead," said Mark and Lyra, in perfect unison.

<br/>

Vespera stared at them both. Then she reached across the table, took Mark's bowl of porridge, pulled it in front of herself, and began to eat it without another word.

<br/>

Mark decided that was fair.

<br/>

---

<br/>

Inspector Valerius arrived an hour later, accompanied by two Enforcers and, to Mark's lack of surprise, Archmage Ignis.

<br/>

Ignis was in a magnificent mood. He swept into the West Wing courtyard in his crimson robes, his oiled beard practically quivering with anticipation, and pointed at Mark across the flagstones like a man identifying a culprit in a play.

<br/>

"*There* he is," Ignis announced. "Inspector, the Anchor flares on two separate occasions, and on both occasions—*both*—the boy is within a hundred yards of it. I warned you. Clean execution! Vulgar shortcuts! I *said* no good would come of it!"

<br/>

Valerius regarded Ignis with the flat, weary expression of a man who had been listening to this for the entire carriage ride.

<br/>

"Apprentice Vance," Valerius said. "Where were you last night?"

<br/>

"At my assigned disciplinary duty, sir. In the archive. As ordered by Archmage Ignis."

<br/>

Ignis's mouth opened. It closed again. Mark watched him work out, in real time, that he couldn't accuse Mark of being near the archive without admitting he'd personally sent him there.

<br/>

"And did anything happen during this duty," Valerius asked, "that might account for a severe zero-impedance surge, a localized thermal collapse, and a second alert on a First Era Anchor?"

<br/>

Mark looked at Lyra. Lyra looked at Mark. Vespera, standing behind them, very quietly closed her eyes.

<br/>

"Yes, sir," Mark said. "It might be easier to show you."

<br/>

---

<br/>

Mark led them down the long spiral staircase to the first landing, bracing himself the entire way.

<br/>

He had the explanation ready. He'd been building it in his head since dawn: the construct, the plates, the ancient defense mechanism awakened by an unknown trigger, a brave disciplinary apprentice and his Engraving Specialist acting in self-defense to protect Academy property. It wasn't a lie, exactly. It was a carefully scoped truth. He'd delivered a hundred of them in boardrooms.

<br/>

He reached the bottom of the stairs, stepped through the iron door into the archive, and stopped dead.

<br/>

The archive was spotless.

<br/>

The shattered shelves had been repaired, or replaced so precisely Mark couldn't tell which. The cracked flagstones were swept clean. The frost was gone. The eleven thousand scattered plates had been gathered up and restacked into the leaning tower in the back corner. It stood as tall and precarious as ever, propped up by a broom.

<br/>

A *new* broom.

<br/>

The old iron-bound cases were gone. Not empty, not moved. *Gone*. The section of floor where they had stood for centuries was bare stone, clean and dust-free, as if nothing had ever been there.

<br/>

And the construct—seven feet of silver-white metal frozen mid-reach—was nowhere at all.

<br/>

Archivist Mott looked up from his desk as they filed in, and beamed.

<br/>

"Lovely and tidy, isn't it?" he said proudly. He peered at the Inspector, then at Ignis, then at the Enforcers. "Are you all new ones?"

<br/>

Mark stood in the middle of the immaculate room. Beside him, Lyra's mouth had fallen open.

<br/>

In forty years of operations, Mark had seen every kind of production incident there was. He'd seen outages, breaches, corrupted databases, cascading failures, and one memorable occasion when a junior developer had deleted the backups *of* the backups. But he had never once, in his entire career, seen a production incident clean itself up.

<br/>

It was not comforting.

<br/>

"*Well*," Ignis said, his voice swelling with triumph. "Where is it, boy? This great disturbance? This terrible event?" He spread his arms wide, turning in a slow circle to take in the clean, orderly, undisturbed archive. "I see *nothing*! I see a well-kept archive and an apprentice who has invented a fantasy to excuse his failure to perform a simple duty!"

<br/>

"Sir—" Lyra began.

<br/>

"*Fabrication*," Ignis declared, delighted. "Inspector, I trust this concludes the matter. The boy is a fraud and a liar, exactly as I said." He swept back toward the stairs, crimson robes swirling. "I shall expect a full disciplinary report on my desk by noon."

<br/>

He was gone, his footsteps echoing triumphantly up the staircase.

<br/>

The Enforcers followed him. Mott returned to his ledger.

<br/>

Valerius did not move.

<br/>

---

<br/>

The High Inspector stood in silence for a long moment in the center of the clean archive. Then, slowly, he crossed to the spot near the stairs where Mark had stood, blocking the construct's first blow.

<br/>

He crouched. He pulled off one glove and ran his bare fingertips across the floor.

<br/>

The flagstones had been swept clean. But they had not been replaced. Mark could see it from where he stood: a spiderweb of fine cracks radiating outward through the stone, exactly where he had dropped his heel and driven the force of the construct's strike into the floor.

<br/>

Valerius traced one of the cracks with his finger. Then he drew his brass detection rod and held it an inch above the stone.

<br/>

The needle twitched. Faintly. Then held.

<br/>

"Residual frost," Valerius said quietly, to no one in particular. "Deep in the stone. Not a natural chill." He stood up, slowly, pulling his glove back on, and turned to look at Mark.

<br/>

His face was as cold and unreadable as ever. But something in his eyes had changed.

<br/>

"The Anchor in Deep Vault Three," Valerius said, "now has two glyphs lit. One red." He paused. "And one gold."

<br/>

Lyra drew in a sharp breath.

<br/>

"I have audited this province for twenty years, Apprentice Vance," Valerius continued. "I have studied every record of the Anchor that exists. Every report. Every flare." He held Mark's gaze. "There is no record anywhere of the gold glyph ever having been lit."

<br/>

He let that sit in the silence for a moment.

<br/>

Then he turned and walked toward the stairs. At the foot of them, without looking back, he said:

<br/>

"I will write in my report that a minor seismic event disturbed the archive shelving and was promptly remedied by the archivist. Good morning, Mage Vespera. Journeyman."

<br/>

His footsteps climbed away into the dark.

<br/>

Mark, Lyra, and Vespera stood in silence in the empty archive.

<br/>

"Was that," Lyra whispered at last, "a *threat*?"

<br/>

"I don't think so," Mark said slowly.

<br/>

"Was it a *warning*?"

<br/>

"Maybe."

<br/>

"Was it *help*?"

<br/>

Mark looked at the cracked stone under his feet, the faint frost that only a detection rod could find, the space on the floor where the old cases had been.

<br/>

"I genuinely have no idea," he said. "Which, honestly, is the most worrying thing that's happened today."

<br/>

Behind them, at his desk, Mott looked up.

<br/>

"Lovely and tidy," he said again, contentedly. Then, after a moment's thought: "Bit of a mystery, though, who did it. Wasn't me."

<br/>

All three of them turned slowly to stare at him.

<br/>

But Mott had already gone back to his ledger, and whatever he knew, if he knew anything at all, he was keeping it under several layers of grey wool.

<br/>

---

<br/>

Julian Thorne found Mark that afternoon in a quiet corridor near the library, alone.

<br/>

He was not turning his ring. His hands were clasped behind his back, and he was smiling his usual composed, superior smile. Mark noticed that his eyes were slightly shadowed, as if he hadn't slept.

<br/>

"Vance," Julian said.

<br/>

"Thorne."

<br/>

"I heard you told the Inspector quite a story this morning." Julian's smile widened a fraction. "And I heard the archive was immaculate. Not a plate out of place." He tilted his head. "That must have been very embarrassing for you."

<br/>

"It was a little embarrassing," Mark agreed.

<br/>

"So you understand, then," Julian said pleasantly, "that you have no proof of anything. No construct. No plates. No witnesses but a girl from the workshop and a deaf old man who thinks everyone is new. If you were to accuse me of anything—anything at all—you would sound exactly like what Ignis already thinks you are." He stepped a little closer. "A lying scrap boy with delusions."

<br/>

Mark considered him for a moment.

<br/>

"I keep a ledger," Mark said.

<br/>

Julian's smile flickered. "I'm aware. Everyone's seen you scribbling in it."

<br/>

"Everything goes in the ledger," Mark said. "The time you barred the door. What you said through the grate, word for word. 'Keepers of the Measure.' 'Five hundred years.' 'More efficient.'" He watched Julian's face carefully. "The imprint your ring left on the iron latch. A circle cut by a single line. It's the same seal as on the Concord's decree, which I also copied out, word for word."

<br/>

Julian had gone very still.

<br/>

"And here's the thing about my ledger," Mark went on, in the same mild, pleasant tone he used to use in contract negotiations, right before the other side realized they were going to lose. "It's written in a language that nobody in this world can read. Nobody but me. You can't read it. Your father can't read it. The Concord can't read it. You could steal it, and it would do you no good at all. You could burn it, and I've got a very good memory and plenty more parchment." He paused. "And if anything happens to me, there are people who know where it is, and know who to show it to, and know exactly what it's about."

<br/>

That last part wasn't technically true yet. But Mark had always believed in writing the documentation first and building the system to match it later.

<br/>

"So here's where we are," Mark said. "If you expose what I do, you have to explain how you knew there was something down there to expose. If I expose what you did, you have to explain why your family has been keeping a five-hundred-year-old secret about the Empire's magic. Neither of us can move first."

<br/>

He smiled faintly.

<br/>

"Back home," Mark said, "we'd call that mutually assured documentation."

<br/>

Julian clearly had no idea what that meant. But Mark could see in his eyes that he understood it perfectly.

<br/>

For a long, cold moment, they faced each other in the empty corridor.

<br/>

Then Julian's composed expression slipped. Not much. Just for a second. And in that second, he didn't look superior or cruel. He looked tired. And young. And very, very serious.

<br/>

"You think the Measure is a cage, Vance," he said quietly. "Something built to keep you small. To keep you weak."

<br/>

"Isn't it?"

<br/>

"No," Julian said. "It's a dam."

<br/>

Mark frowned. "A dam against what?"

<br/>

Julian looked at him for a long moment.

<br/>

"You have no idea what's on the other side of it," he said. "None of you do. And I pray to the Ancestors you never find out."

<br/>

He turned and walked away down the corridor. He didn't look back. And, Mark noticed, he was turning the ring again—slowly, around and around, as if it had become heavier overnight.

<br/>

Mark stood alone in the corridor for a long while after he'd gone.

<br/>

*"A dam,"* he thought. *"Not a firewall to keep users out of the system. A firewall to keep something else out of the network."*

<br/>

He didn't like that thought at all. He liked even less that it made a certain amount of sense.

<br/>

---

<br/>

On the way back to the West Wing, he passed Corwin, sprawled full-length on his usual sunny bench in the courtyard. Corwin cracked one eye open, took in Mark's bandaged forearms, and opened the other eye too.

<br/>

"You went below the third landing," Corwin said.

<br/>

"No," Mark said. "It came up."

<br/>

Corwin was quiet for a moment. Then he closed both eyes again.

<br/>

"That's worse," he said.

<br/>

"Yes," Mark agreed. "It really is."

<br/>

---

<br/>

They needed somewhere to talk. Somewhere private, somewhere nobody would think to listen, and somewhere that had absolutely nothing to do with the Academy, the Chapterhouse, or House Thorne.

<br/>

There was only one place Mark could think of.

<br/>

It was the Academy's rest day. That afternoon, Mark, Lyra, and Vespera walked across Oakhaven together, down the cobbled avenues, past the plaza with its tax decree and its goats, through the lower district, and into the south armory yard.

<br/>

The yard was humming. Literally. Four furnace blowers sat in a neat row beside the forge, purring away in perfect synchronization: Bessie, Mabel, Dot, and Gertrude, all running clean and cool and quiet. The two apprentices were in the shade, supervising with their eyes closed.

<br/>

Smith Master Torren looked up from his anvil, saw Mark, and broke into an enormous grin.

<br/>

"*Kid!*" he roared, dropping Hammer with a clang and striding across the yard. "Back from magic school already? They throw you out?" He reached Mark, raised a massive hand to slap him on the back—and stopped, his hand frozen in midair, staring at the linen wrappings and the bruises.

<br/>

His grin vanished.

<br/>

"What," Torren said, in a very different voice, "*happened* to your arms?"

<br/>

"Long story," Mark said. "Can we use the shed?"

<br/>

Torren looked at Mark's arms. Then at Lyra's bandaged hand. Then at Vespera's pale, grim face.

<br/>

"Aye," he said quietly. "Course you can."

<br/>

---

<br/>

Mark pulled open the heavy oak door of the shed and stepped inside.

<br/>

Everything was exactly as he'd left it. The oak bench. The dirt floor. The small high window. The cot. And in the corner of the doorframe, a large spider in a large web, who turned all eight of her eyes toward him the moment he stepped through the door.

<br/>

Mark looked up at her. He found, to his mild surprise, that his throat had gone a little tight.

<br/>

"Hi, Legacy," he said quietly. "I missed you."

<br/>

Legacy did not respond. But she didn't turn away either. And after a moment, she moved one leg, very slightly, in his direction.

<br/>

"I've been calling her Margaret," Torren confessed from the doorway.

<br/>

"I know," Mark said. "I heard you. On the way out."

<br/>

"She doesn't seem to mind."

<br/>

Legacy shifted, very deliberately, to put her back to Torren.

<br/>

"She minds," Mark said.

<br/>

---

<br/>

Once they were all crammed inside—Mark on the stool, Lyra on the cot, Vespera standing rigidly by the window, Torren filling the doorway—Mark told the whole story again.

<br/>

It took a while. Torren interrupted frequently.

<br/>

He was horrified by the construct. He was outraged by Julian. He was impressed beyond words by the tower of plates and the two-tenths-of-a-millimeter seam, and he made Lyra describe the measurement three separate times.

<br/>

But when Lyra got to the part about naming the construct, Torren went completely still.

<br/>

"You named it," he said slowly, "*Gerald*."

<br/>

"I did," Lyra said, a little defiantly.

<br/>

Torren stared at her. Mark watched a remarkable sequence of emotions cross the smith's face. Shock. Confusion. Something that might have been hurt. Then something else, warm and profound, rising up through all the rest.

<br/>

He took off his cap and held it over his heart.

<br/>

"That," Torren said thickly, "is the finest thing anyone's ever done for him."

<br/>

Lyra blinked. "I—you're welcome?"

<br/>

Torren nodded, swallowed hard, and put his cap back on. "Twelve years holding down my wagon," he murmured. "And now his name took down a war machine." He sniffed. "He'd be so proud."

<br/>

Vespera, at the window, looked at Mark with an expression of mute despair.

<br/>

"This happens a lot," Mark told her. "You get used to it."

<br/>

---

<br/>

"There's one more thing," Lyra said, when Torren had finished composing himself.

<br/>

She reached into her apron.

<br/>

Mark watched her hand go past the first pocket. The second. Then the ninth, the tenth, the eleventh. Finally, her hand slid somewhere deep behind the apron's bib, into a pocket Mark had never seen and wasn't entirely sure was there.

<br/>

"The twelfth pocket," Mark said slowly.

<br/>

"The secret one," Lyra agreed.

<br/>

She drew out something about the size of a fist and set it on the oak workbench with a soft, heavy *clunk*.

<br/>

It was a crystal. Dull. Grey. Opaque. The lattice was faceted and enormous, far larger than any mana-crystal Mark had ever seen.

<br/>

The construct's core.

<br/>

Vespera actually took a step backward. Torren's mouth dropped open.

<br/>

"*When*," Mark said, staring at it, "did you take that?"

<br/>

"Last night," Lyra said. "While you were eating both sausages and having a moment about the meaning of the Measure. It came out of the chest housing fairly easily once I got my graver in properly. I'd already found the seam." She looked at it, then at him, a little defensively. "I thought we might need *something* left over. In case anyone tidied up." She crossed her arms. "And apparently someone did."

<br/>

Mark looked at the core. Then at Lyra.

<br/>

"Whoever cleaned that archive," he said slowly, "took the cases. The broken plates. Seven feet of construct. They didn't miss a thing." He picked up the grey crystal, turning it carefully in his bruised hands. "Except the most important piece of evidence in the entire building. Because it was in your apron."

<br/>

"You put mana-crystals in a cheese," Lyra said. "I put a construct's heart in my apron. We all have our methods."

<br/>

Mark set the core down on the bench, very carefully, in the exact center.

<br/>

Everyone in the shed looked at it.

<br/>

"Nobody," Mark said, "charges that."

<br/>

"*Nobody* charges that," Lyra agreed instantly.

<br/>

"Not even a little," Vespera said faintly.

<br/>

"What happens if you charge it?" Torren asked.

<br/>

All three of them turned to look at him.

<br/>

"Never mind," Torren said. "Don't charge it. Got it."

<br/>

Up in the doorframe, Legacy shifted, very slightly, away from the crystal. Mark took that as a unanimous vote.

<br/>

---

<br/>

The light through the high window had gone gold with late afternoon. For a long moment, nobody in the shed said anything at all.

<br/>

Mark looked around at them: Lyra on the cot, her bandaged hand in her lap. Torren in the doorway, arms crossed, his face unusually serious. Vespera at the window, staring out at nothing, her fingers wrapped white around her staff.

<br/>

Then he picked up his brass clipboard, cleared his throat, and stood up.

<br/>

"Right," he said. "I'd like to call this meeting to order."

<br/>

Lyra looked at the clipboard. "Is that an *agenda*?"

<br/>

"It's an agenda," Mark confirmed. "Item one. Scope."

<br/>

"You wrote an *agenda*?"

<br/>

"I've been in a lot of kickoff meetings," Mark said. "Old habits. Item one, scope. Somebody deployed a patch on all magic in the Empire five hundred years ago. It throttles every caster alive. It's enforced by automated constructs, maintained by at least one noble house, and possibly protecting us from something none of us understand. Our goal is to find out who, why, and whether it can ever be safely removed."

<br/>

"That's a very large scope," Lyra said.

<br/>

"It's the largest scope I've ever written," Mark admitted. "And I once worked on a project called 'Re-Platform Everything.'"

<br/>

"Wait," Vespera said.

<br/>

They all looked at her. She'd turned from the window. Her face was set, her jaw tight.

<br/>

"If we are going to do this," Vespera said, "it must be done *properly*. A pact of this weight cannot simply be—" she gestured at the clipboard with visible distaste "—*itemized*. There must be a formal oath. A binding vow, sworn before witnesses in High Imperial, sealed by flame." She paused. "And a small amount of blood."

<br/>

There was a short silence.

<br/>

"How small?" Lyra asked warily.

<br/>

"Traditionally, a drop from each palm."

<br/>

Lyra held up her bandaged hand. "I've already *given* a drop from this palm. Several, actually. To a murder statue."

<br/>

"I'd like to propose we skip the blood," Mark said.

<br/>

"Seconded," said Lyra, immediately.

<br/>

"Thirded," said Torren, from the doorway.

<br/>

"You are not part of the pact," Vespera told Torren.

<br/>

"I'd like to be," Torren said. "Facilities."

<br/>

Mark considered this. "Torren can join as Facilities," he decided. "On one condition."

<br/>

"Name it."

<br/>

"You do not, under any circumstances, name the project."

<br/>

Torren looked deeply wounded. "Why not?"

<br/>

"Because you named a blower Bessie. And a ballast block Gerald. And a spider Margaret, even though she already *has* a name."

<br/>

"Those are good names!"

<br/>

"They're *great* names," Mark said. "That's the problem. If you name the project, it'll be something wonderful, and everyone will get attached to it, and then something terrible will happen to it."

<br/>

Torren opened his mouth to argue. Then he thought about Gerald, and closed it again.

<br/>

"Fair," he said gruffly.

<br/>

"Item two," Mark went on. "Credit."

<br/>

"*Full* attribution," Lyra said at once. "In the charter. In writing. In whatever language that is."

<br/>

"Granted. Item three. Team name."

<br/>

"*Skunkworks*," Mark proposed. "It's a classic. Back home, it's what you call a small, secret team working on something nobody's supposed to know about."

<br/>

"What's a skunk?" Torren asked.

<br/>

Mark explained what a skunk was.

<br/>

There was a brief, uncomfortable pause.

<br/>

"I don't love it," Lyra said.

<br/>

"It's named after a *smell*?" Vespera asked, appalled.

<br/>

"It's not *about* the smell," Mark said. "It's—historically, there's a whole—" He looked at their faces. "Fine. It's about the smell. Withdrawn."

<br/>

"We could name it after the cheese," Lyra offered.

<br/>

Everyone pointedly ignored her.

<br/>

"We'll table the name," Mark said, making a note. "Item four." He looked up from his clipboard, and his voice changed. It got quieter. "Risk."

<br/>

---

<br/>

The shed went very still.

<br/>

"I'll say it plainly," Mark said. "This is dangerous. House Thorne knows what we found. They have a ring that tells them when someone runs clean code, and they're willing to let things below the third landing deal with it. Somebody—we don't know who—can make an entire archive's worth of evidence vanish overnight without a trace. The Anchor has a gold glyph lit that's never been lit before. And Julian says there's something on the other side of the Measure that's worse than the Measure itself."

<br/>

He looked at each of them in turn.

<br/>

"Lyra. You're a journeyman with a career and a future. Torren. You have a forge, a business, and four blowers who rely on you. And Vespera." His gaze stopped on her. "You're a sworn mage of the Guild. You took an oath to it. Joining this is treason, plain and simple. If anyone finds out, there's no Statute 402 that'll cover it."

<br/>

Vespera didn't answer at once.

<br/>

She looked down at her staff. Hundred-year-old ironwood, cured in salt, with a core of refined silver. Three years at the Academy to attune it to her blood. Mark remembered her telling him that, in the common room of *The Pine & Anvil*, with a kind of quiet, unquestioning pride.

<br/>

"I have spent my entire life," Vespera said slowly, "enforcing compliance. Every rule. Every template. Every Sacred Restriction. When a craftsman's fitting failed, I was the one who came to collect the fee for re-attuning it. When a student's hands blistered from thermal bleed, I was the one who told them it was the price of the Art." She was quiet for a moment. "I was *good* at it. I was proud of being good at it."

<br/>

Her fingers tightened around the staff.

<br/>

"And it never once occurred to me," she said, "to ask what I was complying *with*."

<br/>

She looked up at Mark.

<br/>

"I am in," Vespera said. "Treason or not."

<br/>

"I'm in," said Lyra.

<br/>

"Facilities," said Torren firmly, "is in."

<br/>

Mark looked at the three of them for a long moment. Then he nodded, once, and set the brass clipboard flat on the oak workbench beside the grey construct core.

<br/>

"No blood," he said. "No flame. No High Imperial."

<br/>

He put his hand flat on the clipboard.

<br/>

Lyra looked at the clipboard, then at Mark, and something like a reluctant grin tugged at her mouth. She put her bandaged hand flat beside his.

<br/>

Torren reached over from the doorway and laid one huge, soot-stained hand down beside hers.

<br/>

Vespera looked at the clipboard for the longest time. Mark could see the mage in her, the woman who had expected candles and vows and ancient words, struggling with the absurdity of it. Then she sighed—a long, deep, profoundly resigned sigh—and laid her gloved hand down on top of the brass.

<br/>

"This," Vespera said, "is the least dignified oath in the history of the Empire."

<br/>

"It's the only object in this world that nobody ever questions," Mark said. "Seemed appropriate."

<br/>

Up in the doorframe, Legacy turned all eight eyes toward the bench. She watched the four of them, hands on the clipboard, for a long, unblinking moment.

<br/>

"And a witness," Mark said quietly. "That makes it official."

<br/>

---

<br/>

Long after the others had gone—Vespera back to the Chapterhouse, Lyra back to the workshop, Torren back across the yard to say goodnight to the girls—Mark sat alone in the shed by the cool blue light of his desktop capacitor.

<br/>

The construct's grey core sat on the bench beside him, wrapped in a clean cloth, labeled with a scrap of parchment on which Lyra had written in large, emphatic letters: *DO NOT CHARGE GERALD*.

<br/>

Mark opened his leather-bound ledger to the last written page. He looked at it for a while. Then he turned to a fresh one, and then, deliberately, skipped a page after that, leaving a blank sheet as a divider, the way he'd always started a new section in a design document.

<br/>

He picked up his charcoal stick and wrote at the top of the page, in large, clean letters:

<br/>

**PROJECT ROOT ACCESS**

<br/>

He looked at it for a long moment. Then, underneath, he began to write.

<br/>

> *Project Charter — Mark Vance*
> *Objective:*
> *Identify who deployed the Measure, why, and what it is protecting against. Determine whether the Restriction can be safely removed, and if so, how. If not, find out why not.*
> *Team:*
> *- Mark Vance: Project Lead / Systems Architect.*
> *- Lyra (Journeyman Engraver): Lead Engineer. Credited Collaborator. Dates plates by their scratches. Measures seams from across rooms. Keeps evidence in pocket #12. Do not tell Lyra she is the most valuable person on this team. (She knows.)*
> *- Vespera (Mage, Chapterhouse): Compliance & Legal. Former enforcer of the Measure. Has committed treason. Still unhappy about the clipboard.*
> *- Torren (Smith Master, quarter-dwarf): Facilities. Has been forbidden from naming the project. Has almost certainly named the project anyway. Do not ask what.*
> *- Legacy: Witness. Security. Senior to all of us.*
> *Known Risks:*
> *- House Thorne ('Keepers of the Measure'). Bear the Concord seal. Can detect clean execution. Willing to let sanitation units resolve 'drift.'*
> *- Unknown party capable of erasing an entire archive's evidence overnight. Identity: unknown. Methods: unknown. Deeply unsettling.*
> *- The gold glyph on the Anchor. Never before recorded. Meaning: unknown.*
> *- Whatever is on the other side of the 'dam.'*
> *Evidence Held:*
> *- Transcribed copy of the Concord decree (Ledger, Log 11).*
> *- Drawings of the First Era plate and the transition plate.*
> *- One (1) drained construct core. DO NOT CHARGE.*
> *Stakeholders:*
> *- The Concord: Probably Thorne. Possibly more.*
> *- Julian Thorne: Threat actor. Possibly also frightened. Monitor both.*
> *- Inspector Valerius: Unknown. Lied in his report on our behalf, or on someone's behalf. Watch.*
> *- Archmage Ignis: Hostile, but mostly to himself.*
> *- Archivist Mott: Unknown. Possibly knows everything. Possibly knows nothing. Is definitely asking if everyone is new.*
> *First Milestone:*
> *Learn to read First Era script without the translation layer. Find out what else the Concord left 'below.'*

<br/>

Mark set down the charcoal and read it over twice.

<br/>

Then he sat back on his stool and looked up through the small, high window at the two moons, green and silver, hanging over Oakhaven.

<br/>

He thought, for some reason, about Brad.

<br/>

About that last Slack message, a lifetime ago, glowing on a monitor in a dark home office in Raleigh, in the rain. The rocket emoji. The magic wand.

<br/>

*Just grab an energy drink, lean into the synergy, and work your magic!*

<br/>

Mark had spent forty years being told to work his magic by people who had no idea what the words meant. Forty years of being handed impossible deadlines, broken architectures, and systems designed by people who never understood them, and being told to make them work anyway.

<br/>

He looked down at his bruised, bandaged arms. At the clipboard. At the wrapped grey core, and the ledger, and the words at the top of the page.

<br/>

For the first time in forty years, it occurred to him, he actually could.

<br/>

In the corner of the doorframe, Legacy shifted one leg. Mark chose to believe it was a nod.

<br/>

"Okay," Mark said softly, to the shed, the spider, and the whole broken, beautiful, five-hundred-year-old legacy system humming away somewhere far below his feet.

<br/>

"Let's see who owns the server."

<br/>

### *Stinger: The Thorne Estate — The Regional Capital*

<br/>

Forty miles from Oakhaven, in the regional capital, the great house of Thorne stood silent beneath the two moons.

<br/>

In a study on its highest floor, lined with books no one else in the Empire was permitted to read, an old man sat alone at a heavy oak desk. His hair was silver. His face was lined and severe. On the third finger of his right hand, he wore a heavy silver signet ring, worn smooth by centuries of other hands before his.

<br/>

A circle, cut by a single line.

<br/>

For five hundred years, the stone set in that ring had been dark. For the past several days, it had flickered red, again and again, and he had watched it, and waited, and said nothing.

<br/>

Tonight, as the moons rose, the stone began to change.

<br/>

The red faded. And in its place, slowly, steadily, a new color rose up through the stone and filled it, until the whole ring glowed with a warm, deep, unmistakable light.

<br/>

Gold.

<br/>

Lord Aldric Thorne looked at it for a very long time.

<br/>

Then he closed his hand around it, so that the light leaked out between his fingers, and said quietly, to the empty room:

<br/>

"So. It has begun again."
