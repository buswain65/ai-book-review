# Chapter 5: The Party

On the inside cover of his Registrar guide, in small, neat letters that nobody but Pip had ever read, Mike kept a record of the last eight years. He had started it out of habit. In his old life, every system he had ever worked on had kept a changelog, and he saw no reason his own life should be any different.

> **Mikel Dobbins: Changelog**
>
> *v1.0:* Hired. Interview: 11 seconds.
>
> *v1.1:* Basement discovered (under URGENT pile).
>
> *v1.2:* Blood contract signed. Regret: on schedule.
>
> *v1.3:* URGENT pile cleared.
>
> *v1.4:* URGENT pile restored (Priscilla). Elapsed time: one week.
>
> *v1.9:* Learned all 47 of the Registrar's rules. Rule 31 is definitely a joke. Nobody will test it.
>
> *v2.0:* Writing desk delivered. Six years, three months after "this weekend." On schedule, per estimate. Da very proud.
>
> *v2.4:* Grew. Finally.
>
> *v2.7:* Guide reaches 312 pages. Known readers: Pip, one rat.
>
> *v2.8:* Rat deceased. Not related to guide (probably).
>
> *v3.0:* Promoted to senior clerk. Reason given: "He knows where everything is."

Osric was still behind his stack. Mike had never seen his face. He had begun to suspect there was no face, only the stack and a voice saying "Edwin knew," and lately, more and more often, something worse.

He heard it on a gray morning in late summer, eight years almost to the day since he had walked into the guild in his one clean shirt.

A new clerk, a nervous boy of twelve named Tam, was standing at the counter holding a contract and looking lost. "Excuse me," Tam said to the room. "Does anybody know which drawer the lumber guild's paperwork goes in? There are two blue drawers."

From behind the stack, without hesitation, Osric's flat voice answered.

"Mike knows."

Mike, at his desk, went very still.

A second clerk passed. "Ask Mike."

A third, from the stairs: "Mike has a system."

Mike looked down at his hands. They were a grown man's hands now, inky at the fingertips. He thought of a scrawled note under an inkpot, and of a man named Edwin fishing somewhere very far away, where nobody could ask him anything.

*Oh no,* he thought. *I've become the documentation.*

---

The reorganization had happened the previous spring, and like all reorganizations, it had been announced as a bold step toward clarity.

The guild was now divided into Operations, Client Delight, and Heroics. Operations reported to Guildmaster Brannoc. Client Delight was run by Priscilla, whose new title was Head of Client Delight, and who had celebrated by ordering a brass nameplate the size of a door. Heroics was run by Gareth the Bold, who had been promoted to Lead Adventurer in recognition of his many heroic deeds, none of which involved paperwork, planning, or leading anyone.

The senior clerk, it had been decided, served all three.

This was the morning Mike learned what that meant. Three orders arrived on his desk before breakfast.

The first was on heavy guild stationery, in Brannoc's huge, slanting hand: *CLERK. Assemble the crypt party. Prep them for the Sunken Crypt. TOP PRIORITY. — B.*

The second was on pink paper scented with violets: *Mikey!! Need the crypt party Thursday for the Hollins wedding — LIVE ENTERTAINMENT with REAL MAGIC!! Already promised!! Top priority!! XO, Priscilla, Head of Client Delight.*

The third was a single line scrawled on the back of a tavern bill, in charcoal: *Need the crypt party for troll. Probably small troll. BE BOLD. TOP PRIORITY. — Gareth (Lead Adventurer).*

Mike laid the three notes side by side and looked at them for a long time.

The same three people. The same day. Three places. Three top priorities.

The skill flickered into view.

**Estimated time to satisfy all three: requirements contradictory.**

"Yes," said Mike to the skill. "Thank you. I'd noticed."

---

He found the crypt party where Brannoc had told him they'd be, at the back table of the guild hall, in the corner where the light was worst. Brannoc had called them "our finest party." It took Mike about four minutes to understand that this meant "the three adventurers nobody else would take."

The first was a round, cheerful woman in her fifties, in white robes embroidered with a symbol Mike didn't recognize: a circle with an arrow curling back on itself.

"Sister Agatha," she said warmly, clasping his hand. "Cleric of Saint Redundia, Goddess of Backups."

"Backups?"

"She keeps a copy of everyone," said Agatha serenely. "Three days ago, every day. When I heal someone, I restore them to how they were three days ago. Good as new!" Her smile slipped, very slightly. "Memories included, I'm afraid. There's a bit of a gap afterward."

As if summoned, Gareth the Bold came striding across the hall, cloak billowing indoors, and stubbed his toe hard on the leg of their table. He swore and hopped.

"Oh, you poor thing," said Agatha, and before anyone could stop her, she laid a glowing hand on his boot.

There was a soft chime, like a bell rung underwater. Gareth stopped hopping. He looked at his foot. He looked around the hall, frowning slightly.

"Why," said Gareth the Bold, "does my door say *Lead Adventurer*?"

It took Brannoc forty minutes and a hastily rewritten letter of appointment to re-promote him. Gareth accepted with tremendous humility, for the second time that week.

The second member of the party was a paladin in very clean silver armor, sitting bolt upright with his helmet on the table in front of him. He had a long, serious face, and he was polishing a gauntlet that was already spotless.

"Sir Roderick Ashvale," he said gravely. "Order of the Lantern. I should tell you at once, clerk, that I have sworn an oath."

"What kind of oath?"

"Never to enter a legacy dungeon." He said *legacy* the way a priest might say *plague*. "Anything more than a hundred years old."

Mike waited for the joke. There wasn't one. "Why?"

Sir Roderick set down the gauntlet. "Because they're unmaintainable," he said. "The traps are undocumented. Nobody alive knows who built them, or why. The previous parties all did something to the place, and none of them wrote it down. You disarm one trap and two others go off, because somebody a hundred years ago tied them together and never told anyone." His voice tightened. "I lost a squire that way. In the Barrows of Elm. Good lad. Pulled a lever marked *Safe*."

There was a short silence.

"That's..." Mike said quietly. "That's actually fair."

Roderick looked at him, surprised. Nobody had ever said that to him before.

"I'd go into one," the paladin added, almost to himself, "if someone gave me proper documentation. A map of every trap. Who built it, and why. What's been changed. But nobody ever has."

Mike thought of the Sunken Crypt, two hundred years old, rebuilt by the river and visited by six previous parties whose only records were *wet*, *wetter*, and *resigned*. He decided this was a problem for later.

The third member of the party arrived late and did not sit down.

She was about Mike's age, tall and quick, with ink-black hair cropped short and a mage's coat covered in pockets. A rolled scroll was tucked under one arm. It was, as far as Mike could tell, about twelve feet long.

"Velka," she said. "Mage. You're the clerk? Good. I need you to put the crypt on my record the second we're done. *Sunken Crypt.* That's going to look *very* good."

She unrolled a few feet of the scroll, as if by reflex. It was a résumé. Mike caught glimpses as it unspooled: *Proficient in Fireball 2.0. Fireball 3.1. Fireball 4.0 (beta). Lightning (experimental). Levitation (pre-release).*

"What happened to Fireball 1.0?" Mike asked.

Velka looked faintly offended. "Nobody uses 1.0. It's *ancient*. It's from *last spring*."

"Does it work?"

"Of course it works. It's just *boring*." She flexed her fingers. "I've got a new one this morning, actually. Illumination 5.0. Brightens any surface. Let me show you."

She turned to the quest board, said a word, and flung out a hand.

The quest board turned purple.

Not glowing purple. Not lit-up purple. Just purple: the wood, the notices, the pins, the URGENT stamps. A deep, permanent shade of plum. A notice for wolves in the east pasture now looked like a notice for very upsetting grapes.

Velka studied it for a moment with her head tilted. "Huh," she said. "Worked in my tower."

---

Mike called a meeting.

It seemed the responsible thing to do. Three bosses, one party, three conflicting orders: surely, if he could get everyone into one room, they could agree on which order came first.

They met in the small council room at the back of the hall. Brannoc sat at the head of the table. Priscilla sat on his left, with her violet-scented notebook. Gareth sat on his right, feet on the table. The crypt party lined the far wall, Sir Roderick at attention, Agatha knitting, Velka examining her fingernails for traces of purple. Mike stood at a slate board with a piece of chalk.

"So," he said. "Three requests for the same party on the same day. We need to decide which comes first."

"Crypt," said Brannoc. "Obviously. Priority one."

"The wedding," said Priscilla, "is priority one. The Hollinses are *wonderful* clients, and I've already promised."

"Troll," said Gareth. "Priority one. Be bold."

Mike turned to the slate and, as neutrally as he could, wrote:

*Priority One: Crypt.*

*Priority One: Wedding.*

*Priority One: Troll.*

He stepped back and looked at it. It had the clean, stark beauty of a perfectly accurate description of a disaster.

"Right," he said. "So we've got three priority ones, which means—"

"Ooh," said Priscilla, pressing a hand to her temple. "All this arguing. I've got a *terrible* headache."

"Allow me," said Sister Agatha, and laid a glowing hand on her forehead.

The underwater chime rang. Priscilla blinked. She looked around the council room, at the slate, at everyone sitting there.

"Oh!" she said brightly. "Are we having a meeting? What's it about?"

They started again.

It took an hour to get back to where they had been. Brannoc said crypt. Priscilla said wedding, and also, now that she thought of it, there was a lovely family in Westmarch who needed a griffin removed, and she'd *sort of* said yes. Gareth said troll. Mike rubbed out the slate and wrote *Priority One* three times again, then a fourth time for the griffin.

Gareth yawned, cracked his neck, and winced. "Pulled something," he said. "Fighting."

Sister Agatha set down her knitting and rose, hands beginning to glow.

Mike moved faster than he had moved in eight years. He stepped between them, chalk in hand, arms out.

"*Nobody,*" he said, "heals anyone until the minutes are written."

The room went quiet. Sister Agatha, slowly, lowered her hands. Behind her, Velka was looking at Mike with an expression he couldn't read.

The meeting went on for another hour. At the end of it, the slate read exactly what it had read at the beginning, plus a griffin.

Then Brannoc stretched, stood, and clapped his hands together with the satisfied air of a man who had accomplished a great deal.

"Good meeting," he said. "Let's take this offline."

And they all left, smiling, in three different directions, except Mike, who stood alone in the council room with four Priority Ones and a piece of chalk.

The skill, gently, did not say anything at all. Mike appreciated that.

---

He had been back at his desk for ten minutes when Velka appeared beside it.

"You," she said. "Clerk. That meeting was a waste of my *entire* morning. I have three new spells to test, and I've been sitting in a room watching you write *priority* on a—"

She stopped. She was looking at his desk.

The Registrar guide lay open there, all 312 pages, in his neatest hand. It was open to Rule 23: *If the Registrar begins to hum, step back and wait. Do not try to help. (Tested by Edwin, according to Osric. Edwin required three days of rest.)*

Velka picked it up. Mike braced himself for mockery.

It didn't come. She turned a page, then another. She read the rule about the number seven, then the long note underneath it, where Mike had recorded every test he'd run in eight years, every time he'd tried filing a seven before noon, and exactly what had happened. She read for longer than Mike expected. She read like someone who understood what she was looking at.

"You tested all of these," she said at last. It wasn't a question.

"Most of them."

"Before you trusted them."

"Yes."

She closed the guide and set it down carefully, squared to the edge of the desk. "You test everything before you trust it," she said.

"You don't," said Mike. "The quest board is purple."

She tilted her chin. "Testing," she said, "is for people with nothing to prove."

Then she left, with her twelve-foot résumé under her arm, and Mike found that he had been watching her walk away for slightly longer than was strictly professional. He frowned and went back to work.

Across the hall, by the purple quest board, Velka stopped, turned around, and looked back at his desk with a small, puzzled frown of her own. Then she shook her head and kept walking.

---

"*Mike knows,*" said Pip, aghast, down in the Basement that afternoon. "They said *Mike knows*?"

"Three of them," said Mike. "In a row."

Pip took off his spectacles and pressed his hands to his face. "Footnote: this is how it starts. Footnote: this is *exactly* how it started with Edwin. Footnote: first they say you know. Then they stop learning. Then one day you go fishing, and the building catches fire."

"I'm not going fishing."

"Footnote: that is also what Edwin said."

Durgan, in his chair, lowered his pamphlet. He had more gray in his braid now than eight years ago, and the singed end from the blood contract had long since grown out. But his small, sharp eyes were the same.

"So write it all down," he said. "Every bit of it. Not just the Registrar. The drawers. The contracts. Who to ask for what. All of it." He went back to his pamphlet. "Before somebody else has to."

Mike looked at the guide in his hands, 312 pages that took up one tiny corner of everything he knew, and felt the scale of the job settle over him.

The skill offered no estimate. He suspected it was being tactful.

---

It was Pip who noticed the river.

For eight years, on a peg at the end of the rune wall, Pip had kept a small round gauge carved with water-runes and connected by a long silver thread to a marker stone sunk in the riverbed. For eight years, every day, he had checked it. For eight years, it had glowed a steady, sullen blue.

That afternoon, as Mike was climbing the ladder, Pip gave a strangled squeak.

Mike turned. The gauge was still blue, but paler. Much paler. And as they watched, one ring of runes around its edge flickered and went dark.

"Dry season," Pip whispered. "It's dropping."

The skill, which had been so tactful all afternoon, made up for it all at once.

**Estimated time until the crypt can be entered: 9 days.**

And beneath it, a line Mike had been watching count down quietly for eight years:

**Estimated time until Brannoc remembers this contract: 9 days.**

He climbed the rest of the way up the ladder and came out behind the front counter, thinking hard. And so he was standing there, three feet away, when the main doors swung open and a familiar figure in a black coat with gold buttons swept in.

The Duke's steward. Older now, grayer at the temples, but with the same narrow, patient face, the face of a man who had been sent to fetch things at inconvenient hours his entire life.

He approached the counter. Priscilla, behind it, lit up like sunrise.

"His Grace the Duke of Varrenhold," said the steward, "requires an escort. Tonight—"

"*Absolutely!*" said Priscilla.

The steward, who had clearly been about to say more, closed his mouth. He considered her for a moment, then inclined his head a quarter inch and left.

Priscilla wrote it down on a pink slip, stamped it URGENT, and handed it to Mike with a dazzling smile. "Isn't this exciting? A *personal escort* for the *Duke*."

Mike looked at the slip. He looked at the window, where the afternoon light was already turning gold. He looked at the hall, where every adventurer he could see was either on a quest, assigned to a wedding, promised to a troll, or purple.

The skill, helpful as ever, laid it out for him.

**Estimated time until midnight: 6 hours.**

**Estimated escorts available: 0.**
