# Chapter 20: Seven Days

Malphus arrived at dawn, exactly on time.

Mike knew it was exactly on time because Malphus said so. He came through the front door of the guild hall as the first bell of the morning finished ringing, ducking his horns politely under the lintel, and he set a small brass hourglass down on the front counter. The last grain of sand fell through it as he let go.

"Good morning," he said pleasantly. "Exactly on time."

He was wearing the same dark coat as before, and carrying the same ledger, bound in something red that Mike had decided, the first time he saw it, not to think about. He looked around the hall with the warm, interested expression of a man visiting an old friend's house and noticing they'd moved the furniture.

The guild was all there. Brannoc had called everyone in early. He stood in the middle of the hall in his best tunic, holding an empty mug, because Wren Halloway held a mug, and the Guildmistress of Tanner's Row was very successful. Priscilla stood beside him, smiling brightly at nobody. Gareth the Bold stood beside her, in his yellow headband, with his hand on his sword, in case the payment turned out to be the kind you fought.

And Rupert Spindlewick, Chief Ink Officer, stood at the front, with his three roommates behind him in their green belts, and an easel.

"Master Malphus!" said Rupert, coming forward with both hands out. "Welcome, welcome. Before we begin, I'd love to walk you through where we are."

Malphus looked at the outstretched hands with courteous interest and did not shake either of them.

"Where you are," said Malphus, "is the first payment. It's due today." He opened his ledger. "The interest on the seal, the interest on the door, the interest on the OMNIFILE, and a small late fee on the golem, from last week, when the golem was late." He turned the ledger around so they could see the number at the bottom of the page.

Somebody near the back made a noise like a kettle.

Mike, who had spent the whole night putting the guild's records in order, already knew what the number was. He also knew what was in the guild's strongbox. He had counted it at four in the morning, twice, because he had hoped he was wrong. He was not wrong. The skill had told him so.

The Big Rewrite had cost a great deal of money. The OMNIFILE's licensing fees had cost a great deal more. And the Lean Six Sigil program had cost, among other things, seventy-two colored belts, a black belt with gold thread, a full-time Sigil Sensei from out of town for three weeks, and a tasting menu at the Silver Pheasant to celebrate eliminating waste.

"Absolutely," said Rupert. "Absolutely. And we hear you. But before we talk about *payment*, I think it's important to talk about *value*." He nodded to Tolliver. Tolliver pulled the cloth off the easel.

Underneath was a chart. It was very large and very colorful. It had boxes and arrows and a little drawing of a wagon wheel in one corner, and across the top, in Tolliver's large, neat letters, it said *VALUE STREAM MAP: GUILD OPERATIONS (FUTURE STATE).*

"What we're proposing," said Rupert warmly, "is that we offer you this. In lieu of liquidity."

Malphus looked at the chart for a long moment. He leaned in and examined one of the arrows. He read the little caption under the wagon wheel.

"No," he said.

"I hear that," said Rupert. "Can I ask what's driving the no?"

"I don't take charts," said Malphus. "I take payment. Or I take records." He turned his head slightly, and his dark, pleasant eyes moved past Rupert, past the easel, past the roommates, past Brannoc and his empty mug, until they found the pillar at the back of the hall. "I believe I sent someone a note."

Mike stepped out from behind the pillar. He was carrying a box. Behind him, Pip was carrying another box, and behind Pip, Durgan was carrying four boxes stacked on top of each other, without any visible effort, as if they were empty.

They set them down on the front counter, one after another, with eight loud thumps.

"Records," said Mike. "Everything since the crypt. Ledgers, work orders, incident records, test logs. The inquiry findings. The objection with two signatures. The Known Issues list, eleven pages." He paused. "And Form E-1."

Malphus raised one eyebrow.

"It was invented after the failure," said Mike. "It's dated. I put a note on it."

Malphus opened the first box. He lifted out the top ledger and turned a few pages. He made a small sound, the one Mike remembered from the night Malphus had read the guild's rules cover to cover: the sound of a creditor who had expected a mess and had been handed a table of contents.

"Someone kept these," he said.

"Every day," said Mike.

Malphus turned a few more pages. He ran one long finger down a column, and stopped, and read a margin note, and his mouth moved very slightly at one corner.

"I can't pay you today," said Mike. "I'm not going to pretend I can. I'm asking for an extension."

"Good," said Malphus. "I'll grant one." He looked up. "On a written request."

There was a pause.

Mike looked at Brannoc. Brannoc looked at Rupert. Rupert looked at Tolliver. Tolliver looked at Dex. Dex looked at the chart.

There was no form for requesting an extension. Mike knew every form in the building. There was a form for requesting a form. There was not a form for asking a creditor from below for more time, because in two hundred years nobody at the guild had ever thought far enough ahead to need one.

Mike took a fresh sheet of paper from the counter. He took out his pen. He ruled a margin on the left side, exactly one thumb's width, the way Osric had taught him. He wrote across the top, in clear, ordinary letters, *FORM E-2: REQUEST FOR EXTENSION OF PAYMENT*. Underneath, he wrote the amount, the date, the reason, the records attached, and a line for a signature. He signed it. He blotted it. He handed it across the counter.

Malphus held it up to the light.

"Nice margins," he said.

He took out his silver pen. He wrote something in his ledger in red ink, and the ink smoked very faintly, the way it always did.

"Seven days," said Malphus. "On the seventh day, I'll come back, and I'll audit everything. Every box." He closed the ledger. "If the records are as good as these, we'll talk. If they're not, the guild defaults." He smiled courteously at the room. "I described the terms last time. A bell in every room. Rung at random. Forever."

Nobody said anything. Behind the front counter, as if it had been listening, one of the three bells rang.

Malphus picked up his hourglass. He turned it over, with no comment at all, and set it back down on the counter. The sand began to fall. It was, Mike noticed, falling very slowly. It was the slowest sand he had ever seen.

Then Malphus went out the front door, ducking his horns, and the door swung shut behind him.

Rupert turned to the guild with a confident smile.

"I think that went *really* well," he said. "Great alignment."

---

The barrier flickered at four o'clock that afternoon.

It lasted three seconds. Mike, who was at the front window, saw it happen: the faint, pale shimmer at the edges of the city wall went dark, the way a candle goes dark when a door opens, and then came back. Down in the market square, every single person looked up at the same time. A woman selling cabbages dropped a basket of cabbages. A man selling eggs dropped a basket of eggs. A juggler dropped everything he was juggling, which turned out to be knives, which caused its own small separate problem.

Then the shimmer was back, and the sky was blue, and for a long moment, nobody in the square moved.

Within the hour, a notice went up on the guild's front board. Rupert had written it. Dex nailed it up.

> *GUILD NOTICE: BARRIER STATUS*
>
> *We are aware of reports of barrier instability and are investigating. The barrier remains at 99.97% uptime. We thank the public for their patience as we continue to deliver excellence.*
>
> *— Office of the Chief Ink Officer*

Mike read it from the front step. Down in the square, the market was still picking cabbages up off the street.

"Ninety-nine point nine seven," said Pip, beside him, doing the arithmetic on his slate. "That's... technically right. If you count since it was built."

"It was built a hundred and eighty years ago," said Mike.

"So it's been down for three seconds out of a hundred and eighty years," said Pip. He looked at his slate. "That's actually very good."

"It wasn't down for three seconds out of a hundred and eighty years," said Mike. "It was down for three seconds *today*."

Pip looked at the cabbages. He thought about it. Then, slowly, he rubbed out his number.

Durgan came up the Basement ladder. He had his tool bag on his shoulder and a look on his face that Mike had seen only twice before: once the night the chandelier caught fire, and once the day they moved the door.

"Crypt," said Durgan.

They went down.

The barrier was connected to the guild's seal. Thessaly had mentioned it, cheerfully, in the middle of a story about something else: he had built the seal in an afternoon, for the founders' grand opening, and *wired it into the barrier for a bit of extra strength.* In a hundred and eighty years, nobody had written that down anywhere. Nobody had drawn a diagram. Nobody had told the city. One seal held the demon, and the same seal held up the sky over Brindlemoor, and the only record of it was a line in Pip's footnotes, between a goat and a weathervane.

The crypt was cold, and damp, and smelled the way it always had, of old stone and older trouble. The seal was set into the floor in the middle of the room: a great circle of carved runes, with Durgan's overnight patches here and there in brighter stone, like fillings in a tooth. One of the runes near the edge was glowing faintly red. It hadn't been, last month.

Mike knelt beside it. He didn't need to. He asked the skill anyway.

**Estimated time until the demon wakes: 7 days.**

He stood up slowly.

"Seven," he said.

Durgan didn't say anything for a while. He crouched down beside the red rune and laid his broad hand flat on the stone next to it, not on it, the way you'd put a hand near a stove to feel how hot it was.

"Malphus said seven," said Pip, in a small voice.

"Same day," said Durgan.

"The audit and the demon," said Mike. "On the same day."

Durgan looked at the seal for a long time.

"Nineteen years I've watched that seal," he said at last. He stood up, knees cracking. "Seven days is the least time I've ever had."

Then he took out his chisel and started work on the red rune anyway, because that was what Durgan did.

---

The Duke's summons came the next morning, on paper so heavy it could have been used as a door. It was addressed to Brannoc, but Brannoc brought Mike along, because Brannoc had learned, over many years and with great reluctance, that if he went to a meeting with the Duke without someone writing things down, he would later have no idea what he had agreed to.

This turned out not to help. Writing things down only tells you what happened. It does not stop it.

The Duke of Varrenhold received them in his long hall, seated in a great carved chair, holding the dragon tooth. He looked very pleased.

"BRANNOC," he said.

"Your Grace."

"THE HARVEST FESTIVAL."

"Yes, Your Grace."

"IS IN SEVEN DAYS."

Mike's pen stopped moving.

"I want," said the Duke, "A FULL GUILD ESCORT. Every adventurer. Banners. Drums. And your golems." He pointed the dragon tooth at Brannoc. "BOTH OF THEM. MARCHING. AT THE FRONT."

Mike opened his mouth. The old Registrar weighed about as much as a small barn, and the round room had been built around it, and it had not gone anywhere in two hundred years, because it couldn't. The OMNIFILE had been built to file. Mike was not sure it knew how to walk. He was fairly sure that if it did, it would play music while doing it.

"And afterward," said the Duke, before Mike could say any of this, "THE PARADE WILL END AT THE GUILD HALL." He beamed. "SO MY GUESTS CAN SEE THE WALL."

There was a short, terrible pause.

The wall. Two hundred and thirteen gilt frames, floor to ceiling, in Brannoc's office. Each of them had once held a single word, in the Duke's own seal: *ADEQUATE.* Each of them now held a different word, in red, that had crawled across the paper like a worm on the night the OMNIFILE had stamped the Duke's paperwork. The word was *MEDIOCRE.* Brannoc had hung a curtain over it. The curtain was purple. It had cost, Mike happened to know, eleven silver crowns, and was itemized in the records under *Office Improvements (Urgent)*.

"Two hundred and thirteen ADEQUATES," said the Duke dreamily. "I'VE TOLD EVERYONE. The Margrave is coming. The Countess of Lyle is coming." He leaned forward in his great chair and lowered his voice, which brought it down to the level of an ordinary shout. "This won't take long, will it?"

Mike looked at Brannoc. Brannoc was looking at the Duke. On Brannoc's face was an expression Mike had seen many times, on many faces, in two different lives: the expression of a man who has just heard a client ask for something, and whose mouth has decided to answer before his brain has finished listening.

"Of course, Your Grace," said Brannoc. "Not long at all."

The Duke raised the dragon tooth.

"ADEQUATE," he said.

---

The meeting was in the war room.

The war room was what Brannoc called the long room on the second floor with the big table in it. In two hundred years, the guild had never once fought a war, but Brannoc felt it was important to be ready. There was a map of the city on one wall and a large chalkboard on another, and at the start of the meeting, the chalkboard was completely clean.

Everyone was there. Brannoc stood at the head of the table. Priscilla sat beside him with her pink notebook. Rupert sat beside her, with the roommates behind him in a row. Gareth sat at the far end, because he had heard the word *war*. Pip, Durgan, Velka, and Osric sat along one side, and Mike sat at the very end of the table, where the light from the window didn't quite reach, which was where he had been sitting since Rupert moved his desk.

Brannoc wrote three things on the board.

*1. MALPHUS AUDIT*

*2. DEMON*

*3. HARVEST FESTIVAL*

Then, beside each one, he wrote *DAY 7*. Then he stepped back and looked at it, as if hoping it might look better from further away. It did not.

"So," said Brannoc. "Three things."

"I think what we're looking at here," said Rupert, "is a *convergence-driven synergy event*."

Everyone looked at him.

"Three workstreams," said Rupert, steepling his fingers. "One deadline. Massive opportunity for alignment. Honestly, the timing could not be better."

"The timing could not be worse," said Mike.

"That's a very negative framing, Mike."

Brannoc turned to Mike. "Estimates," he said. "On each."

Mike asked the skill. He already knew, more or less, but he asked it properly, one at a time, because that was the rule.

"The audit needs every record since the crypt checked and cross-filed," he said. "Five days, with three people. The seal needs a full re-carve of the outer ring before the demon wakes. Six days, with Durgan and Pip, and nobody else, because nobody else knows how. The festival needs the whole guild for a full day of marching, plus two days of rehearsal, plus banners."

Brannoc nodded slowly, as if all of this were very reasonable.

"Great," he said. "So can we do them all in parallel?"

"With different people, some of it," said Mike. "Not the seal."

"No, no." Brannoc waved his empty mug. "*In parallel.* By the same people." He thought about it. "But faster."

Mike closed his eyes for a moment.

"Brannoc," he said, "can you rank them? If we can only finish one of them properly, which one is it?"

Brannoc looked at the board. He picked up the chalk. With great seriousness, beside each of the three lines, he wrote: *TOP PRIORITY.*

He stepped back and nodded, satisfied.

"That's not a ranking," said Mike. "They're all the same."

Brannoc looked at the board again. He frowned. Then his face cleared. He picked up the chalk and, above all three lines, he wrote, very carefully: *PRIORITY ZERO.*

"There," he said. "Now we have a ranking."

"What's in Priority Zero?" said Pip.

"Nothing yet," said Brannoc. "But it's there if we need it."

"What if," said Brannoc, warming to the idea, "we just add more people?" He gestured at the city map. "Hire every adventurer in Brindlemoor. Put them all on the seal."

"Brannoc," said Mike. "Last time we sent more people into that crypt, the ceiling came down, and half of them spent the whole job sealed outside it."

Brannoc nodded slowly. He looked at the map. He looked at the board. He tapped the word *DEMON* with his chalk, thoughtfully, the way a man taps a piece on a chessboard.

"Well," he said, completely seriously, "can't we just move the demon?"

There was a silence.

"Move it," said Durgan.

"Somewhere else," said Brannoc. "Out of the crypt. Where it's not under the guild. We moved the door, didn't we?"

"We did move the door," said Mike. "That's why the demon stirred."

"Exactly," said Brannoc. "So we've done it before."

Durgan stood up. He didn't say anything. He simply walked out of the war room, and down the stairs, and they heard the Basement hatch open and close, and they understood, all of them, that Durgan had gone back to the seal, and that he would not be returning to the meeting.

"Great energy," said Rupert. "Let's capture that." Bramble wrote *MOVE DEMON?* on the board, under *DEMON*, and underlined it twice.

After that, it went the way meetings go.

The quartermaster said that, while everyone was here, the armory roof had a leak, and it would be a quick fix. Bramble wrote it on the board. The head of the kitchens said that the festival would of course need a feast, and that it would be a shame not to. Bramble wrote it on the board. Priscilla said that the six Contraption clients had been very understanding about the river, but would need a *small gesture of goodwill*, and she had a few ideas. Bramble wrote all of them on the board. Gareth said that he would like a new cape for the parade. Bramble looked at Rupert. Rupert nodded. Bramble wrote *CAPE (GARETH)*.

The board filled. The chalk got shorter. The writing got smaller, and then went up the sides, and then went around the corners. Mike watched it the way you watch weather coming.

"And," said Priscilla, brightly, putting up her hand, "could the festival have a float?"

Everyone turned to look at her.

"A float," said Brannoc.

"For the parade! The Duke would *love* a float."

There was a pause, and in that pause, Mike waited for somebody to say no. He waited for a long time.

"Ooh," said Dex, suddenly, from the back. It was the first thing Dex had said in the meeting. "What about the Contraption? It's still in the river. It already floats. It's just upside down." He looked around, delighted. "We flip it over, and it's a float."

Nobody said no.

Bramble wrote *FLOAT (CONTRAPTION)* on the board. Then he looked at the board. There was no room left anywhere on it. There was only one empty space, at the very top, beside the words *PRIORITY ZERO*.

Bramble wrote it there.

"Great," said Brannoc. "Great meeting, everyone." He rang the gong that he kept on the war room table, for occasions. "Let's make it happen."

---

The war room emptied. Mike stayed behind.

He sat at the end of the long table, in the bad light, and looked at the board. *MALPHUS AUDIT. DEMON. HARVEST FESTIVAL. MOVE DEMON?* An armory roof. A feast. Six small gestures of goodwill. A cape. And at the very top, above everything, the float.

He had asked the skill a great many questions over a great many years. He had asked it about wagon wheels, and wolf packs, and bells, and doors, and ropes, and metrics, and lunches. He had asked it how long it would take for things to go wrong, and it had always told him, and it had always been right.

There was one question he had never asked. He had been careful, for years, not to ask it.

He asked it now.

**Estimated time until the guild collapses: 7 days.**

Mike looked at the words for a while.

Then he laughed. It was not a big laugh. It was the kind of laugh that gets out of you when there is nothing else useful to do, the kind you hear in a server room at four in the morning when the backup turns out to have been backing up an empty folder for a year and a half. It echoed a little in the empty war room. It did not make anything better. But it was, he thought, the only reasonable reaction.

He asked again, in case he'd asked it wrong.

**Estimated time until the guild collapses: 7 days.**

The skill was never wrong.

He sat with that for a minute. Then, slowly, he asked one more question. It was a question he had not planned to ask, and once it was in his head, he could not get it out.

*Estimated time until the guild collapses if I quit?*

**7 days.**

Mike stared at it.

Seven. Exactly the same. Whether he stayed or left, whether he worked through the night every night for a week or walked out the front door tomorrow and never came back, the guild fell down on the same day.

He did not know what to do with that. He turned it over and over in his head, the way you turn over a coin you've found in the street, trying to work out which country it came from. It could mean that nothing he did here mattered. Or it could mean that he was free to go. He honestly could not tell which, and he was not sure which one was worse.

The next morning, there was a new bulletin on the front board.

> *GUILD BULLETIN: LEADERSHIP UPDATE*
>
> *The Office of the Chief Ink Officer is proud to recognize Tolliver, Dex, and Bramble as this week's CONVERGENCE CHAMPIONS, for their visionary work in identifying the convergence. A plaque will be presented.*
>
> *To ensure clear ownership going forward, Senior Clerk Mike Dobbins has been designated ESTIMATE OWNER for all Day 7 workstreams.*
>
> *— R. Spindlewick, C.I.O.*

The plaque was presented at the stand-up that morning. It was brass, and quite large, and it had all three of their names on it, and Rupert pinned it to the wall beside the window where the roommates' desks were, where the light was good.

Mike read the bulletin twice, standing in the hall with his tea going cold.

*Estimate owner.* It wasn't a promotion. It came with no money, no desk, no window, and no authority over anything. He knew exactly what kind of title it was. In his old life, people had been given titles like it, right before a project went wrong, so that when the project went wrong, everyone would know whose name to say. It was a title in the same way that a lightning rod is a decoration.

Gareth the Bold stopped beside him and read it too.

"*Estimate Owner*," said Gareth. He clapped Mike on the shoulder. "Congratulations. Sounds important."

"It is," said Mike. "Legally."

---

The guild emptied late that night.

The adventurers went home first, then the clerks, then the kitchen staff, then the quartermaster, still talking about his roof. Rupert and the roommates went to the Silver Pheasant to celebrate the plaque. Brannoc went upstairs to his office and drew the purple curtain more tightly across the wall. Down in the Basement, Mike could hear the faint, steady *tink, tink, tink* of Durgan's chisel, and he knew it would go on all night.

Velka came by his desk on her way out.

She was wearing her mage's coat, with soot on one sleeve, and the plain brown hat. The one he'd given her. The one he'd held over a candle for ten minutes and let a stable dog chew for an hour, to be sure. She hadn't taken it off since. She'd spent the day at the east gate with Pip, walking the barrier stone by stone, looking for anything else that was glowing red. She looked tired. She leaned on the corner of Osric's stack for a moment, the way she did now, and looked down at the estimates spread across his desk.

"Bad?" she said.

"Bad," said Mike.

She nodded, as if that was about what she'd expected. She tugged the brim of the hat down.

"See you tomorrow," said Velka, and went out.

Mike listened to the front door close behind her.

Then the hall was quiet. The lamps were out, except for his. Osric's stack rustled faintly in the dark, the way it always did, as if the paper were turning over in its sleep. On the front counter, Malphus's hourglass sat in a little pool of lamplight. The sand was still falling. It hadn't gotten any faster.

Downstairs, in the Basement, in the drawer Durgan had marked *MIKE*, there were four scrolls, each tied with a red ribbon, each one thrown down the ladder by a junior clerk who then fled. Each one said, in one way or another, that something Mike had warned about had happened, and that it was Mike's fault. He had never untied any of them. He didn't need to. Durgan counted them out loud every time.

Mike opened his own desk drawer.

Inside, on top of his spare pens, was a letter. The paper was plain and good. The handwriting was plain and good. It was from Tanner's Row, and it was signed *Wren Halloway, Guildmistress*, and it had been in this drawer for a long time.

He took it out.

He'd read it so many times that he could have recited it, but he unfolded it and read it again anyway, all the way through. *Senior Clerk. Steady hours. Fifteen-minute stand-up.* A guildmistress who wrote down every obstacle and cleared it herself by noon. A clean desk by a window. Down at the bottom, in Wren's small, careful handwriting, almost an afterthought, there was a list of terms. *No night bell.* *No crypt.* *No demons, so far as we know.* *Estimates are estimates.*

He read the whole thing again.

Then he sat for a long time in the lamplight, holding the letter in one hand, and listening to the chisel in the Basement, and watching the slow sand fall.

**Estimated time until the guild collapses: 7 days.**

Seven days, if he stayed. Seven days, if he left.

Mike picked up a pen.
