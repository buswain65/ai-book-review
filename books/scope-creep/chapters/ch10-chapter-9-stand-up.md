# Chapter 9: Stand-Up

At eight o'clock the next morning, exactly as predicted, the guild had a daily stand-up.

Nobody knew what one was. That did not stop it.

Brannoc had gathered everyone in the main hall: adventurers, clerks, Priscilla, Gareth, the crypt party, a few confused clients who had come in to post quests and been swept up in it, and Osric, whose stack had been carried into the hall by two junior clerks so that he could attend. They stood in a ragged circle under the chandelier while the Guildmaster strode into the middle, beaming, holding a mug.

The mug was empty. Mike checked.

"Right!" said Brannoc. "Everyone over by the window."

There was a pause.

"Which window?" asked someone.

"*The* window." Brannoc pointed at the tall window on the east wall, which looked out over the square. "Everyone stands by the window. That's how they do it."

The circle shuffled sideways and reformed, much more tightly, in front of the east window. Somebody's elbow went into Mike's ear.

"Guildmaster," said Mike carefully. "How do you know that's how they do it?"

"I *saw* it," said Brannoc, with tremendous pride. "Went over to Tanner's Row yesterday morning. Had a look through the window. They were all standing by it! Their guildmistress had a mug." He raised his own empty mug, as if presenting evidence. "And there was a dog."

"A dog," said Mike.

Brannoc put two fingers in his mouth and whistled. From the direction of the kitchens, a large, shaggy, brownish dog of no identifiable breed trotted into the hall, looked around at the crowd, sat down in the exact center of the circle, and began to scratch its ear.

"Got him this morning," said Brannoc. "From the stables."

"Whose dog is it?"

"The guild's," said Brannoc. "For the stand-up."

The dog finished scratching and looked up at Mike with an expression of profound, friendly emptiness. Mike looked back. He had the distinct feeling that the dog understood exactly as much about what was happening as everyone else in the room.

From the back of the circle, a junior clerk raised a nervous hand. "Excuse me, Guildmaster. What do they actually *do*? At a stand-up?"

Brannoc looked at him as if he were simple.

"They *stand*," he said.

And so they stood.

They stood for ninety minutes. For eighty of those minutes, Brannoc talked. He talked about the crypt, and about heroism, and about the importance of standing, and about a boar he had once hunted in his youth that he felt had a lot to teach them all about standing. Somewhere around minute forty, the dog lay down and went to sleep. Somewhere around minute sixty, so did one of the clients, standing up, which Brannoc praised as "the spirit of the thing."

Mike stood by the window with an elbow in his ear and read the skill.

**Estimated duration: 1 hour, 30 minutes.**

**Estimated useful content: 0 minutes.**

At half past nine, Brannoc clapped his hands. "*Excellent* stand-up, everyone. Same time tomorrow!"

The circle broke up. Behind the front counter, all three of the Duke's bells had been ringing, on and off, for an hour and a half.

---

That afternoon, Mike went to see what a stand-up actually was.

He told nobody except Velka, and he only told Velka because she caught him leaving and asked where he was going, and he found, to his own surprise, that he wanted her to come. She said it sounded boring. Then she came anyway.

The guild on Tanner's Row was on the far side of the river, in a converted wool warehouse with whitewashed walls and a sign that read, simply: *ADVENTURERS. Quests accepted. Quotes in writing.* Inside, it was quiet. It smelled of fresh paper and ink. There were no bells. Nothing was purple.

They came back the next morning, early, and watched through the same window Brannoc had.

At half past seven exactly, about twenty adventurers drifted in and stood in a loose circle by the window, because, Mike realized, that was where the light was good. A tall, gray-haired woman in a plain brown coat stood with them, holding a mug of tea, which was not empty. A large, sleepy hound lay under a desk nearby, ignoring everyone. Nobody seemed to know whose hound it was either.

The woman said, "Morning, all. Go."

And one by one, around the circle, each of them answered three questions. What did you do yesterday? What will you do today? What's in your way?

"Cleared the Hendry cellar of rats. Starting the Mill Lane bridge troll today. In my way: we're out of rope."

"Got it." The gray-haired woman wrote something on a slip of paper. "Next."

"Finished the escort to Westmarch. Day off today. Nothing in my way."

"Enjoy it. Next."

"Still on the Thornwood survey. In my way: the lumber guild keeps changing which section they want first."

The woman's eyes narrowed very slightly. She wrote that one down twice. "I'll talk to them. Next."

That was it. Each person took less than a minute. Nobody gave a speech. Nobody chanted anything. When the circle came back around to the gray-haired woman, she looked at her slips of paper, tucked them in her pocket, said, "Right. I'll have these sorted by noon," and everybody went to work.

The whole thing had taken fifteen minutes.

Mike stood at the window and looked at it for a long time. Beside him, Velka was frowning.

"It's very..." she said, and searched for the word.

Mike waited.

"*Orderly*," said Velka, as if it were an accusation. She lowered her voice to a whisper. "Nobody's on fire."

"That's the point."

"Nobody's even purple."

"That's *very* much the point."

She looked at the adventurers filing out to their quests: competent, punctual, well-rested, and, it had to be said, faintly sleepy. Not one of them was carrying a twelve-foot résumé. "I'd go mad here in a week," she said. But she said it slowly, the way people say things they aren't sure about.

The gray-haired woman came over to the window, opened it, and leaned out with her tea.

"You're Brannoc's clerk," she said. It wasn't a question. "The one who estimated the crypt."

"Yes, ma'am."

"Wren Halloway." She did not offer to shake hands; her hands were full of tea and obstacle slips. "I heard about the nineteen days." She looked at Mike over the mug for a long moment, the way you might look at a horse you were considering buying. "Right to the hour, they say."

"Something like that."

"Mm." She sipped her tea. "If you ever want a job where people read past the number, come and see me."

She went back inside and closed the window. Through the glass, Mike watched her sit down at a desk and start working through the slips, one after another. By the time they left, she had already sent someone out with a coil of rope.

They walked back across the river bridge in silence. Halfway across, Velka stopped and leaned on the parapet, looking down at the water.

"Their secret isn't the standing, is it?" she said.

"No."

"Or the window. Or the dog."

"No." Mike leaned on the parapet beside her. "It's that somebody listens. And then *does* something about it."

Velka was quiet for a while.

"That's even more boring than I thought," she said. Then, after a moment: "Is she right? About people reading past the number?"

"I don't know," said Mike. "Nobody's ever read past mine."

Velka looked at him sideways. "I did," she said. "The guide. All 312 pages."

Then she pushed off from the parapet and walked on before he could think of anything to say.

---

He tried. He really did.

At the next morning's stand-up, by the east window, with the dog asleep in the center of the circle and Brannoc holding his empty mug, Mike raised his hand.

"Guildmaster," he said. "At Tanner's Row, they do something we're not doing. They ask three questions. What did you do yesterday, what will you do today, and what's in your way."

Brannoc's face lit up like a bonfire.

"*Three questions!*" he boomed. "Excellent! Marvelous! Everyone! Repeat after me!"

And then, to Mike's dawning horror, Brannoc led the entire guild hall, in unison, in reciting them.

"WHAT DID YOU DO YESTERDAY?" roared sixty voices. "WHAT WILL YOU DO TODAY? WHAT IS IN YOUR WAY?"

The dog woke up and barked.

"*Wonderful!*" said Brannoc. "Again!"

They recited the three questions three times. Then Brannoc went on to talk for seventy-five minutes about the importance of reflection. Nobody answered any of the questions. It did not seem to occur to anyone that they were supposed to.

That was day two.

On day three, Brannoc added a talking stick. It was the dragon tooth from his necklace, a long, yellow, curved fang the size of a dagger, and the rule was that only the person holding it could speak. Since Brannoc held it for the first eighty minutes, this did not change much.

On day three, the guild motto was also added, to be chanted three times at the start: *Bold in Deed, Swift in Service, Adequate in All Things.* Nobody remembered when "adequate" had gotten in there. Mike suspected the Duke.

On day four came assigned standing positions, marked on the floor in chalk, by seniority. The dog got the center. Mike got a spot behind a pillar.

Also on day four, Priscilla introduced the Client Delight Minute, in which she shared exciting updates from the world of Client Delight. It ran twenty minutes. On day five it ran thirty-five, because she had promised a family in Hollins a magic show for their goat.

And every day, there was Gareth's Hero Moment.

It had begun on day two, with Gareth stepping into the center of the circle, very modestly, to share the story of how he had rescued the crypt party. It was a good story. It had some excitement and a lot of rubble.

On day three, there was a troll in it.

"A *troll*?" Velka whispered to Mike, from her chalk mark.

"It came up through the rubble," said Mike, listening. "Apparently."

On day four, there were dragons. Two of them. They had been guarding the crypt, and Gareth had held them off with nothing but his sword and his voice.

On day five, Gareth had rescued the crypt party from the dragons by carrying all five of them out on his shoulders, ring raised high, exactly as shown in the painting, which now hung in the hall behind him and which he gestured to modestly at the climax.

Sister Agatha, who had been one of the five people carried, listened to this with her hands folded and an expression of perfect serenity. "He tells it so *well*," she murmured. "I almost remember it."

And on the evening of day five, after the stand-up, Brannoc announced a new meeting. It would be held at half past nine, directly after the stand-up, sitting down, in the council room. It would be for discussing the stand-up.

Mike wrote it in his schedule. Then he sat and looked at the schedule for a while.

*8:00. Stand-up. 9:30. Sit-down about stand-up.*

Somewhere in another life, in a conference room in Raleigh, there had been a meeting to plan the meeting about the meetings. He had always thought that was as far as it could go.

He had been wrong. It could always go further. It just needed more time.

---

On day three, by order of the Guildmaster, the Basement was required to attend.

Mike didn't know who told Brannoc the Basement existed. He suspected the town crier. But at five minutes to eight, the trapdoor behind his desk creaked open, and Durgan Flintmantle climbed out of it, blinking in the daylight like a mole being forcibly introduced to the sun, followed by Pip, who had brought a slate.

It was the first time either of them had been upstairs, during the day, in years.

The hall went quiet as they crossed it. People stared. A few of the younger clerks clearly had no idea who they were.

"Who's that?" Mike heard someone whisper.

"I think he does the lights."

Durgan took his chalk mark. It was at the very edge of the circle, next to the coat rack. Pip stood beside him, clutching his slate to his chest. They stood through the motto. They stood through the three questions, recited in unison. They stood through Brannoc talking about bravery with the dragon tooth, and Gareth talking about the dragons, and Priscilla talking about the goat.

And then something happened that had never happened before.

Brannoc, flushed with enthusiasm, waved the dragon tooth over his head and roared: "And NOW! Let us HEAR from our people! Let us actually ANSWER the questions!" He looked around the circle for a volunteer, and his eye fell on the stout, gray-bearded, unfamiliar figure by the coat rack. "You! You there! Whoever you are! Yesterday, today, and what's in your way!"

He tossed Durgan the dragon tooth.

Durgan caught it in one hand. He looked at it. Then he looked up at the Guildmaster, with his small, dark, sharp eyes, and answered all three questions, completely and in order, in a voice like gravel settling.

"Yesterday," said Durgan, "I kept the building from burning down."

There was a little ripple in the circle.

"Today," said Durgan, "same."

The ripple stopped.

"What's in my way," said Durgan. He paused. He held Brannoc's gaze. "You."

The silence that followed was the longest Mike had ever heard in the guild hall. It was so complete that he could hear the dog breathing. Somewhere behind him, a junior clerk dropped a quill, and the sound of it hitting the floor was like a gunshot.

Brannoc's face went through several colors.

Then he cleared his throat.

"Let's," said Guildmaster Brannoc, "take that offline."

Durgan nodded once, gravely, as if this was exactly what he had expected. He handed the dragon tooth back. Then he stood at his chalk mark beside the coat rack for the remaining fifty-one minutes of the stand-up, completely silent, while everyone around him tried very hard not to look at him.

Pip, beside him, tried once. Just once. When Priscilla paused for breath in the middle of the goat, Pip raised his slate and said, in a small, urgent voice: "Excuse me, the fire ward is under serious strain from the bells, and footnote one—"

"No footnotes," said Brannoc, without looking at him. "Not in the stand-up. Rule."

Pip lowered his slate.

And behind the front counter, all through it, the bells rang.

They had been ringing since day one. Brannoc had ruled, early on, that nobody could leave the circle once the stand-up had started. It was disrespectful to the stand-up. So for ninety minutes every morning, the Duke's three bells rang, and rang, and rang, and nobody answered them.

They rang on day one. They rang more insistently on day two. And on day three, while Durgan was still standing silently by the coat rack, the great front doors of the guild hall burst open, and the Duke of Varrenhold strode in.

He was wearing his dressing gown. He was still faintly purple. He was holding one of his own bell cords, the tasseled end, torn clean out of his bedroom wall, and he was trailing several feet of silvery wire behind him across the guild hall floor.

The circle parted around him in terror. He walked directly into the middle of it, stepped over the dog, stopped in front of Brannoc, and spoke.

"IS IT DONE YET?"

Brannoc stared at him, mouth open, utterly lost. Then, with the reflexes of a man who had been running a stand-up for three days and had learned exactly one rule, he did the only thing he could think of.

He handed the Duke the talking stick.

The Duke looked down at the dragon tooth in his hand. He turned it over, examining it, the way he had examined the signet ring. Then he looked around the circle, at sixty silent people and one dog, all looking at him.

"ADEQUATE," he said.

And he took a spot by the window.

He came back the next day. And the next. Every morning at eight o'clock sharp, the Duke of Varrenhold arrived in his dressing gown, took the dragon tooth from Brannoc, and stood by the window with it for ninety minutes. At some point in every stand-up, usually during Gareth's Hero Moment, he would raise the tooth and ask "IS IT DONE YET?" Nobody knew what "it" was anymore. It had become part of the ritual. By day five, some of the newer clerks had started saying it under their breath along with him.

The skill offered a tally, at the end of the week.

**Time lost to stand-ups this week: 47 adventurer-hours.**

**Quests completed this week: 2.**

**One of these was the dog's.**

Mike never found out what that last line meant. He decided he didn't want to.

---

On day five, it was hot.

It was the last breath of summer, the kind of airless, sticky late-season heat that settles into stone buildings and stays there. By eight in the morning, the main hall was already warm. By half past, with sixty people and a Duke packed in front of the east window in the full morning sun, it was an oven.

Sir Roderick Ashvale, of the Order of the Lantern, stood at his chalk mark in full plate armor. He always wore it. A paladin, he had told Mike once, was always on duty. He stood very straight, very still, and slowly, over the course of the next hour, turned a deep, glistening red.

Mike, from behind his pillar, watched him with growing concern. He could see the skill flickering faintly in the corner of his eye.

**Estimated time until Sir Roderick falls over: 4 minutes.**

He was halfway across the circle, stepping over the dog, when Roderick went down.

He fell like a dropped armory: a long, grand, clanging collapse, full-length, face first, straight down onto the guild hall floor with a sound that echoed off the rafters like someone kicking a pile of saucepans down a staircase. The dog leapt three feet in the air. The Duke raised an eyebrow. Gareth stopped in the middle of the second dragon.

Sister Agatha was there first. She knelt beside him, laid one soft, glowing hand on his helmet, and the underwater chime rang out through the hall.

Sir Roderick sat up.

He pushed his visor back. He looked around the hall, at the sixty people standing in a crowded circle by a window, at the chalk marks on the floor, at the Guildmaster with an empty mug, at the dog, and at the Duke of Varrenhold in a dressing gown holding a dragon tooth. His long, serious face was completely bewildered.

"What," he said, "is going on?"

There was a pause.

"The stand-up," said someone.

Roderick frowned. "What's a stand-up?"

Nobody answered.

The pause went on. Mike watched it spread around the circle, from face to face, as one person after another opened their mouth to explain, thought about it, and closed it again. Sixty people had stood in this room for ninety minutes every morning for five days. Not one of them could say what it was for.

Gareth tried. "It's where we... stand," he said.

"Why?"

Gareth thought about it. "Because they do it at Tanner's Row."

"Why do they do it at Tanner's Row?"

Gareth looked at Brannoc. Brannoc looked at his mug. The mug, being empty, offered no assistance.

"It's..." said Brannoc finally. "It's how they *do it*."

The dog lay back down in the center of the circle and sighed heavily, as if it had heard this before.

That afternoon, Brannoc posted a new rule on the board, beside the stand-up schedule. Mike read it on his way out.

*NOTICE. For reasons of SAFETY, adventurers in plate armor may now LEAN during the stand-up.*

A leaning post had already been installed beside Roderick's chalk mark.

---

That night, after the hall emptied, Mike sat at his desk with the lamp turned low and Wren Halloway's offer in his hand.

She had sent it the day after their visit, by an ordinary runner, not a pigeon. One page, plain paper, neat handwriting. *Senior Clerk. Same pay, plus a quarter. Stand-up is fifteen minutes. Quotes in writing. People read past the number. — W.H.*

He read it again. Then he asked the skill, as clearly as he could, the question he had been avoiding all week.

*How long until things get better here?*

The answer came slowly, as if the skill were considering it.

**Estimate unavailable. Requirements unclear.**

Mike stared at the line. He had seen it once before, a lifetime ago, in a cobbler's back room, when he was six years old and thought a desk job at a stable institution sounded like paradise.

He almost laughed.

He looked around the dark hall. At the alcove behind his desk, where the trapdoor led down to a room full of runes, terrible tea, a gnome who footnoted his own feelings, and a dwarf who had carved his name on a drawer eight years ago and waited. At the Registrar's door, behind which a golem older than anyone's memory would be knocking out its quiet, cranky, undocumented work until long after everyone in this building was gone. At the hook on the wall beside his desk, where the charred, stamped-flat remains of a mage's hat had been hanging for a week, ever since Velka had dropped it there on her way out of the crypt, looked at it, and never asked for it back.

He folded Wren's offer carefully and put it in his desk drawer. Not thrown away. Just put away.

Then he turned down the lamp and went home.

---

The next morning, at eight o'clock, the guild had its sixth daily stand-up.

They stood by the window. Brannoc held his empty mug. The Duke held the dragon tooth. Roderick leaned on his leaning post. The dog slept in the center. Sixty voices recited the three questions in unison, and nobody answered them, and the bells rang.

And about forty minutes in, during Gareth's Hero Moment, as the second dragon was rearing up with flame in its throat, the front doors burst open again, and Priscilla came running in.

She was glowing. She was absolutely radiant. She was holding a contract over her head with both hands, like a trophy.

"*Everyone!*" she cried. "I have *wonderful* news!"

The circle turned to look at her. Gareth's dragon hung, forgotten, in midair.

"I've just signed the biggest deal in the guild's history," said Priscilla breathlessly. "The River Merchants' Consortium! A full year's contract! For our new *dragon-proof caravan escort service*!"

There was a silence.

Mike, behind his pillar, turned very slowly to look at her.

"Our *what*?" he said.

"Our dragon-proof caravan escort service!" Priscilla beamed at him. "I told them we've *basically* got one."

The word hit Mike in the chest like a thrown brick. *Basically.* He had heard it before. In a sales meeting he had not been invited to, in another world, about a routing feature that did not exist.

"We don't have one," he said. "We don't have anything dragon-proof. We don't have *one* person who's even *seen* a dragon—"

"I have," said Gareth. "Twice. This week."

"*Wonderful!*" boomed Brannoc, over all of them. He raised his empty mug in a toast. "Wonderful news! Add it to the stand-up!"

The hall cheered. The dog woke up and barked. The Duke raised the dragon tooth. "IS IT DONE YET?"

And behind the pillar, Mike stood very still while the skill, with what he could only describe as a deep and weary sigh, put up its readout.

**Estimated time to build a dragon-proof escort: unknown.**

**Requirements: dragon-proof.**
