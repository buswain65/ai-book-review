# Chapter 22: The War Room

Mike did not ask Brannoc's permission.

He thought about it. He sat at his desk behind Osric's stack, with Wren's card in the drawer and the clean board waiting upstairs, and he thought about going up to the office and knocking and asking, very politely, whether the Guildmaster would mind if some of the staff got together to save the guild. He even got as far as drafting the request.

Then he asked the skill how long it would take Brannoc to approve it.

**Estimated time until approval: day eight.**

He tore up the draft.

Instead, he took seven small cards from his drawer and wrote the same thing on each of them, in his clearest handwriting:

> *War room. Two o'clock. Bring what you know.*
>
> *— M.*

He gave the first one to Pip, who read it, gasped, footnoted it, and ran down the Basement ladder to show Durgan. He gave one to Tam, who read it three times and then asked, in a whisper, whether he was in trouble. He sent one to Velka at her tower and one each to Sister Agatha and Sir Roderick, at the chapel and the barracks.

He slid one into the side of Osric's stack, at about knee height.

For a long time, nothing happened. Then, very slowly, the card slid back out again. Someone had written on the back of it, in thin, shaky handwriting: *Edwin would have come.* Folded inside it was a single sheet of paper, brown and brittle at the edges, covered in faded ink. It was a page from a ledger. The handwriting was small and neat and very old, and at the bottom, in the corner, there were two initials: *E.W.*

Mike didn't know yet what it was. But he knew Osric didn't give anyone anything without a reason. He put it carefully inside his jacket.

That left one card.

He stood at the end of the hall for a while with it in his hand, looking at the front counter, where Priscilla was explaining to a nervous merchant that his missing cart would *absolutely* be found by Thursday, and that the guild would throw in a full inspection of his other carts, free of charge.

He thought about yesterday. The list. One name next to every job. A time next to every name. *Not this time, Gareth.* A party, delivered to the minute, with cookies.

He waited until the merchant left. Then he went over and put the last card on the counter in front of her.

Priscilla read it. Her face lit up like a festival lantern.

"A *meeting*! Oh, Mike, I *love* a meeting. What's it for? Who's coming? I can absolutely—"

"There's one condition," said Mike.

"Anything!"

"You can't promise anything," said Mike. "To anyone. The whole time."

Priscilla opened her mouth. Then she closed it again. She looked, for a moment, the way a fish looks when someone explains to it about land.

"Not even a *little*—"

"Not even a little."

She looked at the card for a long time. Then she nodded, slowly and bravely, the way people nod at a doctor.

"I'll try," she said.

---

At a quarter to two, Mike went up to the war room and pinned a sheet of paper to the door.

> *NO GONG.*
>
> *NO CHART.*
>
> *NO BELTS.*
>
> *BRING WHAT YOU KNOW.*

Then he went in and did something nobody had ever done in the war room, in two hundred years.

He put out chairs.

There had always been chairs in the war room, technically. They were stacked against the back wall, under a dust sheet, because Brannoc believed that meetings went faster when everyone stood, and that people who sat down got *comfortable*, and that comfortable people were not *hungry*. Mike pulled off the dust sheet and set them out around the long table, one by one. There were exactly enough.

They came at two. Durgan first, with his tool bag, smelling of stone dust, and Pip behind him with three slates and an armful of notes. Then Tam, very pale, clutching a pen. Then Sister Agatha in her gray robes, carrying a small cloth bag, which she set on the table with the air of someone who always brought a small cloth bag, just in case. Then Velka, in the brown hat, with ink on her fingers.

Then Priscilla, who stopped in the doorway, read the sign on the door twice, and came in looking as though she were entering a church of a religion she had only read about.

And last of all, with a great clanking, Sir Roderick Ashvale, of the Order of the Lantern, in full plate.

He stopped just inside the door. He looked at the long table. He looked at the chairs.

"Are those," he said, in a careful voice, "for *sitting*?"

"Yes," said Mike.

Sir Roderick had once, in the second month of Brannoc's daily stand-up, stood in full plate armor for ninety minutes in a hot hall, listening to Gareth describe his plans for the day in great detail, and had fainted, and been carried out on a door. He had not, since then, been to any meeting that lasted longer than a quest briefing. He approached the nearest chair the way a man approaches a horse he has heard good things about. He lowered himself into it with a long, grinding creak of steel.

He sat there for a moment, very still.

"Oh," said Sir Roderick softly. His eyes were wet. "Oh, that's *nice*."

Mike went to the head of the table. He did not stand on anything. He did not ring anything. He looked at the clean board, with *WHAT ACTUALLY MATTERS* written across the top in his own handwriting, and he asked the skill one question first, because he wanted to know.

**Estimated meeting length: 47 minutes.**

Forty-seven minutes. Brannoc's shortest meeting had been ninety. Mike looked around the table at the people who did the work: the dwarf who had kept the guild's wards running for nineteen years, the gnome who footnoted everything, the thirteen-year-old who could file with the Registrar, the cleric, the paladin, the mage, and the saleswoman sitting very straight with both hands folded on the table in front of her, as if afraid of what they might do on their own.

"Okay," he said. "Let's start."

At that moment, the door opened.

"Is this the war?" said Gareth the Bold, hopefully.

He was wearing his yellow headband and carrying his sword. He looked around the room, at the chairs and the table and the board.

"No," said Mike. "It's a meeting."

Gareth looked crestfallen. "But the sign says *war*."

"The sign says *war room*," said Velka. "It's the name of the room."

Gareth thought about this for a while. "Then why is it called that?"

Nobody had a good answer. Gareth went away, looking thoughtful, and closed the door.

---

"Rule one," said Mike. "Everything goes on the board. Every single thing anyone's been asked to do this week. Big or small, real or stupid. Nobody argues about any of it yet. We just write it down."

He picked up the chalk and started.

*THE SEAL (DEMON).*

*MALPHUS AUDIT.*

*HARVEST FESTIVAL ESCORT.*

"Both golems marching," said Tam, quietly. "In the escort. The Duke said."

Mike wrote it down. *(BOTH GOLEMS.)*

"The Registrar can't fit through the door," said Pip.

"Rule one," said Mike. "We don't argue yet."

Pip opened his mouth, closed it again, and wrote a footnote instead.

They went around the table. Mike wrote. *THE FLOAT (CONTRAPTION).* *ARMORY ROOF.* *FEAST.* *SIX GESTURES OF GOODWILL.* Priscilla read them out from her pink notebook, all six of them, in a slightly strained voice. One of them was a pie oven. *CAPE (GARETH).*

When they got to the end, Durgan stood up.

He came around the table without a word and held out his hand for the chalk. Mike gave it to him. Durgan walked up to the board, and very slowly, in large, square, careful letters, he wrote:

*MOVE DEMON?*

Then he looked at it for a while. Then he underlined it. Once.

It was much worse than if he'd underlined it twice.

He gave the chalk back to Mike and sat down.

"Now," said Mike, "estimates."

He went down the list, one at a time, and asked the skill, and wrote down beside each item exactly what it told him, without rounding up or down or making it sound better.

*THE SEAL: 6 days (Durgan, Pip).*

*AUDIT PREP: 5 days (3 people).*

*FESTIVAL ESCORT: 3 days (whole guild).*

*BOTH GOLEMS MARCHING: impossible (see Pip).*

"Oh, we can *absolutely*—" said Priscilla, suddenly, and then stopped, with her mouth still open. Everyone looked at her. She closed her mouth. She looked down at her hands on the table with an expression of deep betrayal, as if they had said it.

"Sorry," she said. "Habit."

*FLOAT: 4 days. ARMORY ROOF: 2 days. FEAST: 2 days. GOODWILL GESTURES: 6 days. CAPE: 1 day.*

*MOVE DEMON: 340 years.*

Pip footnoted every single one. Some of the footnotes had footnotes.

When it was done, Mike stood back. Then he added them up, on the side of the board, in a column, the way he'd been taught to add by Master Quillon when he was eight years old, carrying the ones.

He drew a line under it. He wrote the total.

**Estimated total: 31 days.**

He did not count the demon. Nobody felt that was unfair.

Mike wrote the other number underneath, the one from the hourglass on the front counter.

*TIME LEFT: 3 days.*

There was a silence.

Mike had been in a great many rooms where that silence happened. He had been in it at Synergex, at the end of every sprint planning that had ever been planned, when somebody finally put the real numbers next to each other on a whiteboard. He knew what came next. What came next was usually somebody important saying *well, let's see what we can do*, and everyone looking at their shoes, and then everyone working until four in the morning for a month.

But nobody here was important. Everyone was just looking at the board.

And it was strange, Mike thought. It was a terrible number. Thirty-one days in three. It was ten times more work than there was time. And somehow, written down there in white chalk where everyone could see it, it was less frightening than it had been all week, when it was only a feeling in the pit of everyone's stomach. You couldn't argue with a feeling. You could do something about a number.

Then Tam, at the end of the table, put up his hand.

"Could we," he said, nervously, "do it faster?"

The entire room turned, slowly, to look at him.

Tam went red to the tips of his ears. He was thirteen years old. He had worked at the guild for exactly one year. And in that year, he had heard *can we do it faster* so many times, at so many stand-ups, from so many people standing on so many tables, that it had gotten into him, the way soot gets into a chimney. He hadn't even meant to say it. It had just come out.

Pip, beside him, reached over and very gently wrote a footnote on Tam's notes.

> *¹ Do not.*

Tam read it. He nodded. He put his hand down.

At that moment the door opened, and Gareth put his head in.

"I'm here about the cape," he said.

---

"Rule two," said Mike, when Gareth had gone. "We rank them. One at a time, top to bottom. And to do it, we're going to ask the same question about every single one."

He wrote it at the top of the board, under *WHAT ACTUALLY MATTERS*, in smaller letters:

*WHAT HAPPENS IF WE DON'T DO THIS?*

"The seal," he said. "What happens if we don't fix the seal?"

"The demon wakes up," said Durgan. "Comes out. The barrier's drawn off the seal, so the barrier goes too." He shrugged. "City falls."

Mike wrote it down: *City falls.*

"The audit," he said. "What happens if we don't get the records ready?"

"Malphus takes the guild," said Velka. "Bells in every room. Forever."

*Bells forever.*

"The festival escort."

There was a pause.

"The Duke," said Priscilla slowly, "would be... disappointed?"

*Duke disappointed.*

"The float."

They all thought about it, honestly, for quite a long time. Pip checked his footnotes.

"Nothing," said Sister Agatha at last. "I believe nothing happens."

*Nothing.*

"The cape."

"Gareth is sad," said Velka.

*Gareth sad.*

They went through all of it. It turned out that most of the things on the board, when you asked what would happen if you didn't do them, came back with an answer like *nothing*, or *someone is mildly annoyed*, or, in the case of three of Priscilla's six gestures of goodwill, *the client doesn't find out, because the client never asked for it.*

When they were done, there were three things at the top that were not like the others. The seal. The audit. The festival.

Sir Roderick raised one gauntleted hand.

"With respect," he said, "the seal and the audit seem equally urgent. Both on the same day. Both catastrophic. I propose we rank them *jointly*. First equal."

Mike had been waiting for this. He had known it was coming since he wrote the question on the board. It always came. Somebody always said it. It was the most reasonable-sounding sentence in the world, and it was how every meeting he'd ever been in had ended up with nine top priorities.

"No ties," he said.

"But—"

"No ties. Every item gets its own number. One, two, three. If two things are both number one, then nothing is number one." He looked at the corner of the board. Under the wiped chalk, if you looked closely, you could still see the faint gray ghost of what Brannoc had written there two days ago, before Mike wiped it. *PRIORITY ZERO.* "There is no Priority Zero."

He took the rag and wiped the corner again, harder, until the ghost was gone.

Sir Roderick looked troubled. "Then how do we choose between them?"

It was Velka who answered.

"If the city falls," she said, "there's no guild to audit."

Everyone thought about that.

"Malphus can't take a guild that's under a demon," Velka said. "Well. He could. But I don't think he'd want it."

"Seal first," said Durgan.

Mike wrote a large *1* beside *THE SEAL*. And a *2* beside *MALPHUS AUDIT*. And a *3* beside *FESTIVAL ESCORT*. And then, slowly, all the way down the board, a *4*, and a *5*, and a *6*, and so on, until every single thing had its own number, and no two numbers were the same, and *CAPE (GARETH)* was number eleven.

He looked over at Priscilla. She was sitting on her hands. She had been for some time. Every now and then her shoulders twitched, as if her hands were trying to get out and promise someone something.

"Are you all right?" said Mike.

"I'm *fine*," said Priscilla, through her teeth.

The door opened.

"I heard there's a war," said Gareth.

---

"Rule three," said Mike. "Every item gets an owner. One name. Not two. Not *the team*. Not *everyone*. One person, who's responsible for making sure it gets done."

He went to the board, to the top, to the number one.

"The seal," he said. "Who owns the seal?"

Everyone looked at Durgan.

Durgan didn't move. He sat with his big arms folded on the table and looked at the word *SEAL*, and at the *1* beside it, and at the empty space where a name would go. And then he looked at Mike, from under his eyebrows, and asked a question.

"Owns," said Durgan. "Or gets blamed?"

The room went very quiet.

Durgan had been at the guild for nineteen years. For nineteen years, he had kept the wards running, all of them, on his own, with a chisel and a pamphlet and a gnome. For nineteen years, every time anything had gone wrong with anything, a scroll had come down the Basement ladder. He had been on a Performance Improvement Scroll since the day they moved the door. He had a drawer with Mike's name on it, full of other people's blame. Nobody had ever, in nineteen years, asked him to *own* anything. They had only ever told him it was his fault.

"Owns," said Mike. "You decide how it's done. You say what you need. If anyone tells you to do it differently, you tell them no, and you point at the board. And if it goes wrong, it goes wrong in this room, and we figure out why together." He held out the chalk. "Nobody throws a scroll."

Durgan thought about that for a long time.

"Different thing," he said.

He got up, and took the chalk, and wrote his name beside the seal. *DURGAN.* Square, careful letters. Then he sat down again, and didn't say anything else, but something about the set of his shoulders had changed, the way a wall changes when you take a weight off it that it has been holding for so long that nobody remembers it was there.

They went down the board.

Pip took Thessaly. Somebody had to go to the east gate and ask the old archmage to come back for the one day he had promised, and somebody had to bring his notes, and somebody had to make sure the notes were the right notes. "Thessaly will tell a story about a goat," Pip said. "I'll footnote it."

*PIP: Thessaly + notes.*

The audit went to Tam, and to Osric, who was not in the room, but who, Mike suspected, had known about this meeting before Mike did. *TAM + OSRIC: records. MIKE: checking.* Tam looked at his name on the board as if it were written in gold.

The barrier went to Velka. She would go to the east gate and watch the stones, and she would cast nothing that hadn't been tested at least three times, at the horse pond, first. She didn't argue. She just nodded. Mike decided not to make anything of it.

Then the crypt.

Not the seal. Durgan had the seal. But someone had to go down into the crypt under the guild with Durgan, to the chamber where the seal was, and keep the stairs clear, and keep the way open, and keep whatever might crawl out of the cracks away from the dwarf with the chisel.

"Sir Roderick," said Mike.

He wrote it down. *RODERICK: crypt.*

Sir Roderick Ashvale, of the Order of the Lantern, looked at the board. He looked at his own name, and at the word beside it. He went white.

The Order of the Lantern had one oath above all others: no knight of the Lantern would enter a legacy dungeon that had not been documented. Every room mapped. Every trap noted. Every door labeled. In the Sunken Crypt, Sir Roderick had only been able to go in at all because Mike had crawled ahead of him with a lantern and a notebook, and drawn every room before Roderick set foot in it.

But the crypt under the guild was older than the Sunken Crypt by a hundred years. Durgan had been down there. Mike had been down there, once, a few days ago. Nobody had ever drawn it.

"Is it," said Sir Roderick faintly, "documented?"

Everyone looked at Pip.

"There's a footnote," said Pip.

Sir Roderick closed his eyes. "Only a footnote?"

"It's a very *good* footnote," said Pip. "It has a diagram. Well. Thessaly drew it on a napkin. I copied the napkin."

Sir Roderick sat for a long time with his eyes closed. Then he opened them, and looked at the board, and at his name, and he didn't say yes. But he didn't ask for it to be erased, either.

Then Velka, who had been very quiet, said: "How do we know?"

"Know what?" said Mike.

"When each one is *done*." She was leaning forward, frowning at the board the way she frowned at a spell draft. "You've got what it is. You've got who owns it. You've got how long it takes. But you haven't got what *finished* looks like." She tapped the table. "At the horse pond, I don't stop when it *feels* like the spell works. I stop when the wagon floats and the pond doesn't. You need that. For every one."

Mike looked at her for a second. Then he drew another line down the board, and wrote a new heading at the top of a new column.

*DONE MEANS.*

They went down the list again. It took twelve minutes. Mike thought it was possibly the most useful twelve minutes in the history of the guild.

*THE SEAL: Red rune not red.*

*MALPHUS AUDIT: Malphus says "nice margins."*

*BARRIER: No cabbages on the street.*

Sister Agatha had been sitting patiently through all of it, with her hands folded on top of her little cloth bag. Now she cleared her throat.

"Mike, dear," she said. "Why does my name say *standby*?"

Mike looked at the board. She was right. Beside *AGATHA*, where every other name had a job, he had written *standby*. He didn't remember deciding to. He had just written it, the way you sometimes write the right answer on a test before you know why.

Sister Agatha was a cleric of Saint Redundia, Goddess of Backups. She could heal any wound, cure any illness, mend any broken bone, by the simple method of restoring the person to exactly how they had been three days earlier. Memories included. It worked every time. It was also why Gareth had once had to be promoted twice.

"I don't know," Mike admitted.

He asked the skill.

**Estimated time until you need Sister Agatha: 2 days, 23 hours.**

Mike read it out. Everyone looked at each other.

"What does that mean?" said Tam.

"I don't know," said Mike.

"That's very nearly three days," said Pip, doing the arithmetic. "That's very nearly the exact—"

"I know," said Mike.

Sister Agatha considered this calmly. Then she nodded, and patted her cloth bag.

"Well," she said. "I'll pack a lunch."

The door opened. Gareth put his head in. He had his sword out this time, just in case.

"Is it a war *yet*?"

Mike looked at him for a long moment.

Then he had an idea.

"Gareth," he said. "I've got a job for you. A very important job."

Gareth's face lit up like the sun coming out.

"Do you see the sign on this door?" said Mike. "*No gong. No chart. No belts.*" He pointed at it. "I need someone to guard the door, and make sure nobody comes in who breaks the rules. Nobody. No matter who they are."

Gareth looked at the sign. He looked at Mike. He drew himself up to his full height, which was considerable, and squared his shoulders, and saluted with his sword in a way that nearly took the light fixture off the ceiling.

"Nobody shall pass," said Gareth the Bold, "who breaks the rules."

He went out and closed the door, and they heard him take up a position outside it, and stamp his feet, and settle in.

"That," said Velka, "is the first job he's ever been good at."

---

"Rule four," said Mike. "This is the hard one."

He drew a line down the middle of the board, from top to bottom, a long white stripe. On the right side of it, at the top, he wrote two words.

*NOT DOING.*

Everyone looked at it. Somehow it seemed to take up more room on the board than anything else.

"Everything we can't do in three days, and doesn't matter as much as the first three, goes over here," said Mike. "We write it down. We say it out loud. We don't do it. And when anybody asks why not, we point at this."

He held out the chalk to Priscilla.

"Would you write them?"

Priscilla stared at the chalk as if it were a snake.

She stood up. She came to the board, very slowly. She took the chalk. Her hand was shaking, just slightly, the way a hand shakes when it is being asked to do something it has never done before, and has been specifically trained its whole life not to do.

Sister Agatha got up from her chair without a word, went to stand beside her, and took Priscilla's other hand in both of hers, the way you'd hold the hand of someone at the dentist.

"You can do it, dear," said Sister Agatha.

Priscilla wrote, in her round, cheerful, bubbly handwriting:

*THE FLOAT.*

She took a deep breath.

*THE CAPE. THE ARMORY ROOF. THE FEAST.*

She was going faster now. Her face had gone pale and determined.

*THE GOODWILL GESTURES (ALL SIX).*

And then, last, *MOVE DEMON?*, which Durgan watched her write with an expression of deep, private satisfaction.

Then Priscilla stepped back and looked at it. And then, before anybody could stop her, she darted forward and added, at the very top, beside the heading, in very small letters:

*NOT DOING (YET!)*

Mike took the rag.

He didn't say anything. He just reached past her and wiped off the *(YET!)*, carefully, leaving the *NOT DOING* behind.

Priscilla watched him do it. She swayed a little. Sister Agatha held on to her hand.

"Oh," said Priscilla faintly, and sat down.

At that moment, out in the hall, there was a voice.

"Absolutely *love* the energy in here. Let me just pop in and—"

"Halt," said Gareth.

Through the door, they all heard it. Every word.

"Gareth! Hi! Great to see you. I just need to—"

"You," said Gareth, in a voice of slow, terrible, delighted authority, "are wearing a *belt*."

There was a pause.

"Well, yes. It's my black belt. I'm a Sigil Master, Gareth, I earned—"

"The sign says *no belts*."

"That's... I don't think that applies to *leadership*—"

"*Nobody*," said Gareth, "shall pass. Who breaks the rules."

There was a much longer pause.

"I'm not taking off my belt, Gareth."

"Then you shall not pass."

Through the door, they heard Rupert Spindlewick, Chief Ink Officer, holder of the guild's only black belt with gold thread, which he had awarded himself before training anyone, draw a long breath as if to say something. And then think better of it. And then, after a while, the scrape of a bench in the hallway as he sat down on it, outside, to wait.

He was still there forty minutes later.

Inside, Mike turned back to the board. There was one item left that hadn't gone in either column. Number three.

*HARVEST FESTIVAL ESCORT.*

"We can't do it," said Mike. "Not and do the seal. Not and do the audit. The whole guild for three days. It goes over here." He pointed at *NOT DOING*.

"Someone," said Pip, "will have to tell the Duke."

Nobody said anything.

Slowly, one by one, every head around the table turned. Durgan's. Pip's. Tam's. Agatha's. Sir Roderick's, with a creak. Priscilla's, still pale. They all turned, and looked, at the one person in the room who had a title that made him officially, formally responsible for every estimate in the guild. A title he hadn't asked for. A title that had been given to him so that there would be somebody to blame.

"Senior Estimate Owner," said Velka.

Mike sighed.

He took the chalk. In the *NOT DOING* column, beside *HARVEST FESTIVAL ESCORT*, he wrote: *Tell the Duke. MIKE.* And in the *DONE MEANS* column beside it, after a moment's thought, he wrote:

*Mike still alive.*

---

There was one more thing he wanted to know before they finished. He asked the skill.

**Estimated time until Brannoc tries to add something: 4 minutes.**

Mike frowned. He looked at the door.

The door opened.

"Your Guildmastership," said Gareth from outside, with enormous dignity, "may pass."

Brannoc came in, holding his empty mug. He looked around the room, at the chairs, and the people sitting in them, and the long board covered from end to end in white chalk. Behind him, through the open door, Mike could see Rupert on his bench in the hall, craning his neck to see in, and Gareth standing in front of him with folded arms.

"Mike," said Brannoc. "You said you'd have the float estimate this afternoon."

"It's on the board," said Mike.

Brannoc turned to the board. He began to read. He read it from the top, the way people read a menu, looking for the one thing they came in for. *WHAT ACTUALLY MATTERS. WHAT HAPPENS IF WE DON'T DO THIS? 1. THE SEAL. DURGAN. 2. MALPHUS AUDIT. TAM + OSRIC. 3. FESTIVAL ESCORT.* His eyes went down the left side of the board and found nothing. Then they crossed the long white line and went down the right side.

They stopped.

*NOT DOING. THE FLOAT.*

Brannoc stood very still. Something happened in his face, a sort of slow reddening that started at the beard and worked upward.

Nobody said anything.

Mike watched the clock on the wall. Three minutes. Three and a half.

Brannoc put down his mug. He reached out, and picked up the chalk from the tray at the bottom of the board. He took one step toward the board, toward the left-hand side, toward the space under the number three where there was still room to write something. He raised the chalk.

Nobody in the room moved. Nobody spoke. Durgan didn't. Pip didn't. Tam didn't. Sir Roderick didn't, even creaking. Velka didn't, and Priscilla, with enormous effort, didn't, and Sister Agatha didn't. They just sat, all seven of them, in their chairs, and looked at him.

Not angrily. Not even unkindly. They just looked. At him, and at the board, and at the white line down the middle of it. And there was something about being looked at, all at once, by every single person who actually did the work, that made it very hard to write *FLOAT* on a board.

Brannoc stood with the chalk raised for a long moment.

Then he put it down.

"Yesterday," said Mike quietly, "you planned a farewell party in three hours."

Brannoc turned to look at him.

"You made one list," said Mike. "You put one name next to every job. You gave every job a time. And when Gareth wanted a sword fight, somebody told him no, and he went away." He nodded at the board. "This is the same thing. It just has a demon in it."

Brannoc looked at the board again, for a long time.

He didn't approve it. He didn't say *good work* or *great plan* or *let's make it happen*. He didn't pick up the chalk again, either.

"Fine," said Brannoc at last. He picked up his mug. "But I want it on the record that this is *your* plan."

"It's on the record," said Mike.

He took the chalk, and down in the bottom right-hand corner of the board, in small, neat letters, he wrote:

*Plan owner: M. Dobbins.*

Brannoc looked at it. He grunted. He went out past Gareth and past Rupert, who stood up and fell into step beside him, talking quickly and quietly, and the door swung shut behind them both.

Mike looked at the clock on the wall.

It had been forty-seven minutes.

Nobody moved. Sir Roderick shifted in his chair. Tam looked at his pen. Pip looked at his slate, as if there might be an agenda item he had missed. Mike realized, slowly, that none of them knew what to do now. In two hundred years, no meeting at the guild had ever ended early. Nobody had ever had *extra time* before. They sat there with it, looking at each other, the way people look at a present they're not sure is meant for them.

"I suppose," said Sister Agatha at last, "we could go and do the work."

They got up.

Before they left, Mike asked the skill the last question of the meeting.

**Estimated time to complete the plan: 2 days, 22 hours.**

He stood still for a moment and read it again.

Two days, twenty-two hours. And on the front counter downstairs, in Malphus's hourglass, there were a little under three days left. It fit. With an hour to spare.

In twenty years, in two lives, he had given more estimates than he could count. Hundreds of them. Thousands. Every one of them had been right. And every single one of them had come in over the deadline someone else had already set. Every single one. That was the whole shape of his life, in both worlds: the right number, arriving too late, to someone who didn't want it.

This was the first one that fit.

He turned around. The room was empty except for Durgan, who was standing in front of the board with his tool bag on his shoulder, reading it all the way through, top to bottom, left to right, the way he read the runes on a ward when he was checking it for cracks.

When he got to the end, he looked at Mike.

"Good plan," said Durgan.

Then he went down to the Basement to fix the seal.

---

The next morning, there was a new bulletin on the front board.

> *GUILD BULLETIN: GOVERNANCE UPDATE*
>
> *The Office of the Chief Ink Officer has become aware of an UNSANCTIONED WORKSTREAM operating out of the second-floor war room without appropriate Ink oversight. This workstream is currently UNDER REVIEW.*
>
> *(The Convergence Champions look forward to supporting its success.)*
>
> *— R. Spindlewick, C.I.O.*

Mike read it twice. The first line was in large letters. The last line was in very small ones. He knew exactly what it meant. If the plan failed, it had never been approved. And if it succeeded, the Champions had supported it all along.

He took a fresh sheet of paper, copied the bulletin out word for word, small print included, dated it, and filed it.

Then he went down the Basement ladder to see how the seal was doing.
