## Chapter 13: Malphus of Accounts

"The Guildmaster," said Malphus, "is asleep, I take it."

He said it the way a man might observe that it was raining: without blame, without surprise, simply noting a fact for the record. He was still standing on the step outside the new door, ledger under one arm, inkpot in his hand, faintly steaming in the cool air before dawn.

"Yes," said Mike. His voice came out smaller than he'd meant it to.

"Then I'll wait." Malphus looked past him into the dark hall. "May I come in? It's rather chilly. And I find doorsteps make people nervous."

Mike found, to his own surprise, that he had already stepped aside.

Malphus came in, wiped his feet carefully on the mat, and looked around the guild hall with mild, polite interest, the way a visitor might look around a museum that was not quite to his taste. His gaze passed over the leaning candles, over Durgan, still kneeling by the doorframe with his bag of chisels, over Pip, clutching his teacup, and over Velka, who had risen to her feet with her fingers crackling very faintly blue.

"Please don't," Malphus said to her pleasantly, without looking around. "It won't do anything, and you'll only singe the furniture."

Velka's fingers stopped crackling. She did not sit down.

Malphus crossed the hall to the clerk's alcove behind the front counter and stopped beside Mike's desk. On it, where Mike had left it the night before, lay the Registrar guide: three hundred and twelve pages in Mike's neatest hand, bound in board and twine, with *The Registrar: A Guide* written on the cover.

Malphus looked at it. Then he looked at Mike.

"Yours?"

"Yes."

"May I?"

And before Mike could think of a reason to refuse, the most terrifying being he had ever met in two lifetimes pulled out his chair, sat down at his desk, opened his guide to page one, and began to read.

---

He read quickly. Very quickly. His long pale finger moved down each page and turned it with a soft, regular whisper, every few seconds, like a clock. But he was not skimming. Mike could tell. Every so often the finger would stop, and go back up a line, and Malphus would make a small sound of interest, or approval, or once, at the long note under Rule 17, of something that might have been laughter.

Pip lasted about a quarter of an hour.

He had been standing as far from Malphus as the hall allowed, clutching his teacup, staring at the devil with enormous frightened eyes. But somewhere around page forty, Malphus stopped, frowned slightly, and tapped a line with his fingertip.

"Hm," he said. "Rule 12. *The Registrar will not accept a filing on a damp day without first being shown a dry towel.* Is that the full rule?"

And before he could stop himself, Pip said, in a small, high, automatic voice: "Footnote: it has to be *linen*."

Malphus looked up. His pale face lit with genuine delight.

"Does it! *Linen*. How marvelous." He took a slim silver pen from his coat pocket and wrote, very small, in the margin of Mike's guide, in red: *linen (per P.)*. Then he looked back at Pip. "Are there more?"

Pip stared at him. Then, slowly, like a man stepping onto ice, he came across the hall and stood beside the desk.

"Rule 14," he said. "Footnote: it only applies in winter."

"In winter," murmured Malphus, writing. "Of course it does."

Ten minutes later, Pip was perched on the corner of Mike's desk with his slate on his knees, and the two of them were bent over the guide together, heads almost touching, murmuring.

"Footnote: the left-side rule may be structural."

"Footnote to your footnote: in my experience, left-side rules almost always are."

"*Footnote*—"

"Footnote—"

Durgan, kneeling by the door with his chisels, watched this with an expression Mike had never seen on his face before. It was the expression of a man watching his oldest friend become best friends, over the course of ten minutes, with a contract devil.

"I don't like it," he said quietly to Mike.

"No," said Mike.

"He's *good* at it."

"I know."

The sky in the windows was turning gray when Malphus turned the last page. He sat for a moment with the guide closed in front of him, his long hands resting flat on its cover. Then he looked up at Mike.

"Very thorough," he said. "Truly. I've audited guilds that kept worse records in their treasuries than you keep on a golem." He tapped the cover once. "Rule 31 is a joke, you know."

Mike went very still.

Rule 31. *When filing any quest involving geese, address the Registrar as "Your Honk."* He had copied it down in his first month, from Osric, who had gotten it from Edwin, who had gotten it from somebody else. He had never tested it. Nobody had ever tested it. For eight years he had lain awake some nights wondering about it. It was very clearly a joke. But what if it wasn't? What if, the one time somebody filed a goose without saying *Your Honk*, the Registrar did something terrible? It was the most famous unanswered question in the guild.

"How do you know?" he said.

"I was here when it started," said Malphus. "Reign of Guildmaster Petronel. A clerk named Tobin Tully put it in the procedures book to see if anyone would notice." He smiled faintly. "No one did. For a hundred and forty years."

He slid the guide back across the desk to Mike, squared neatly to the edge.

"I'd leave it in," he said. "With a footnote. It's part of the system now."

---

The guild came in for the stand-up at eight. Malphus was still there.

He had taken a spot by the window, with the morning light behind him, and he stood there with his ledger under his arm and his hands folded and his pleasant, unremarkable face turned politely toward the circle as it formed. Nobody asked who he was. Since the Duke had started coming, Mike suspected, the guild had simply given up asking who anyone at the stand-up was.

The dog and the sheep were still outside, refusing to come in. They took one look at Malphus through the window and lay down flat on the cobbles.

Then Brannoc came striding out of his office, rubbing his hands, ready for the day. He saw the tall gray figure by the window, and stopped dead.

Mike braced himself for the explosion.

Instead, the Guildmaster's broad face broke into an enormous, delighted grin.

"*Malphus!*" he roared. He crossed the hall in three strides and seized the devil's thin pale hand in both of his own and pumped it. "Old friend! It's been, what, a year?"

"Eleven months," said Malphus. "And four days."

"Eleven months! Ha!" Brannoc clapped him on the back. Malphus did not move at all. "You're looking well!"

"Thank you. I always do."

"Everyone!" Brannoc turned to the circle, beaming. "This is Malphus! Old friend of the guild! Old friend of *mine*! We've done business for *years*!" He leaned toward Malphus and lowered his voice, though not by much. "You'll join us for the stand-up, of course?"

"I'd be delighted," said Malphus.

And so the stand-up began. The guild chanted the motto: *Bold in Deed, Swift in Service, Adequate in All Things.* Malphus did not chant. He listened to *Adequate* with a slight, thoughtful tilt of his head.

Then Brannoc raised the empty mug and led the three questions in unison, the way he did every morning, sixty voices together.

"WHAT DID YOU DO YESTERDAY? WHAT WILL YOU DO TODAY? WHAT IS IN YOUR WAY?"

And into the silence that always followed, the silence where nobody ever answered, a quiet, pleasant voice by the window answered.

"Yesterday," said Malphus, "compounded."

The circle went very still.

"Today," said Malphus, "compounding."

Someone dropped something.

"In my way," said Malphus, and paused, thoughtfully, as if considering the question with genuine care. "Nothing."

He looked around the circle, at sixty faces, one by one, with his mild, polite smile.

"Nothing," he added, "is ever in my way."

No one breathed.

At the edge of the circle, by the coat rack, Durgan Flintmantle, who was the only other person who had ever actually answered the three questions, looked across at the devil. The devil looked back. Something passed between them: an acknowledgment, Mike thought, from one honest answer to another.

Then Malphus reached into his coat and drew out a single sheet of folded paper, sealed with red wax. He held it out to Brannoc.

"Your interest notice," he said.

Brannoc took it, broke the seal, glanced at it, and his grin faltered for the first time. Then it came back, broader than ever, with the slightly too-wide cheer of a man trying not to look at a number.

"Ah," said Brannoc. "Yes. Yes, of course. Now, Malphus, old friend, about this. Could we perhaps, just this once, roll it over? To next year?"

Malphus looked at him with something very like fondness.

"You've rolled it over forty-one times," he said gently.

Brannoc opened his mouth. He closed it. He looked at the notice again, and then, slowly, his face changed. It filled with the bright, crafty light of a man who has just remembered he is rich.

"Wait," he said. "Wait right there."

He strode off to his office. He came back a minute later, staggering, with his arms full: a great armful of gilt frames, a dozen of them at least, stacked against his chest, each holding a single cream page with a single word in deep, wet-looking red.

He dumped them proudly on the floor at Malphus's feet.

"*Prestige!*" Brannoc announced. "Two hundred and thirteen of these, Malphus, and there's more where they came from! From His Grace the Duke of Varrenhold *himself*! Six years of *adequate*! Surely that's worth *something*?"

By the window, the Duke of Varrenhold, holding the dragon tooth, raised one eyebrow very slightly.

Malphus bent down. He picked up one of the frames and examined it with careful, courteous attention, holding it up to the light. He read the single word. He studied the seal. He looked, for a moment, at the red ink, and something flickered behind his pleasant face that might have been recognition.

Then he straightened and handed the frame back to Brannoc.

"I'm afraid," said Malphus, "that prestige is not legal tender in any of the nine hells."

The hall was silent.

Then, from the window, the Duke of Varrenhold raised the dragon tooth, as he did every morning, and asked the question.

"IS IT DONE YET?"

Malphus turned to him with genuine interest, as if meeting a colleague.

"Ah," he said. "Not yet." He tucked his ledger more firmly under his arm. "But it's *accruing*."

---

Brannoc could not read the terms.

This became clear after the stand-up, in the Guildmaster's office, when Malphus laid his ledger open on the desk and Brannoc stared at it for some time with his lips moving, then turned it sideways, then upside down, then pushed it away.

"Very small writing," he said.

"It's very small debt," said Malphus. "There's just a great deal of it." He looked around the office, past the weapons and the dragon head and the wall of red frames. "Is there someone who keeps your books?"

Brannoc, Priscilla, and Gareth, who were all in the room, all turned and pointed at Mike.

So Mike took the ledger to the Registrar.

He did not know why. It just seemed like the right place. He carried the heavy, dark-bound book into the round room, knocked three times on the lectern, said, "Esteemed Registrar, I need to read something," and laid it on the lectern beside the golem's own great ledger.

The Registrar's blue eyes swung down to the dark book. And for the first time in eight years, Mike saw the golem do something that was not in his guide. It drew its stamp arm back, very slowly, until it was pressed against its own stone chest, as far from Malphus's ledger as it could get. Its eyes dimmed to a faint, wary glimmer. Its humming stopped.

Mike noted that down, in the margin, for later.

Then he opened the ledger and began to read.

The first entry was two hundred years old. It was written in a faded brown that had once been red, and it was signed in the same long, looping hand that Mike had first seen when he was twelve years old, on a brittle page in the Registrar's oldest ledger. That signature had still been faintly warm then. It was faintly warm now.

> *Loan to the Founders of the Adventurers' Guild of Brindlemoor:*
>
> *For the purchase of one plot of land near the square (one demon, sealed).*
>
> *For one seal, standard grade, to contain the same.*
>
> *Terms: interest compounding annually, in perpetuity, until repaid in full.*

Mike read the second line again. *One seal, standard grade.*

Standard. Not the best. Not even good. Just the cheapest seal you could get. The founders couldn't afford a proper one. So they had borrowed the money for a cheap one, and promised themselves they'd come back and do it properly.

Before winter.

He turned the page.

After the founders came the Guildmasters, one after another, two hundred years of them. Each one had added a line or two in the dark ledger, and each line was the same shape, over and over, like a rhyme. Mike read them slowly.

> *Guildmaster Petronel: moat (decorative).*
>
> *Guildmaster Aldous: second moat (to drain first moat).*
>
> *Guildmaster Wenna: bridge (over both moats).*
>
> *Guildmaster Aldous II: removal of both moats and bridge.*

He stopped there for a moment.

Four Guildmasters. Four loans. A moat that nobody needed, then a second moat to fix the first, then a bridge so people could get across both moats, then the cost of removing all three. Eighty years of borrowing, and at the end of it, the guild hall had exactly what it had started with: no moat. And four lines of debt, compounding.

He had seen this before, in another world, in a thousand codebases. *Quick fix for the bug. Quick fix for the quick fix. Workaround for the second fix. Rewrite to remove all three.* Every leader paying for the last leader's shortcut with a shortcut of his own. Nobody ever paying for the first one.

He read on. Guildmaster after Guildmaster. A tower that leaned. A fountain that didn't. A wing that was built and then demolished and then rebuilt in the same place, slightly smaller. All of it on credit, all of it compounding.

And then, at last, Brannoc.

Brannoc's section was the longest in the book. It went on for pages. Mike recognized every line.

> *Bells, priority (3).*
>
> *Wizard, quick job (2).*
>
> *Twine, ward (one spool), for Contraption.*
>
> *Portrait, oil, of Gareth the Bold (large).*
>
> *Medal of Valor.*
>
> *Bunting, Grand Reopening.*
>
> *Scissors, golden.*

And after each line, in that same neat red hand, a number. And after each number, a smaller number. And after that, a smaller one still: the interest, and the interest on the interest, and the interest on that, going back and back and down and down, into figures so small and so many that they ran together into a thin red smear at the edge of every page.

---

He found the penalty clause on the last page. He read it twice.

Then he had to sit down.

There was no chair in the Registrar's room. He sat down on the floor, with his back against the lectern, and the dark ledger open on his knees.

> *In the event of default by the Guild, the following shall apply:*
>
> *1. The guild hall and all lands beneath it shall pass to the Lender.*
>
> *2. All members of staff shall be placed on call, in perpetuity.*

He looked at the second line for a very long time.

*On call. In perpetuity.*

He thought about a phone buzzing at 11:52 on a Tuesday night in Raleigh. He thought about Chad's voice: *Mike, buddy*. About a drive through dark streets to a dark office. About a coffee maker, and a new outlet, and the word BREW.

He had died on call. That had been the very end of his first life: answering a midnight page for a client who typed in capital letters. And here, in this world, in a dark book bound in something that was not quite leather, was a contract that would put him on call forever.

"Malphus," he said. He had not heard the devil come in. He was simply there, in the doorway, as if he'd always been. "What does *on call* mean? In the nine hells?"

Malphus considered the question with his usual care.

"Oh, it's quite simple," he said pleasantly. "A bell in every room. Rung at random. Forever."

Mike thought about the three brass bells behind the front counter. He thought about how they rang at a quarter to three every night, at random, for a man in a dressing gown who wanted to know if it was done yet.

"We're already halfway there," he said.

Malphus followed his gaze through the open door toward the counter, and nodded thoughtfully, as if Mike had made a fair point in a negotiation.

"Rather more than halfway," he said.

Mike looked down at the ledger, and asked the skill.

**Estimated total debt:**

The numbers began to appear.

They appeared one digit at a time, glowing, in a line. Then they kept appearing. The line ran across his field of vision, past the edge of the ledger, past the lectern, past the Registrar's great stone feet. It ran right off the edge of what he could see and kept going. When he turned his head to follow it, it scrolled along with him, still going, digit after digit after digit, with no sign of an end.

He closed his eyes. When he opened them, the skill had mercifully given up on the total and offered a simpler line instead.

**Estimated time to repay at current rate: 340 years.**

---

They held a meeting about it. Of course they did.

It was in the Guildmaster's office that afternoon: Brannoc behind his desk, Priscilla on his left, Gareth on his right, Malphus standing politely by the wall of red frames, Mike standing in front of the desk with the ledger. He had prepared. He had written it all out on one page, the way he'd written the crypt estimate, eight years ago: the total, as near as the skill would let him estimate it; the interest, year by year; the penalty clause; and at the bottom, in large clear letters, his recommendation.

*Stop taking new loans. Start paying the old ones down. Begin with the oldest.*

Brannoc read the recommendation. To Mike's surprise, he read it slowly, and nodded.

"Sensible," said Brannoc. "Very sensible. Stop borrowing. Pay it down." He nodded again, more firmly. "Yes. That's what we'll do."

Mike felt a small, astonished flicker of something that might have been hope.

Then Priscilla said: "Oh, but the *uniforms*."

Brannoc paused. "The uniforms."

"For Client Delight! New uniforms! I've already ordered them. Blue and gold, with little embroidered dragons. They're arriving Thursday."

"Ah," said Brannoc. "Right. Well, we'll need to pay for those, of course."

"And the Contraption," said Priscilla. "The consortium wants *five more*, remember? And they all have to float."

"Mm," said Brannoc.

"And my statue," said Gareth.

Everyone looked at him.

"For the square," said Gareth, with simple dignity. "By the fountain. Facing the new door. In bronze. They've already started the plinth."

There was a long silence.

Malphus stepped forward from the wall. He reached into his coat, drew out his silver pen, uncapped it, and turned to the Guildmaster with the warm, helpful attentiveness of a very good shopkeeper.

"Would you like," he said, "to increase your limit?"

Brannoc's face lit up like dawn.

"*Could* we?"

Mike stood in front of the desk with his single page of recommendations in his hand and watched the silver pen move across the dark ledger, adding three new lines. *Uniforms (Client Delight). Contraptions (5), floating. Statue, bronze, of Gareth the Bold.*

When it was done, Malphus capped his pen. As he slipped it back into his coat, his eyes met Mike's across the office.

It was only for a moment. But in that moment there was something in the devil's pale, pleasant face that Mike had seen before, many times, in another world: on the faces of finance people, and database administrators, and anyone else who had ever stood at the back of a meeting and watched a room full of people choose the new thing over the old debt, again, knowing exactly how it would end.

It was a look of quiet professional sympathy, between two people who both understood compound interest.

Mike found that he could not decide whether that was comforting, or the most frightening thing that had happened all day.

---

Malphus left at sundown.

Mike walked him to the door. He didn't know why. It seemed like the polite thing to do, and Malphus was, in his strange way, the most polite person who had ever visited the guild. They walked together through the hall, past the leaning candles, to the new oak door on the east wall.

Malphus stopped at the threshold, and looked down at the doorframe.

He crouched, unhurried, the way he did everything, and studied the copied runes. Then his gaze traveled down to the bottom of the frame, to the two small notes carved side by side between the runes: the wizard's faithful copy, *TEMPORARY. REPLACE BEFORE WINTER.*, and beside it, square and fresh and dwarvish, *TEMPORARY. SORRY.*

He read them both. Then he smiled.

It was not his usual smile, the pleasant, polite one that never went anywhere. This one went all the way up into his pale eyes and lit them from inside, like a lamp in a window. It was the warmest expression Mike had ever seen on his face. It was, Mike realized, the smile of a man looking at something he truly loved.

"I do love a temporary solution," said Malphus softly.

He straightened up.

"They're my best customers."

Then he turned to Mike. And his voice changed, very slightly. It became quieter, more serious, almost kind.

"You keep very good records," he said. "Rare, in a place like this. Very rare. I want you to know I noticed." He tucked his ledger under his arm. "Your first payment is due in forty-one days. On that day, I'll be back, and I'll audit the guild's accounts." He paused. "When I audit, I only ever audit the records. Nothing else. Not speeches. Not medals. Not prestige." He glanced, for a moment, toward the Guildmaster's office and the wall of red frames inside it. "Only what's written down."

He inclined his head a quarter inch, exactly the way the Duke's steward did, and Hesper Vane.

"Good evening, Mikel Dobbins," he said.

And he stepped out through the new door into the dusk and was gone, between one step and the next, the way a shadow is gone when you light a lamp.

Mike stood in the doorway for a long time.

Then he asked the skill. He already knew what it would tell him. He asked anyway.

**Estimated time until the first payment: 41 days.**

**Estimated time until the demon wakes: 48 days.**

One week apart. Two clocks, ticking toward each other.

Behind him, in the darkening hall, footsteps. Mike turned. Guildmaster Brannoc was standing alone in the middle of the floor. The office door was open behind him, and the wall of red ADEQUATEs glowed faintly in the candlelight, every one of them leaning slightly north.

Brannoc was not looking at Mike. He was looking past him, at the door, at the place where Malphus had stood. His big face, for once, was perfectly still. There was nothing in it at all. No boom, no bluster, no grin. Just a tired old man in a hall full of leaning candles.

He stood there for a long moment.

Then he said, to nobody, very quietly:

"I need a drink."
