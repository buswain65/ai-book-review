### Chapter 10: Resource Allocation & Hardware Hacking

<br/>

The news of what happened on the south road traveled through Oakhaven’s lower district with surprising speed. By the time Mark arrived at the south armory forge the next morning, the usual ambient chatter of the laborers died down, replaced by wide-eyed glances and hushed whispers.

<br/>

Garek had checked into a local apothecary late last night to get his wrist wrapped, claiming he had "tripped over a stone." But everyone in the timber yards knew the truth: the Ironhead Syndicate’s meanest enforcer had been dropped in five seconds by the new fourteen-year-old coal boy.

<br/>

Smith Master Torren was waiting by the storage pit, his thick, hairy arms crossed over his leather apron. He gave Mark a long, appraising look as the boy stepped up to the wheelbarrows.

<br/>

"Garek came by my house before dawn," Torren grunted, his deep voice rattling his chest. "Told me to keep you away from his turf, said you move like a demon and use un-sanctioned stone magic."

<br/>

Mark picked up a shovel, his face completely neutral. "I don't use magic to haul coal, Torren. And Garek’s brother was harassing an errand boy."

<br/>

Torren stared at him for three long seconds, then let out a sharp, sudden bark of laughter that shook his braided beard.

<br/>

"Harassing? Garek’s a brute who needed his teeth kicked in three years ago," Torren chuckled, wiping a speck of soot from his eye. "Put the shovel down, Mark. I'm taking you off coal."

<br/>

Mark paused. "Am I fired?"

<br/>

"Fired? Hell no. You're too useful," Torren said, gesturing toward a heavy wooden door at the rear of the main forge. "I need someone who pays attention. You're working the scrap sorting and tool repair yard now. Five coppers a day, plus access to the offcut bins."

<br/>

Mark’s eyes lit up. "Five coppers?"

<br/>

"And if you need a place to stay that isn't a loud tavern, there's a stone storage shed behind the coal pit," Torren added, tossing a iron key to Mark, who caught it smoothly. "It’s dry, it’s got a heavy bolt on the door, and it’s yours for two coppers a week."

<br/>

Mark looked at the iron key in his hand. A private, secure workspace in the heart of the metalworking district, paid for by half a day’s wages.

<br/>

"Deal," Mark said.

---

By late afternoon, Mark had settled into his new "laboratory."

<br/>

The shed was small—roughly eight by ten feet—with thick granite walls, a heavy oak door, and a dirt floor set two feet below street level. A small high window let in the pale green afternoon sunlight, illuminating a sturdy workbench made of solid oak timbers.

<br/>

Mark sat at the bench, pulled his leather notebook from his tunic, and opened it to a fresh page, writing in swift, clean English cursive:

<br/>

> *System Architecture Notes — Mark Vance*
> *Log 03: Workspace Secured & Hardware Acquisition*
> *Current Objective: Reverse-engineer local magical artifacts to identify the physical substrate layer of spell logic. Moving beyond biological channeling to static hardware execution.*

<br/>

He set the charcoal stick down and looked at the item he had salvaged from Torren’s scrap bin earlier that afternoon.

<br/>

It was a discarded, broken Aether-lamp regulator—the same kind used in the town's streetlamps. The casing was cast bronze, stamped with the seal of the Oakhaven Mages' Guild, but the top nozzle was charred black from a short-circuit explosion that had cracked the glass housing.

<br/>

Mark reached into his pocket, pulled out a small iron chisel, a magnifying lens he’d borrowed from Torren, and a small needle-file, and got to work.

<br/>

He disassembled the casing methodically, laying the components out in a neat, organized row on his oak bench like an engineer performing an autopsy on a server motherboard:

<br/>

1. **Power Supply:** A fingernail-sized shard of pale blue mana-crystal, dull and depleted, resting in a padded lead socket.
2. **Conductors:** Two thin, flat copper ribbons routing from the crystal socket toward the burner head.
3. **Control Board:** A circular, silver-plated brass disc, about the size of a silver dollar, mounted directly between the copper conductors and the ignition nozzle.

<br/>

Mark picked up the silver-plated disc and held it under the magnifying lens, angling it toward the green sunlight from the window.

<br/>

The surface of the disc was covered in microscopic, razor-thin grooves—intricate, geometric patterns that the locals called "runes." Near the center of the disc, a thick black scorch mark bridged three parallel lines, where the metal had melted and fused together.

<br/>

Mark stared at the microscopic grooves, his software-architect brain instantly recognizing the layout.

<br/>

*"It's not writing,"* Mark whispered, a cold surge of electricity running down his spine. *"It's not a prayer, and it's not a spell."*

<br/>

He leaned closer, tracing the lines with his needle-file.

<br/>

The grooves weren't decorative. They were **printed circuit traces**.

<br/>

The depth of the cut dictated the resistance of the line. The angle of the intersections created physical switches and logic gates. Where two lines crossed separated by a thin layer of non-conductive varnish, it formed a basic capacitor. The entire "rune" was simply a three-dimensional passive circuit board carved out of metal to route Aether without needing a human brain to hold the instructions.

<br/>

"The mages aren't performing miracles," Mark murmured to the empty shed, a wide, triumphant grin breaking across his face. "They're just burning hardcoded ROM chips."

<br/>

When the regulator had shorted out, it wasn't because of a curse or a failure of faith—a surge of Aether had jumped the gap between two un-insulated copper traces, melting the silver plate and creating a bridge that locked the circuit in a closed-loop feedback failure.

<br/>

"Standard short-circuit," Mark said. "Fixable in five minutes."

<br/>

He took his needle-file and carefully scraped away the melted, fused silver, clearing the scorch mark until the two parallel traces were physically separated once more. He tore a tiny strip of dry parchment from his notebook, soaked it in a drop of resin from a pine wood scrap, and wedged it between the traces as a temporary insulator.

<br/>

Next, he looked at the grounding pathway. The original design relied entirely on the bronze casing to absorb the feedback, which had eventually overheated the unit.

<br/>

Mark took a six-inch piece of thin copper wire from Torren's offcut bin, wrapped one end securely around the brass disc’s outer ring, and drove an iron nail straight through the dirt floor into the damp earth below, wrapping the other end of the wire around the nail.

<br/>

*"Grounding strap installed,"* Mark noted mentally.

<br/>

He picked up the tiny blue mana-crystal shard, re-seated it into the lead socket, and pressed the small copper actuator lever on the side of the housing.

<br/>

*Click.*

<br/>

There was no explosion. No violent spark. No cloud of smoke.

<br/>

A steady, silent, perfectly formed needle of cool blue flame erupted from the burner nozzle, casting a bright, flicker-free light across the stone walls of the shed. The bronze casing remained completely cool to the touch, the excess feedback draining silently down the copper wire and into the dirt beneath his feet.

<br/>

Mark sat back in his stool, watching the blue light reflect in the smooth skin of his young hands.

<br/>

In Raleigh, he had spent forty years maintaining legacy systems designed by people who thought complexity equaled brilliance. Here, in a fantasy world of wooden carts and cloaked mages, he had just fixed his first piece of hardware using basic electrical theory and a scrap file.

<br/>

He picked up his charcoal stick, opened his English ledger, and wrote a single, quiet declaration at the bottom of the page:

<br/>

> *Log 03 (Summary): Runes identified as passive hardware logic circuits. Disassembled, repaired, and re-grounded a Guild-standard regulator unit. System operational.*
> *Conclusion: I don't need their Academies. I just need more copper.*

<br/>

Mark closed the ledger, blew out the blue flame with a quiet smile, and sat in the peaceful dark of his workshop, listening to the rain begin to patter softly against the roof outside.
