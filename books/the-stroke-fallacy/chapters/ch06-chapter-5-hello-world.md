### Chapter 5: Hello World

<br/>

The cart groaned rhythmically as its wooden wheels ate up the miles of dirt road heading south. Pine needles blanketed the trail, dulling the sound of the draft horse's hooves, while the late afternoon sun filtered through the dense canopy in golden bars.

<br/>

Mark sat wedged between two burlap sacks of grain in the back of the wagon, staring down at his smooth, fourteen-year-old hands.

<br/>

In software engineering, before you build a sprawling enterprise architecture, you write a tiny, foolproof script. You print `"Hello, World!"` to the console. You verify that the compiler works, the environment is live, and your inputs produce a predictable output.

<br/>

Mark closed his eyes and began to tune his awareness inward.

<br/>

He didn't use the grand, mystical meditation postures taught in fantasy books. Instead, he defaulted to what his body already knew: the diaphragmatic breathwork from his Kung Fu forms and Jiu-Jitsu sparring. Inhale through the nose for four counts, expand the belly, hold, exhale smoothly through the mouth.

<br/>

Within three cycles, his heart rate slowed. His perception of his own body sharpened—the weight of his hips against the wooden cart floor, the subtle tension in his shoulders, the warm blood pulsing through his capillaries.

<br/>

And then, just beneath his skin, he felt it.

<br/>

It wasn't a mystical revelation. It felt remarkably like static electricity—the faint, prickling buzz you get on your forearms when you stand near an old CRT monitor or touch a metal door handle on a dry winter day in North Carolina. The air around the cart was saturated with it.

<br/>

*"The ambient Aether,"* Mark noted silently. *"Background voltage."*

<br/>

He picked up a smooth, gray river pebble from the bottom of the wagon. Holding it loosely between his right thumb and index finger, he attempted his first program.

<br/>

He didn't chant pseudo-Latin. He didn't picture a mystical dragon. He treated the static prickle in his chest like fluid, channeling a tiny droplet of it down his arm, through his forearm flexors, and into the stone, aiming for a basic thermal output: *Increase temperature by five degrees.*

<br/>

For three seconds, nothing happened. Mark adjusted his mental alignment, focusing on the friction of heat—the way he used to imagine warming his hands after kayaking cold mountain rivers.

<br/>

*Zap.*

<br/>

A tiny, brilliant blue spark popped between his thumb and the stone. It delivered a sharp static shock that made his arm jerk violently, dropping the pebble onto the cart floor.

<br/>

"Ow," Mark hissed, shaking his stinging hand.

<br/>

Across the cart, Vespera’s head snapped toward him. Her sharp brown eyes locked onto the pebble, which was now emitting a faint, wispy curl of grey smoke where his thumb had rested.

<br/>

Her expression turned to sheer, unadulterated shock.

<br/>

"What did you just do?" she asked, her voice dropping an octave.

<br/>

"Failed execution," Mark muttered, rubbing his stinging thumb. "Voltage spiked. Need a better resistor."

<br/>

Vespera slid across the bench, leaning over him, her wooden staff held tight in her hand. "Did you just draw Aether? Without a catalyst? Without an incantation?"

<br/>

"I was just testing the line," Mark said defensively. "I tried to transfer a tiny amount of thermal energy into the rock. Got a static discharge instead."

<br/>

"You could have scorched your own nerve pathways, you foolish boy!" Vespera scolded, though her tone was laced with disbelief. "Aether is not a toy to be poked with your bare fingers! The incantation acts as the vessel to shape the force. Without the vessel, the energy grounds itself through *you*!"

<br/>

*"A grounding issue,"* Mark thought, his eyes lighting up. *"Exactly like an ungrounded circuit."*

<br/>

"So that's why you carry the staff?" Mark asked, pointing to the carved wood in her hand. "It's an external conductor. An antenna to offload heat and feedback so it doesn't cook your nervous system?"

<br/>

Vespera opened her mouth to lecture him on sacred tradition, then paused, her brows knitting together as she parsed his strange vocabulary.

<br/>

"It... it acts as an anchor, yes," she admitted slowly. "A focused conduit of refined wood or metal absorbs the residual backlash. But no one—no student at the Academies—simply *feels* the flow on their first attempt without hours of guided chanting!"

<br/>

"It's just body awareness," Mark said simply. "If you know how to breathe and control your nervous system, you can feel where the energy gathers. It's like finding your center of gravity when someone tries to sweep your leg in BJJ."

<br/>

"In what?"

<br/>

"Never mind." Mark picked up the pebble again, turning it over in his hand. "So if I want to run a script—a spell—safely, I either need a conduit to ground the excess, or I need to write... err, *speak* the syntax very precisely to bound the parameters."

<br/>

Vespera stared at him as if he were speaking an ancient dialect of dragons. "You are the strangest child I have ever encountered."

<br/>

Before Mark could reply, the horse slowed, and Garin’s booming voice called back from the driver's bench.

<br/>

"End of the line, folks! Oakhaven ahead!"

<br/>

Mark stood up in the wagon, looking over the driver's shoulder.

<br/>

Where the forest cleared, a sprawling settlement sat nestled in the valley. High timber palisade walls protected a bustling town of timber-framed houses, blacksmith chimneys billowing dark smoke, and cobblestone streets filled with travelers, merchants, and armored guards. Beyond the town, rolling green hills stretched toward jagged, snow-capped mountains in the far distance.

<br/>

A heavy wooden sign hung above the main gatehouse, lit by glowing glass lanterns that flickered with a soft, steady blue light—Aether-powered streetlamps.

<br/>

Garin turned to Mark with a grin. "Welcome to civilization, kid. First round's on me at *The Pine & Anvil*."

<br/>

Mark looked at the glowing lanterns, the timber gates, and his own young, undamaged hands. His 62-year-old life of corporate deadlines, arthritic joints, and endless Slack messages felt a million miles away.

<br/>

He hopped down from the cart as it rolled through the gates, landing lightly on his feet.

<br/>

*"Hello, World,"* Mark thought with a grin. *"Let's see what else this system can do."*
