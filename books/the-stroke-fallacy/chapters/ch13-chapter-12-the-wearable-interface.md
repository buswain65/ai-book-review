### Chapter 12: The Wearable Interface

<br/>

The late-night air inside Mark’s private stone shed was thick with the smells of pine resin, hot metal, and oiled leather. A single tallow candle flickered on the edge of the oak workbench, casting long shadows against the granite walls.

<br/>

Mark sat on his stool, his needle-file working with meticulous, rhythmic strokes across a thin strip of refined copper plate.

<br/>

He had spent two of his hard-earned silver coins at Torren’s supply bin for raw materials: four feet of fine silver wire offcuts, a flexible strip of thick pigskin leather, and two small brass rivets.

<br/>

To an outsider, he was making a simple leather wrist guard—the kind an apprentice blacksmith wore to keep flying sparks from burning their forearms. But under Mark’s magnifying lens, it was an integrated circuit board.

<br/>

Using his iron scribe, Mark traced razor-thin grooves along the inside of the leather strap. He took the fine silver wire and inlaid it directly into the grooves, laying down two parallel traces that mirrored the pulse point on his inner right wrist, right where his radial artery sat closest to the skin.

<br/>

On the outer face of the leather, he riveted the copper plate, filing three shallow, diagonal grooves across its surface to serve as passive heat-dissipation fins.

<br/>

He connected the inner silver traces to the outer copper plate using two tiny brass rivets, creating a solid conductive bridge through the leather.

<br/>

*"Conduit complete,"* Mark thought, examining his handiwork under the candle flame. *"Hardware Prototype 1.0."*

<br/>

He slid the cuff over his right hand and buckled the brass clasp tight against his forearm. The pigskin leather was stiff, but the smooth silver wire rested flush against his skin, cool and unobtrusive. It didn't restrict his wrist movement or interfere with his grip.

<br/>

He flexed his fingers, making a tight fist, then rotated his forearm.

<br/>

*"Low-profile personal heatsink,"* Mark noted mentally. *"No staff required. No conspicuous magical paraphernalia."*

---

Mark sat barefoot on the cold dirt floor of the shed, positioning his legs into a comfortable, cross-legged posture. He rested his hands on his knees, palms facing up, the copper cuff catching the dim candlelight.

<br/>

He closed his eyes and initiated his breathing sequence.

<br/>

*Inhale: four counts. Expand the diaphragm, drawing a controlled trickle of ambient Aether into his chest.*
*Exhale: four counts. Route the energy down through his right shoulder, past his elbow, and into his forearm flexors.*

<br/>

Normally, without a staff or an incantation, this was the point where the ungrounded voltage would spike—delivering a painful static shock to his nerves or causing a brutal cognitive headache as his brain struggled to contain the force.

<br/>

This time, the moment the Aether reached his wrist, the silver wire traces caught the current.

<br/>

Mark felt a faint, prickling warmth against his skin—not a burn, but a smooth, steady transfer of thermal energy. The silver traces drew the feedback away from his radial nerve, funneling the excess heat directly into the outer copper plate.

<br/>

The copper cuff on his wrist gave off a faint, soft blue glow, radiating the thermal energy harmlessly into the cool air of the room.

<br/>

Mark opened his eyes, staring at his arm in awe.

<br/>

There was no burning pain in his tendons. No numbness in his fingers. No thumping tension headache behind his left eye. The physical hardware was taking the thermal load off his biological tissue, acting as a passive surge protector between his nervous system and the ambient Aether.

<br/>

*"System feedback managed,"* Mark grinned, a deep surge of professional satisfaction washing over him. *"Hardware bug resolved."*

<br/>

Now he needed to test it under load.

---

Mark slipped out the rear door of his shed and stepped into the deserted armory yard.

<br/>

The town was silent beneath the silver and green moons. A light fog hung over the cobblestones, catching the pale blue glow of the distant streetlamps. The heavy iron hearths stood dark and cold, and the stacks of raw timber sat undisturbed in the shadows.

<br/>

Near the center of the yard stood a thick, vertical oak post—a three-foot-wide structural support used for testing iron fittings.

<br/>

Mark stepped up to the post, stopping six feet away.

<br/>

He didn't reach for a staff. He didn't speak a word. He stood tall, relaxed his shoulders, and cleared his mind, calling up his pre-compiled **Impact Macro**.

<br/>

He mapped the instruction block simultaneously:

<br/>

1. **Source:** Ambient Aether in a two-foot radius around his torso.
2. **Conduit:** Route through right arm into the wristband.
3. **Conversion:** Kinetic compression + localized thermal friction.
4. **Target:** Oak post, six feet forward.

<br/>

Mark stepped into a low, crisp Bow stance, sinking his weight into his thighs and dropping his right heel hard onto the dirt floor—locking his root into the earth.

<br/>

He thrust his right palm straight forward toward the oak post, exhaling sharply through his nose.

<br/>

*Pop!*

<br/>

A sharp, compressed crack echoed through the quiet yard, sounding like a small whip snapping in the fog.

<br/>

A invisible wave of compressed force slammed into the oak post, punching a two-inch-deep crater straight into the hard timber and scorching the surrounding grain black with a flash of heat.

<br/>

The copper cuff on Mark’s wrist flared with a brilliant, clear blue light for half a second as it dumped the residual thermal backlash, the heat dissipating into the night air with a soft *hiss*.

<br/>

Mark pulled his hand back, settling into an upright stance.

<br/>

He looked at his right wrist. The skin beneath the cuff was slightly pink, but entirely undamaged. His nerves were fine. His head was clear.

<br/>

"Clean execution," Mark whispered, a triumphant smile breaking across his face. "Zero syntax overhead."

<br/>

Then, his knees buckled.

<br/>

Mark stumbled backward, catching himself against a wooden wheelbarrow to keep from falling into the dirt.

<br/>

A sudden, intense wave of dizziness swept over his head. His heart hammered in his chest, and his stomach let out a violent, painful cramp—a sharp, hollow hunger that made his vision swim.

<br/>

He slid down the side of the wheelbarrow, sitting hard in the dirt, panting for air as if he had just run a marathon.

<br/>

*"What... what was that?"* Mark thought, rubbing his swimming temples. *"The heat didn't burn my arm. The voltage didn't overload my brain."*

<br/>

He sat in the dark for two minutes, taking slow, ragged breaths, until his heart rate finally began to settle.

<br/>

His analytical mind immediately began troubleshooting the crash.

<br/>

The Aether was ambient—it came from the air around him. The wristband handled the thermal feedback. The stance handled the grounding.

<br/>

*So why was he exhausted?*

<br/>

Mark reached into his tunic pocket, pulled out a cold, leftover sausage he had saved from dinner, and devoured it in three ravenous bites. As the food hit his stomach, the intense dizziness began to fade, replaced by a dull, familiar fatigue.

<br/>

Mark stared at the scorched oak post, the realization hitting him like a cold shower.

<br/>

*"Energy conversion requires a catalyst,"* Mark analyzed silently. *"Aether provides the external force, but my biological metabolism is the spark plug. Converting ambient voltage into physical kinetic work burns actual calories. My fourteen-year-old body isn't a magical well... it's a battery."*

<br/>

The wristband had solved his **hardware/thermal bug**, but it had instantly exposed his **power supply bottleneck**. High-voltage output required high-calorie input. If he tried to cast a high-level spell without proper physical fuel, his adolescent body would simply drain its own blood sugar and pass out cold.

<br/>

Mark pushed himself up from the dirt, his legs feeling like lead, and walked slowly back into his warm, stone shed.

<br/>

He sat at his workbench, pulled his leather ledger toward himself, dipped his charcoal stick, and wrote in neat, precise English cursive:

<br/>

> *System Architecture Notes — Mark Vance*
> *Log 05: Wearable Conduit 1.0 Deployed & Field-Tested*
> *Results:*
> *1. Wrist-mounted silver/copper heatsink successfully offloads thermal feedback during non-verbal casting. Zero nerve damage recorded.*
> *2. Kinetic/Thermal macro executed silently at 6-foot range.*
> *New Bottleneck Identified:*
> *Power Supply / Battery Capacity. Biological metabolism acts as the primary ignition catalyst. High-voltage execution causes rapid glycemic crash and metabolic fatigue.*
> *Next Phase:*
> *Explore external energy storage. Investigate mana-crystals as external capacitors to offload metabolic drain.*

<br/>

Mark closed the ledger, blew out the tallow candle, and lay down on his simple cot in the dark.

<br/>

His body was exhausted, his muscles ached, and his stomach was already growling for breakfast... but as he closed his eyes, Mark Vance slept with the deep, peaceful contentment of an engineer who had just solved his hardest bug of the week.
