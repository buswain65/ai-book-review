### Chapter 15: Dual-Crystal Logic & The First Glitch

<br/>

The autumn rain lashed against the small, high window of Mark’s shed, drum-rolling on the granite sill. Inside, the air smelled of tallow smoke, fresh pine resin, and the sharp, metallic tang of scraped brass.

<br/>

Lyra sat on a low wooden three-legged stool opposite Mark, a small leather tool roll spread across her knees. On the oak workbench between them sat her delivery: a canvas pouch filled with scrap silver wire offcuts, three dull, slate-grey mana-crystals from the Chapterhouse waste chute, and two blank brass backing plates she had smuggled out in her apron pockets.

<br/>

"So," Lyra said, tracing a finger across a diagram Mark had drawn on a clean sheet of parchment. "You're saying if I connect the second crystal in line with the first, it doesn't double the heat—it doubles the flow?"

<br/>

"In series, voltage adds up," Mark said, tapping the drawing with his charcoal stick. "In parallel, current adds up. If you wire two recharged crystals side-by-side, you aren't just pushing harder—you're opening two lanes on a highway instead of one."

<br/>

Lyra squinted at the drawing, her grey eyes sharp under the flickering blue light of Mark’s desktop lamp. "In the workshop, Master Vane always said placing two stones in the same fitting creates 'aetheric dissonance.' He told us the spirits inside the glass would fight each other and shatter the casing."

<br/>

"Master Vane was looking at un-insulated copper traces," Mark replied smoothly, picking up his needle-file. "If you don't isolate the return paths, the current jumps between the sockets, creates a short, and blows the housing apart. It's not dissonance, Lyra. It's bad circuit board layout."

<br/>

Lyra let out a quiet, breathy laugh, shaking her head. "If my master heard you call two hundred years of Imperial casting 'bad layout,' he’d drop dead of a stroke."

<br/>

"Then let's make sure he doesn't hear us," Mark smiled. "Hand me that small silver wire."

<br/>

For two hours, they worked side-by-side in quiet, focused synchronization.

<br/>

Mark wasn't building a simple repair unit tonight; he was assembling **Hardware Prototype 2.0**.

<br/>

He took a fresh band of thick pigskin leather, wider than his original cuff, and riveted two lead-padded brass sockets onto the outer face. Along the inner lining, he ran a dual-track silver trace array—two independent, isolated channels running parallel to the pulse points on his inner wrist.

<br/>

On the outer copper plate, he filed six angled heat-dissipation fins. But the real upgrade was the switch.

<br/>

Mark crafted a tiny, spring-loaded silver sliding pin mounted right near his right palm. Operated by a subtle, invisible nudge of his thumb against the base of his index finger, the sliding pin allowed him to toggle execution modes on the fly:

<br/>

* **Position Alpha (Left):** Runs Primary Crystal only. Single-target kinetic or thermal macro.
* **Position Beta (Right):** Bridges Primary and Secondary Crystals in parallel. High-throughput dual-channel execution.

<br/>

"Try it on," Lyra urged, leaning forward, resting her chin in her hands.

<br/>

Mark slid his right hand through the leather cuff and buckled the double brass clasp tight around his forearm. The assembly felt solid, well-balanced, and surprisingly lightweight. It fit comfortably beneath the cuff of his linen tunic, completely invisible unless he pulled back his sleeve.

<br/>

He picked up two dull, slate-grey mana-crystals from the bench.

<br/>

Without speaking, Mark sat barefoot on the dirt floor, dropped into his rooted Kung Fu stance, and attached his charging lead to his new wristband. He passed a smooth, low-amperage trickle charge through the silver traces.

<br/>

Beside him, Lyra watched in open fascination as both grey stones pulsed simultaneously. Needle-thin threads of azure light danced through the crystalline facets, expanding rapidly until both shards glowed with a brilliant, cool blue luminescence.

<br/>

Mark unhooked the leads and seated both charged crystals firmly into the brass sockets on his wrist cuff, tightening the copper retaining clips over top.

<br/>

*Snap-clack.*

<br/>

The twin crystals pulsed in quiet harmony, their blue light reflecting off the copper fins on his arm.

<br/>

"System initialized," Mark murmured, flexing his fingers. "Prototype 2.0 ready for load testing."

---

To test a high-throughput dual-crystal macro, Mark couldn't just throw a light palm strike at a wooden post. He needed a dense, unyielding target that could absorb heavy kinetic force without shattering into splinters.

<br/>

Torren had provided the ideal test bed: a thick, hundred-pound rectangular block of cast iron ballast resting against the granite back wall of the shed.

<br/>

"Stand behind me, Lyra," Mark instructed quietly, stepping to the center of the dirt floor. "Right near the door."

<br/>

Lyra didn't argue. She backed up against the heavy oak door, her hand resting on her tool roll, her eyes glued to Mark's right arm.

<br/>

Mark stood eight feet from the iron block. He took a slow, deep diaphragmatic breath through his nose, expanding his core and calming his heart rate.

<br/>

*Inhale: four counts. Clear working memory.*
*Exhale: four counts. Sink weight, engage root.*

<br/>

Using his thumb, Mark nudged the tiny silver sliding pin on his palm to **Position Beta**.

<br/>

*Click.*

<br/>

A faint, high-frequency hum vibrated through the silver traces on his forearm as both azure crystals bridged into parallel execution.

<br/>

In his mind, Mark called up his upgraded **Dual-Target Impact Macro**:

<br/>

1. **Power Supply:** Draw simultaneous ignition current from Sockets 1 and 2 (zero metabolic drain).
2. **Logic Gate:** Split energy stream into two parallel vectors at 15-degree divergence.
3. **Conduit:** Route through silver trace array + triple-finned copper heatsink.
4. **Target:** Iron block—Point A (left face), Point B (right face).

<br/>

Mark dropped into a crisp Bow stance, drove his right heel into the dirt floor, and thrust his right palm forward toward the iron block.

<br/>

What happened next wasn't a loud explosion or a flash of fire.

<br/>

*THUM-CRACK!*

<br/>

An invisible, double-pronged shockwave tore through the air of the shed. The hundred-pound iron ballast block didn't just slide—it was slammed backward into the granite wall with a deafening impact, snapping the heavy cast metal cleanly down the center into two separate jagged halves.

<br/>

The copper fins on Mark’s wristband flared with a blinding, ice-blue light as they dumped the thermal backlash.

<br/>

Then, the world went strange.

<br/>

Mark didn't suffer a glycemic crash. His blood sugar was fine. His nerves didn't burn.

<br/>

Instead, the ambient Aether in the shed was instantly **sucked dry**.

<br/>

The warm air inside the small room dropped twenty degrees in a single heartbeat. Mark’s breath turned into a thick cloud of white mist in front of his face. A thin, crystalline layer of frost instantly bloomed across the workbench, creeping over the glass bottles, the iron files, and the leather ledger.

<br/>

Simultaneously, a sharp, metallic acoustic chime—a strange, crystalline tone vibrating at roughly fourteen kilohertz—echoed through the stone walls. It wasn't a physical sound in the air; it vibrated straight through Mark’s teeth and skull, ringing like a struck tuning fork.

<br/>

At the center of Mark's palm, a faint, pale-blue ring of geometric light pulsed outward—not scattering like normal magic, but expanding in a perfect, rigid circle that passed clean through the granite walls, through the dirt floor, and swept out across the armory district like a radar ping.

<br/>

Silence fell over the shed, save for the patter of rain on the roof and the soft *hiss* of frost melting off the copper fins on Mark’s wrist.

<br/>

Mark stood frozen in his stance, staring at his arm.

<br/>

Beside the door, Lyra was trembling, her mouth wide open, her hands pressed tightly over her ears.

<br/>

"Mark..." she whispered, her voice shaking violently as her breath puffed in the sudden cold. "What... what was that sound? What did you just do?"

<br/>

Mark slowly pulled his hand back, sliding the silver pin back to Position Alpha. The cold air began to settle, though the frost on his ledger was still wet under his fingers.

<br/>

He touched the twin crystals on his cuff. They weren't hot. They were shockingly, unnaturally cold to the touch.

<br/>

"That wasn't a short circuit," Mark said softly, his analytical brain processing the anomalous feedback loops.

<br/>

"It felt like... like the whole room lost its breath," Lyra stammered, stepping forward carefully, her boots crunching on the frosted dirt floor. "Magic doesn't make things freeze like that, Mark! It burns! It always burns!"

<br/>

"It burns when there's resistance," Mark corrected, his eyes narrow and calculating. "When you run a circuit with zero loss, there's no heat generated. But energy can't come from nowhere. The circuit pulled ambient thermal energy out of the room to equalize the vector state."

<br/>

He looked at the two halves of the cracked iron block, then at the dirt floor where the geometric ring had passed through.

<br/>

"And that chime," Mark added, rubbing his jaw where the vibration had lingered. "That wasn't acoustic noise. That was a carrier wave."

<br/>

"A what?" Lyra asked.

<br/>

"A signal," Mark said, a cold prickle running down his spine. "Standard Guild spellcasting is noisy and lossy—it's like a clunky old radio spewing static. But when we removed the impedance and ran dual-crystal parallel logic... we didn't just cast a spell, Lyra."

<br/>

He looked at her, his voice dropping into a quiet, dead-serious tone.

<br/>

"We just broadcast an un-encrypted data packet over a closed network."

---

Late that night, after a thoroughly rattled Lyra had gathered her tool roll and slipped back out into the rainy avenues of Oakhaven, Mark sat alone at his bench under the steady blue light of his desktop capacitor.

<br/>

He wiped the remaining moisture off his leather notebook, opened to a fresh page, and dipped his charcoal stylus.

<br/>

He wrote in swift, precise English cursive:

<br/>

> *System Architecture Notes — Mark Vance*
> *Log 08: Dual-Crystal Logic Array (Prototype 2.0) Field-Tested*
> *Hardware Configuration:*
> *Parallel dual-socket wrist conduit with dynamic thumb-switch execution (Alpha/Beta modes). Dual silver-trace layout with triple-finned copper heat sink.*
> *Test Results:*
> *1. Kinetic output scaled by ~250%. Cracked 100lb cast-iron ballast at 8-foot range.*
> *2. Zero metabolic fatigue recorded. Twin capacitors successfully provided 100% of ignition spark.*
> *Anomalous System Behavior:*
> *1. Thermal Vacuum: Instant drop in local ambient temperature (-20°C). Circuit absorbed ambient heat to satisfy zero-impedance energy conversion.*
> *2. Carrier Wave / System Ping: High-frequency harmonic oscillation (~14 kHz) accompanied by an expanding geometric pulse.*
> *Working Hypothesis:*
> *The global Aether grid is not an unmanaged natural phenomenon. It behaves like a network governed by a low-level background daemon or firewall.*
> *Standard Guild spells use high-resistance, lossy syntax that operates safely within rate-limited parameters.*
> *Clean, zero-impedance hardware execution bypasses these rate-limits, triggering an automated system audit ping.*
> *Next Objective:*
> *Monitor for external network responses.*

<br/>

Mark closed the ledger, unbuckled Prototype 2.0 from his wrist, and set it carefully beside his tools.

<br/>

He lay back on his simple cot, staring up at the dark timber rafters as the rain continued to fall outside. For forty years in North Carolina, whenever an unauthorized administrative override was executed on a secure network, an alert was generated in a security log somewhere.

<br/>

Mark closed his eyes, a dry, cold smile touching his lips.

<br/>

*"The code ran clean,"* Mark thought. *"Now let's see who owns the server."*

### *Stinger: Deep Vault 3 — Oakhaven Chapterhouse*

<br/>

Fifty feet beneath the cobblestone streets of Oakhaven, deep within the subterranean vaults of the Mages' Chapterhouse, the air was still, dry, and cold.

<br/>

Row upon row of iron-locked archives stood in the dark, housing centuries of tax records, land deeds, and Guild-sanctioned rune templates. At the center of the vault stood a six-foot pillar of seamless, unpolished obsidian—the town's ancient **Aether Anchor**, installed during the First Era of the Empire.

<br/>

For five hundred years, the obsidian pillar had stood dark, forgotten, and silent, serving merely as a passive grounding point for the town's warding fence.

<br/>

Then, the rain stopped dripping outside.

<br/>

Inside the pitch-black vault, a faint, high-frequency chime—vibrating at exactly fourteen kilohertz—resonated through the stone floor.

<br/>

On the face of the obsidian pillar, a single, razor-thin groove flared to life. It didn't pulse with the warm, familiar azure blue of standard Guild magic.

<br/>

It glowed a harsh, stark, crimson red.

<br/>

The red light flickered twice, scanning down the ancient face of the stone, recording a precise set of coordinates from the south armory district before settling into a slow, rhythmic, ominous pulse.

<br/>

Beneath the red glyph, a line of forgotten First Era script etched itself into the obsidian:

<br/>

`[ALERT: UNSANCTIONED ADMINISTRATIVE OVERRIDE DETECTED]`
`[ORIGIN: SECTOR 04 - LOCAL GRID]`
`[INITIATING SYSTEM AUDIT...]`
