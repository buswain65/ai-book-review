### Chapter 9: Security Patches and Retaliation

<br/>

Mark sat on a wooden bench in the rear courtyard of *The Pine & Anvil*, his palm-sized ledger rested flat on his knee. The evening air was cool, smelling of damp timber and pine resin.

<br/>

He tapped his charcoal stick against the paper, filling the page with neat, compact English cursive:

<br/>

> *System Architecture Notes — Mark Vance*
> *Log 02: Macro Compilation & Cognitive Caching*
> *Theory: Directing raw Aether in real time requires too much conscious calculation. Solution: Compile physical movements and breath patterns into pre-built subroutines. Like a Kung Fu form, a specific physical trigger (e.g., heel-drop + stance sink) should automatically fire a grounding pass without requiring manual mental evaluation.*

<br/>

A soft scuffle of footsteps made him close the notebook and tuck it into his tunic.

<br/>

Pip, the skinny twelve-year-old errand boy Mark had saved in the alleyway, slipped through the rear gate. He was breathing heavily, his face pale beneath a smattering of freckles.

<br/>

"Mark!" Pip hissed, scurrying over to the bench. "I've been looking all over for you!"

<br/>

"Calm down, Pip," Mark said, his voice entirely even. "Report. What's the incident?"

<br/>

Pip blinked at the odd phrasing, but hurried on. "Jax went straight to his older brother, Garek. Garek’s an enforcer for the Ironhead Syndicate down in the timber yards. Jax told him a foreign noble's kid humiliated him and broke his wrist. Garek’s furious. He’s waiting on the south road between the armory and the inn with two of his men. They know you work the coal pit!"

<br/>

Mark nodded slowly. It was a classic escalation loop—a minor failure in the field reported up the management chain to save face, resulting in over-allocation of resources.

<br/>

"They're going to jump you after dark," Pip warned, clutching Mark’s sleeve. "You can't go back to the forge tomorrow! You have to stay inside!"

<br/>

"Hiding doesn't resolve a security vulnerability, Pip," Mark said calmly. "It just leaves the exploit open until someone takes advantage of it. I appreciate the intelligence report, though. Go on home before your master misses you."

<br/>

Pip stared at him like he had lost his mind, but Mark gave him a reassuring pat on the shoulder, sending the boy back out into the dusk.

<br/>

Mark stood up, flexing his young fingers. He didn't buy a sword—he had no interest in wielding a heavy piece of steel he wasn't trained for. Instead, he walked over to a stack of firewood in the corner of the yard. He selected a thick, dense piece of hardwood offcut about a foot long, heavy as iron and completely dry, and slid it into the inner lining of his sleeve.

<br/>

From his pocket, he pulled a small linen pouch filled with fine iron dust he'd swept off Smith Master Torren's anvil earlier that day.

<br/>

*"Hardware prepared,"* Mark thought. *"Time to deploy the patch."*

---

Twilight had settled completely over Oakhaven by the time Mark walked down the narrow lane bordering the timber yards. High wooden fences flanked the dirt road, casting long shadows under the pale green light of the larger moon. A quarter-mile ahead, the blue glow of an Aether streetlamp marked the entrance to the main thoroughfare.

<br/>

Halfway down the lane, three figures stepped out from the shadows of a lumber stack, blocking the road.

<br/>

In the center stood a towering, broad-shouldered man in a studded leather jerkin. He had a thick, unkempt beard, a broken nose, and carried a heavy, iron-shod wooden truncheon rested lazily against his shoulder. Jax hovered behind him, his injured wrist wrapped in dirty cloth, a smug, vengeful sneer plastered across his face. The third man was lean and wiry, tossing a heavy iron rivet between his hands.

<br/>

Mark stopped twenty feet away, hands casually resting in the pockets of his linen trousers.

<br/>

"That him, Garek!" Jax spat, pointing a finger. "That's the little bastard!"

<br/>

Garek took a slow step forward, his boots crunching on the gravel. He squinted at Mark, clearly unimpressed by the skinny, unarmed fourteen-year-old before him.

<br/>

"You've got a big mouth for a stray, kid," Garek rumbled, his voice like grinding millstones. "Jax says you put hands on him. In Oakhaven, when you mess with an Ironhead, you pay in copper... or blood. Since you don't look like you have two coins to rub together, we're taking it out of your knees."

<br/>

Mark didn't reach for a weapon. He didn't tense up. He simply stepped into a low, relaxed horse stance, sinking his weight into his thighs and dropping his center of gravity, initiating his diaphragmatic breathwork.

<br/>

*Inhale: four counts. Pull ambient Aether into the chest.*
*Exhale: four counts. Route energy down through the legs, establishing a passive root into the damp earth.*

<br/>

"Garek," Mark said, his voice ringing with the quiet, cold authority of an auditor reviewing a failing project. "Your brother attempted to extort a child, lost a leverage match due to terrible balance, and ran to you to fix his mistake. You're deploying three men to handle a single teenager. It's an inefficient use of labor, and it reflects poorly on your syndicate's leadership."

<br/>

Garek’s eyes flashed with anger. "Get him!" he barked to the wiry thug beside him.

<br/>

The wiry man lunged forward with a wild, telegraphed right hook, putting all his momentum behind the punch.

<br/>

Mark didn't flinch. As the fist shot forward, Mark executed a classic Kung Fu deflecting palm—a sweeping *Pak Sau*—catching the thug's forearm from the inside and guiding the strike harmlessly past his ear. But as he redirected the blow, Mark executed his first pre-compiled macro: he released a sharp, silent pulse of grounded Aether directly into the damp earth beneath the thug's lead boot.

<br/>

A tiny, localized burst of thermal energy flash-boiled the moisture in the dirt beneath the man's heel.

<br/>

*Pop.*

<br/>

A small pocket of mud liquefied instantly. Coupled with his off-balance punch, the wiry thug skidded out as if he had stepped on wet ice, falling heavily onto his hip in the rutted dirt.

<br/>

Garek blinked, startled by the sudden slip, but his instincts kicked in. He charged forward, bringing his heavy iron-shod truncheon down in a savage overhead arc aimed straight for Mark’s shoulder.

<br/>

The wooden club came down fast, backed by two hundred pounds of muscle.

<br/>

Mark didn't try to dodge backward. Instead, he stepped *forward* into a low Bow stance, entering the space inside the weapon's effective reach. He brought both forearms up in an intersecting cross-block—a high *Tan Sau* structure reinforced by the hardwood stake tucked inside his right sleeve.

<br/>

*Thwack!*

<br/>

The impact shook the air, but Mark didn't absorb the force in his arms. The moment the wooden club struck his defense, Mark dropped his rear heel hard into the dirt, sinking deeper into his stance to execute his grounding subroutine.

<br/>

The kinetic force of Garek’s blow surged down through the hardwood stake, through Mark’s aligned skeletal structure, and straight down his legs into the earth. The dirt beneath Mark's boots cracked faintly, absorbing the shock wave without bending his frame.

<br/>

Garek’s eyes went wide as his heavy strike bounced off the boy's rigid, rooted frame.

<br/>

"Root," Mark whispered.

<br/>

Before Garek could pull the truncheon back, Mark swept his hands downward, snapping his palms across Garek's lead wrist in a sharp, double-handed redirection. He caught Garek’s wrist with his left hand and pressed his right palm flat against the bare skin of the enforcer's joint, throwing a handful of iron filings between his hand and Garek's skin.

<br/>

Now came the second macro: *Thermal Output.*

<br/>

Instead of an open flame, Mark executed his heat subroutine through the conductive iron dust, targeting the sensitive nerve clusters running along Garek's forearm.

<br/>

*Zap!*

<br/>

A sharp, searing spike of localized heat shot straight into Garek's wrist tendon.

<br/>

"Aagh!" Garek roared, his fingers reflexively snapping open. The heavy iron-shod truncheon clattered uselessly onto the cobblestones.

<br/>

Without breaking motion, Mark stepped past Garek's lead foot, using a swift Kung Fu foot-sweep behind the enforcer's heel while thrusting his palm lightly against Garek's chest. Deprived of his weapon and offset by the thermal shock, Garek’s heavy frame tipped backward, crashing onto his back in the dirt.

<br/>

In three fluid, economy-of-motion seconds, the enforcer was on the ground and his crew was neutralized.

<br/>

Behind them, Jax turned white as a sheet, backing away until his heels hit the timber fence.

<br/>

Mark stood over the syndicate enforcer, settling back into a calm, upright stance. His breathing was steady, his posture relaxed, his hands held loosely at his sides.

<br/>

"Here is the post-mortem on this encounter," Mark said, his voice cool and measured in the evening quiet. "Your strike was telegraphed, your root was weak, and your escalation path failed completely. I am a laborer trying to earn enough copper to buy parchment. I am not a threat to your syndicate unless you make me one."

<br/>

Mark stepped back two paces, hands returning casually to his pockets.

<br/>

Garek sat up slowly, cradling his scorched, aching wrist and staring at the dirt where Mark’s boots had rooted. There was a tiny, faint scorch mark in the mud where the grounded Aether had discharged.

<br/>

"What are you?" Garek rasped, his voice trembling slightly. "You're no farm boy. You move like a Master from the East... or a battle-mage."

<br/>

"I'm an engineer," Mark replied dryly. "And my billable hours are way too high for this kind of nonsense. Take your brother and go."

<br/>

Garek pushed himself up, glaring at Jax with sudden, furious irritation. He grabbed his younger brother by the collar of his shirt, dragging him down the alley toward the main road without looking back.

<br/>

Mark watched them vanish into the blue light of the streetlamps.

<br/>

He looked down at his hands, wiping the remaining iron dust off his palm onto his trousers. There was a faint, satisfying tingle in his forearm, but no headache, no burning nerves, and no cognitive hangover.

<br/>

He pulled his leather notebook from his tunic, flipped to his latest entry, and added a single line in neat English cursive:

<br/>

> *Log 02 (Update): Macro execution successful. Root-and-Deflect subroutine deployed with minimal cognitive overhead. System performance: optimal.*

<br/>

Mark closed the book, tucked it away, and started his walk back to *The Pine & Anvil*, a faint, dry smile playing on his lips.

<br/>

*"No Slack,"* Mark thought happily as the cool night wind blew through his hair. *"No Brad. And my runtime environment is getting smoother by the day."*
