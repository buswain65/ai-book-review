### Chapter 14: Peer Review & The Supply Chain

<br/>

A sharp, rhythmic knock rattled the heavy oak door of Mark’s shed mid-morning, cutting through the quiet rasp of his needle-file.

<br/>

Mark set his magnifying lens down on the workbench, stood up from his stool, and unbolted the door.

<br/>

Vespera stood in the sunlight, her indigo cloak pulled tidy around her shoulders. But she wasn't alone. Beside her stood a girl who looked roughly Mark's age—fourteen or fifteen—wearing a canvas work apron smudged with silver polish and red jeweler's rouge. A leather tool roll bristling with fine engraving chisels, gravers, and calipers hung slung across her chest.

<br/>

She had sharp, intelligent grey eyes, dark hair tied back in a messy knot, and an expression that made it abundantly clear she did not want to be standing behind a coal pit in the armory yard.

<br/>

"Mark," Vespera said, stepping inside without waiting for an invitation and gesturing to her companion. "This is Lyra. She is a journeyman engraver at the Guild-sanctioned Chapterhouse workshop."

<br/>

Mark nodded politely, stepping back to give them room in the small shed. "Mark. Nice to meet you."

<br/>

Lyra didn't return the greeting. She crossed her arms, her grey eyes sweeping over Mark’s cramped workspace—the dirt floor, the simple oak bench, the pile of salvaged bronze regulators, and the small, leather-bound notebook written in an indecipherable cursive.

<br/>

"This is the 'genius' you were telling me about, Vespera?" Lyra asked, her tone dripping with skepticism. "He's a fourteen-year-old scrap boy. He doesn't even have an engraver's vise."

<br/>

"I asked Lyra to come because she actually cuts the rune plates for our Chapterhouse," Vespera said, giving Mark a subtle, apologetic look. "If anyone in Oakhaven understands the physical casting process of Aether regulators, it's her."

<br/>

"I understand how to carve them *correctly*," Lyra corrected firmly, stepping up to the workbench. She pointed a finger at one of the repaired blower discs Mark had set aside for Torren. "Vespera told me you were messing with Guild-stamped fittings. I thought she was exaggerating, but this is a disaster."

<br/>

Mark didn't get defensive. He leaned against the edge of his bench, adopting the calm, dry composure he’d used during code reviews for four decades. "What's wrong with it?"

<br/>

Lyra picked up the silver-plated brass disc, holding it close to her eyes and tracing the grooves with a practiced thumb.

<br/>

"Where do I start?" she scoffed, pulling a brass caliper from her tool roll and snapping it open. "First, the depth of this secondary trace is off by at least two-tenths of a millimeter. You filed away the original silver plating past Guild specification! Second, what is this dried gunk wedged between the parallel channels?"

<br/>

"Pine resin and dried parchment," Mark answered smoothly. "It acts as a physical insulator."

<br/>

"It's an un-sanctioned impurity!" Lyra shot back, her cheeks flushing with irritation. "High Imperial doctrine dictates that all rune channels must be open to the air to allow the Aether to breathe! And worst of all..." She turned the disc over, glaring at the small lead solder point where Mark’s copper wire was attached. "...you drilled a hole straight through the outer retention rim and attached a piece of common blacksmith’s wire! That violates four separate manufacturing safety protocols!"

<br/>

She set the disc back down on the table with a loud *clack*, looking at Vespera. "He's not an engineer, Vespera. He's a vandal. If a Chapterhouse Inspector saw this, they'd break his tools and revoke our workshop's license for even looking at it."

<br/>

Mark let out a slow, quiet breath, picking up his magnifying lens and handing it to her.

<br/>

"Look through the glass, Lyra," Mark said softly.

<br/>

Lyra blinked, thrown off by his complete lack of anger. "What?"

<br/>

"Look at the center of the disc under the lens," Mark repeated, pointing to a discarded, burnt disc sitting in his scrap bin. "That's an un-modified Guild-standard disc that blew out on the timber mill blowers last week."

<br/>

Lyra hesitated, then grunted and pressed the magnifying lens to her eye, angling the burnt disc toward the sunlight.

<br/>

"Tell me what you see at the junction of the third and fourth traces," Mark directed.

<br/>

"It's melted," Lyra muttered. "The silver plate fused across the gap. It happens all the time. Aether flux spikes, the metal overheats, and the logic fails. That's why craftsmen buy replacement discs."

<br/>

"Why does it melt?" Mark asked.

<br/>

"Because Aether is volatile," Lyra replied automatically, quoting her workshop masters. "It burns when the caster or the fitting loses focus."

<br/>

"Aether isn't volatile; your trace layout is a bottleneck," Mark corrected gently, taking a piece of charcoal and drawing a clean schematic on the wooden workbench between them. "Look at the physics. Your Guild specification cuts the primary silver trace wide, but narrows the return channel by forty percent to create 'sacred resistance.' That resistance creates a massive thermal buildup right at the junction."

<br/>

He tapped the drawing with the charcoal stick.

<br/>

"The brass casing can't absorb heat fast enough. So the heat stays in the silver plate until the metal hits its melting point, jumps the gap, and locks the circuit in a short. Your 'specification' is literally engineered to destroy itself."

<br/>

Lyra stared at the diagram. Her brow furrowed, her sharp grey eyes moving back and forth between Mark’s drawing and the burnt disc under her lens. She knew the math of the traces—she spent ten hours a day carving them into brass—but no one in the Chapterhouse had ever explained *why* the lines were laid out in those specific ratios. They were just told to follow the master templates handed down from the Academies.

<br/>

"The pine resin parchment stops the voltage from jumping the gap when it gets hot," Mark continued. "And the 'ridiculous' copper wire gives the excess thermal energy a path of least resistance—a drain pipe straight into the ground. That disc isn't a vandalized ruin, Lyra. It's a patch that keeps your work from melting."

<br/>

Lyra opened her mouth to argue, but the logic was suffocatingly clear. She looked at the modified disc sitting on the bench. The copper wire wasn't random clutter; it was positioned at the exact geometric point where thermal accumulation was highest.

<br/>

"It... it really is just a heat trap," she whispered, her prickly posture softening into genuine confusion. "The master templates... they're designed with a bottleneck."

<br/>

"Planned obsolescence," Mark said. "Good for Guild repair fees. Bad for hardware longevity."

<br/>

"We throw away three out of every ten discs we carve during testing because the silver bridges during the initial attunement," Lyra murmured, her eyes wide as she looked at Mark. "If we added a grounding channel during the initial casting phase... we wouldn't lose a single plate."

<br/>

"Exactly," Mark smiled faintly.

<br/>

"But that's not even the worst waste in your pipeline," Mark added, reaching into his tunic pocket.

<br/>

He pulled out one of the dull, slate-grey mana-crystal shards Torren had told him to throw in the slag pit. He set the stone on the workbench in front of her.

<br/>

Lyra scoffed instinctively. "A dead power shard? What does that have to do with anything? Once the light goes out, the stone is drained. My workshop dumps a barrel of those into the river every month."

<br/>

"Watch," Mark said.

<br/>

He picked up his wearable leather wristband—Prototype 1.1—and buckled it around his forearm. He wrapped a thin silver wire around the grey stone, connected it to the copper plate on his wrist, and dropped into his rooted stance.

<br/>

He closed his eyes, initiated his diaphragmatic breathwork, and passed a smooth, low-amperage trickle charge through the wire.

<br/>

Lyra leaned forward, her mouth opening in sheer, unadulterated shock.

<br/>

Right before her eyes, the dull grey gravel began to pulse. A brilliant, needle-thin point of azure blue light flickered at the center of the stone, expanding rapidly through the crystalline facets until the entire shard was glowing with a vibrant, cool blue light.

<br/>

Mark unwrapped the wire and picked up the fully recharged mana-crystal, tossing it lightly in the air before catching it and setting it in Lyra’s numb, open hand.

<br/>

The stone was warm, vibrating with clean, fully charged Aether.

<br/>

"It's not dead, Lyra," Mark said softly. "It was just at zero percent charge. Your Guild is throwing away functional lithium-ion batteries because nobody taught you how to plug them back into the wall."

<br/>

Lyra stared at the glowing blue crystal in her palm, her breathing fast and shallow. She looked from the stone to Mark’s quiet, unbothered face, then at Vespera, who was watching the exchange with a knowing, subtle smile.

<br/>

"By the Ancestors," Lyra whispered, her voice trembling. "You didn't just fix a blower. You... you completely broke the Chapterhouse supply logic."

<br/>

She looked up at Mark, the skepticism in her eyes completely replaced by an intense, burning curiosity—the undeniable spark of a fellow builder recognizing a master craftsman.

<br/>

"How did you figure this out?" Lyra demanded, stepping closer to the bench. "Who taught you this?"

<br/>

"Forty years of troubleshooting bad architecture," Mark replied with a dry grin.

<br/>

Lyra set the glowing crystal down carefully, her mind racing through the possibilities. "If my workshop master saw this... he'd either execute you for heresy or make you the Chief Artificer of the province."

<br/>

"Let's go with neither," Mark said smoothly. "I like my shed, and I like keeping a low profile. Which brings me to a business proposition."

<br/>

Lyra narrowed her eyes, leaning against the bench. "What kind of proposition?"

<br/>

"You work inside the Guild's primary production facility," Mark said, pacing his words carefully. "You have access to their scrap bins—discarded silver wire offcuts, burnt brass plates, and barrels of 'dead' grey crystals that your master thinks are trash."

<br/>

Lyra nodded slowly. "I throw them in the waste chute every evening."

<br/>

"Don't throw them in the chute," Mark said, pointing to the glowing stone. "Bring the 'trash' to my shed. I will teach you how to read circuit schematics, how to calculate thermal resistance, and how to recharge depleted stones. In exchange, you give me first pick of the raw scrap materials before they hit the dump."

<br/>

Lyra looked at the modified disc, the recharged crystal, and the neat English diagrams in Mark’s notebook. She was a fourteen-year-old journeyman who had spent four years being told to carve lines without ever asking why. Mark was offering her the keys to the entire underlying machine.

<br/>

She reached out her hand, smudged with silver polish, and looked Mark dead in the eye.

<br/>

"Deal," Lyra said firmly. "On one condition."

<br/>

Mark raised an eyebrow, taking her hand and shaking it with a solid grip. "What condition?"

<br/>

"You teach me how to make one of those wearable wristbands," she said, nodding toward Prototype 1.1 on his arm. "I want to see what it feels like to cast without a heavy staff."

<br/>

Mark laughed—a genuine, warm sound that echoed off the stone walls of the shed.

<br/>

"Done," Mark said.

---

Late that night, after Vespera and Lyra had slipped back out into the quiet avenues of Oakhaven, Mark sat alone at his oak bench under the cool blue light of his recharged capacitor.

<br/>

He opened his leather ledger, picked up his charcoal stylus, and wrote in clean, encrypted English cursive:

<br/>

> *System Architecture Notes — Mark Vance*
> *Log 07: Supply Chain Integration & Stakeholder Onboarding*
> *Key Events:*
> *1. Peer review conducted with Lyra (Journeyman Engraver, Mages' Chapterhouse Workshop).*
> *2. Demonstrated thermal feedback mechanics and crystal re-charging protocol. Resistance from traditional dogma successfully overcome through empirical proof.*
> *3. Strategic Partnership Established: Secured direct access to Guild scrap streams (silver offcuts, discarded logic plates, discharged mana-crystals) in exchange for technical instruction in circuit design.*
> *System Status:*
> *Resource pipeline fully secured. Material costs for Prototype 2.0 reduced to zero.*
> *Next Development Phase:*
> *Begin design on dual-crystal logic arrays to enable multi-target kinetic subroutines.*

<br/>

Mark closed the ledger, unbuckled his wrist cuff, and set it neatly beside his tools. He lay back on his simple cot in the dark, listening to the soft rain pattering against the granite roof, completely satisfied with his new development pipeline.
