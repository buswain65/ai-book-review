### Chapter 7: The Bottleneck and the Guild

<br/>

Mark woke to a shaft of bright morning sunlight cutting across his face, accompanied by a headache that felt like a rusty nail driven behind his left eye.

<br/>

He sat up slowly, groaning as a dull, lingering numbness tingled down his right forearm. His brain felt heavy, sluggish, and thoroughly fried—the exact cognitive hangover he used to get after spending sixteen straight hours debugging asynchronous threading issues on a red-eye deployment.

<br/>

He looked across the room at the candle on the small table. The wick was still charred black from the night before.

<br/>

*"Output confirmed,"* Mark thought, rubbing his throbbing temples with both hands. *"Process cost: astronomical."*

<br/>

He swung his legs over the edge of the cot and rested his bare feet on the cold floorboards, taking slow, deep breaths to clear the brain fog.

<br/>

The experiment had proven his theory—magic didn't inherently require a staff or a spoken chant—but it had also exposed a glaring system bottleneck. His fourteen-year-old brain possessed the raw agility, but not the working memory bandwidth to calculate energy collection, thermal conversion, targeting, and safety cut-offs simultaneously without burning out his neural pathways.

<br/>

*"It's a compiler issue,"* Mark realized, staring at his palms. *"I tried to manually evaluate every single line of code in real-time. In martial arts, you don't consciously calculate hip rotation, weight distribution, and vector force every time you throw a jab or execute a hip escape. You drill the movement five thousand times until it becomes muscle memory. The subconscious handles the execution."*

<br/>

If he wanted to cast spells silently and staffless without giving himself an aneurysm, he couldn't rely on raw, conscious willpower. He needed to build **cognitive subroutines**—mental muscle memory that his subconscious could execute on command.

---

Downstairs in the common room, the morning rush was in full swing. The air smelled of fried salt pork, baked barley bread, and cheap ale.

<br/>

Garin was already at a corner table, inhaling a mountain of eggs and ham. Vespera sat across from him, sipping herbal tea, her indigo cloak neatly folded over her lap and her ironwood staff leaning against her chair.

<br/>

"Look who finally crawled out of grave," Garin boomed, slapping the table as Mark approached. "You look worse than you did yesterday, kid. What were you doing up there, fighting ghosts?"

<br/>

"Optimizing throughput," Mark muttered, dropping onto the bench. He pulled a fresh bread roll toward himself and took a large bite.

<br/>

Vespera’s sharp eyes narrowed as she looked at him. She set her teacup down with a soft, deliberate *clink*.

<br/>

"There was an erratic fluctuation in the local Aether flux around midnight," Vespera said quietly, her voice dropping so the surrounding tables couldn't hear. "It was faint, but unrefined. Spiky. Like someone trying to strike flint underwater." She leaned across the table. "Mark... what did you do in that room?"

<br/>

Mark chewed his bread calmly, giving nothing away. "I was practicing my breathwork. Told you, I have a fast metabolism."

<br/>

Vespera stared at him, clearly unsatisfied with the answer, but before she could push further, Garin wiped his mouth with the back of his hand and stood up.

<br/>

"Well, whatever you were doing, break's over," the merchant announced. "I've got iron to unload at the smithies, and Vespera's got business at the local Mages' Chapterhouse. Mark, you're coming with me to the Guildhall."

<br/>

Mark raised an eyebrow. "Guildhall?"

<br/>

"You're a long way from home, kid, and you're broke," Garin said pragmatically. "I bought you dinner and a bed for helping with the cart, but today you're on your own copper. The Trades and Adventurers Guild handles short-term labor contracts for travelers—everything from hauling grain to guarding livestock. It's the best place to earn a few coppers if you don't mind sweating."

<br/>

Mark nodded. He needed local currency—not just for food and lodging, but to buy basic supplies. Notebooks, ink, maybe some raw copper wire or mineral samples to test as thermal conductors.

<br/>

"Lead the way," Mark said.

---

The Oakhaven Guildhall was a massive, timber-framed building near the town square, buzzing with activity. Merchants, off-duty guards, armored mercenaries, and local laborers crowded around large wooden notice boards pinned with parchment contracts.

<br/>

Garin led Mark past the crowd to a long oak counter where an overworked clerk with ink-stained fingers was furiously stamping papers.

<br/>

"Harlan!" Garin barked cheerfully. "Got a fresh hire for your labor roster."

<br/>

The clerk, Harlan, didn't look up from his ledger. "Name, origin, skill set."

<br/>

"Mark. Downriver," Mark answered directly. "General labor, physical coordination, mechanics, problem-solving."

<br/>

Harlan finally glanced up, squinting at Mark over a pair of wire-rimmed spectacles. He took in Mark’s lean frame, smooth skin, and youth.

<br/>

"Fourteen? Fifteen?" Harlan sighed, dipping his quill in ink. "You look like you'd snap under a sack of grain, boy. Can you lift?"

<br/>

"I understand leverage," Mark replied smoothly. "Put me where the work is heavy."

<br/>

Harlan grunted, scribbling Mark’s name onto a parchment sheet. "Fair enough. Blacksmith's yard behind the south armory needs coal moved from the storage pit to the furnaces. Two copper bits for four hours of hauling. Don't steal the iron, don't piss off the smith, and try not to break your back."

<br/>

He slapped a small bronze wooden token onto the counter. "Show that to Smith Master Torren."

<br/>

"Appreciate it," Mark said, scooping up the token.

---

An hour later, Mark was standing in the sweltering heat of the south armory forge yard, stripped down to his linen trousers, shovel in hand. A massive pile of black coal sat on one side of the yard; three roaring brick furnaces sat on the other.

<br/>

Around him, two other teenage laborers were already sweating profusely, groaning under the weight of oversized wooden wheelbarrows as they struggled across the rutted dirt.

<br/>

Mark didn't rush. He stepped up to his wheelbarrow, checked the balance, and analyzed the movement.

<br/>

*"Physical labor is just dynamic breathwork,"* Mark thought, taking a deep breath through his nose.

<br/>

He didn't just haul with his arms. He dropped into a shallow Kung Fu stance, engaging his legs, hips, and core. He shoveled the coal with rhythmic, fluid efficiency, keeping his spine straight and his center of gravity low.

<br/>

While his body executed the physical labor on pure muscle memory, he opened up a secondary background thread in his mind.

<br/>

With every breath in, he pulled a tiny, controlled trickle of ambient Aether into his chest. With every breath out, he routed the energy down through his legs and into the dirt beneath his feet, maintaining a constant, subtle ground.

<br/>

*Inhale, pull Aether. Exhale, shovel coal, discharge to ground.*

<br/>

The work was grueling, but to Mark, it was meditative. He wasn't just moving coal to earn two copper coins; he was building the physical and mental infrastructure for his new life. He was training his nervous system to handle energy flow automatically while his body performed complex physical tasks.

<br/>

By the time the four hours were up, the other two laborers were collapsed against the stone wall, gasping for air, their arms trembling with exhaustion.

<br/>

Mark stood beside his empty coal pile, wiping a streak of black dust from his forehead. His arms were sore and his skin was covered in soot, but his breathing was steady, his head was clear, and he felt a deep, satisfying hum of energy thrumming just beneath his skin.

<br/>

Smith Master Torren—a burly dwarf-like man with a braided beard—walked out of the forge, looking from the empty coal pit to Mark.

<br/>

"Well, be damned," Torren grunted, tossing two heavy copper coins to Mark, who caught them smoothly. "You move like a machine, kid. You want work tomorrow?"

<br/>

Mark flipped the coins in the air, catching them with a grin.

<br/>

"Tomorrow," Mark said, "I'm going to need a notebook."
