### Chapter 16: The Chapterhouse Audit

<br/>

Dawn had barely broken over Oakhaven when a heavy, frantic pounding rattled the oak door of Mark’s shed.

<br/>

Mark was already awake, seated at his workbench with Prototype 2.0 unbuckled and set beside his leather ledger. He stood up, unbolted the heavy latch, and pulled the door open.

<br/>

Vespera stood in the damp morning fog, her indigo cloak pulled tight around her shoulders. Her face was deathly pale, her sharp brown eyes wide with a panic Mark had never seen in her before. Beside her stood Lyra, her canvas apron smudged with soot, her knuckles white as she clutched her leather tool roll.

<br/>

"Pack your ledger, Mark," Vespera whispered, her voice trembling slightly. "Pack everything. Now."

<br/>

Mark didn't move recklessly. He stepped back into the shed, gesturing for them to enter. "What happened? What’s the incident?"

<br/>

"At three o'clock this morning, the Chapterhouse's ancient Aether Anchor flared red," Vespera hissed, stepping inside and pulling the heavy door shut behind them. "It hasn't registered an anomaly in five hundred years. By four in the morning, a carrier pigeon arrived from the capital. High Inspector Valerius and a squad of Chapterhouse Enforcers arrived via fast carriage twenty minutes ago."

<br/>

"Red?" Lyra squeaked, her voice cracking. "Vespera, the Anchor is a First Era relic! It only flares red for high-level unsanctioned casting or spatial ruptures!"

<br/>

"It logged an 'Aetheric Shift' originating from Sector 04," Vespera said, her eyes locking onto Mark. "Right here. In the south armory district. Valerius has six sweepers walking the lanes with bronze detection rods, tracking the residual thermal vacuum and static frequency signature."

<br/>

Mark remained entirely calm, adopting the cool, unflappable composure of a principal architect facing an unannounced regulatory audit. He looked at the cracked hundred-pound iron ballast block resting against the back wall, then down at his leather-bound notebook.

<br/>

"When internal audit shows up unannounced," Mark said dryly, "you don't hide the files in the trash. You present them wrapped in a compliant regulatory framework."

<br/>

"Are you insane?" Lyra gasped. "If Valerius finds a fourteen-year-old scrap boy living in a shed filled with modified Guild regulators and recharged mana-crystals, he won't issue a fine! He’ll seize Torren’s forge and lock you in a dark cell for heresy and IP theft!"

<br/>

"Not if I'm not an unregistered scrap boy," Mark replied smoothly. He turned to Vespera. "Vespera, what is the exact legal status of a Chapterhouse apprentice?"

<br/>

Vespera blinked, thrown off by the question. "An apprentice is bound to a licensed master or workshop. They are exempt from individual equipment inspections provided their work is supervised under research protocols..." She paused, her brows knitting together as the bureaucratic logic clicked. "Imperial Statute 402."

<br/>

"The Prodigy Clause," Lyra gasped, her grey eyes going wide. "A licensed journeyman and an accredited Chapterhouse Mage can jointly nominate an unregistered assistant as a 'Sponsored Research Apprentice'!"

<br/>

"Exactly," Mark said, pulling a blank sheet of heavy parchment from his drawer and dipping his quill. "Vespera, you provide the academic accreditation. Lyra, you provide the workshop affiliation. We predated the registration to three weeks ago, citing an internal field study on furnace thermal efficiency. When Valerius walks through that door, he isn't finding a vandal—he's finding a Guild-sanctioned skunkworks project."

---

Ten minutes later, the main gates of the south armory forge yard burst open.

<br/>

Smith Master Torren stood by his central anvil, his iron hammer held tight, as a tall, imposing figure in crisp silver-and-white Guild robes swept into the yard. Flanking him were two heavily armored Chapterhouse Enforcers carrying long, brass-tipped detection rods that hummed with a low, vibrating resonance.

<br/>

High Inspector Valerius had cold, sunken eyes, a razor-thin mustache, and an aura of sharp, dogmatic authority. He held a glowing crystal pendulum in his gloved hand, its needle pointing directly toward the rear storage shed behind the coal pit.

<br/>

"By order of the Provincial Chapterhouse," Valerius announced, his voice ringing over the hum of the furnaces, "this forge is under immediate inspection for unsanctioned Aetheric manipulation. Stand aside, smith."

<br/>

Torren opened his mouth to bark a protest, but Vespera stepped out from the shadow of the storage shed, her indigo cloak fluttering in the morning breeze. Her wooden staff was held high, her posture erect and completely composed.

<br/>

"Inspector Valerius," Vespera called out, her tone smooth and formal. "An unexpected honor. What brings a senior auditor to Master Torren's humble armory?"

<br/>

Valerius stopped, his sharp eyes narrowing as he recognized her. "Mage Vespera. I was not informed you were stationed in Oakhaven. Three hours ago, the town's Aether Anchor registered a severe, zero-impedance energy surge originating from this exact quadrant. My detection rods indicate a localized thermal drop of over twenty degrees occurred here last night."

<br/>

He stepped toward the stone shed, his enforcers trailing behind him. "Someone in this yard is executing unauthorized, high-throughput casting. Search the shed."

<br/>

"There is no need for force, Inspector," Mark’s voice called out calmly.

<br/>

Mark stepped out of the shed, wearing a clean canvas apron smudged with charcoal. In his hands, he carried a polished brass clipboard holding a freshly inked sheet of parchment bearing the official seals of both Vespera and Lyra.

<br/>

Valerius sneered down at the fourteen-year-old boy. "And who is this child?"

<br/>

"Mark Vance," Mark answered directly, stepping up to the auditor without a single trace of flinching. "Sponsored Research Apprentice under Imperial Statute 402, assigned to the Oakhaven Chapterhouse Experimental Division."

<br/>

He handed the parchment to Valerius.

<br/>

Valerius snatched the page, his eyes darting across the text. His brow furrowed as he read the signatures: *Mage Vespera, Academic Supervisor* and *Journeyman Lyra, Engraving Specialist*.

<br/>

"A research project?" Valerius scoffed. "An unregistered boy running experiments in a blacksmith's scrap yard?"

<br/>

"We are conducting a field trial on static thermal dissipation in Guild-standard furnace blowers, sir," Mark replied smoothly, using his most dry, bureaucratic corporate tone. "If you step into the main forge, you can inspect the assembly yourself."

<br/>

Valerius marched into the forge, his detection rod humming softly. He stopped directly in front of Torren's main air-blower. His sharp eyes immediately locked onto the thin copper wire soldered to the bronze regulator housing, running straight down into a crack in the stone floor.

<br/>

"What is this vandalism?" Valerius demanded, pointing a gloved finger at the wire. "You have drilled directly through a Guild-sealed retention casing!"

<br/>

"It's a static-discharge safety cable, Inspector," Mark explained, standing beside the unit with his hands folded behind his back. "Under heavy operational loads, Guild silver traces experience localized thermal expansion, causing short-circuit failures. The copper wire offloads excess thermal voltage directly into the earth foundation. It protects the Guild's valuable hardware from accidental degradation."

<br/>

Valerius stared at the wire, then at Mark. "Demonstrate it."

<br/>

Mark stepped up to the blower, keeping his right arm tucked loosely at his side so his tunic sleeve completely hid the dual-crystal copper cuff beneath. He tapped the copper actuator lever.

<br/>

*Click.*

<br/>

The blower hummed to life instantly, sending a clean, super-heated, perfectly regulated draft of air into the coal hearth. The fire turned white-hot in seconds, yet the bronze casing remained completely cool to the touch.

<br/>

Valerius raised his brass detection rod, holding it an inch from the housing.

<br/>

The rod's needle barely twitched. It registered **zero Aetheric drift, zero thermal bleed, and zero volatile leakage**. The flow was impossibly clean, completely grounded, and running at 100% efficiency.

<br/>

Valerius stared at the rod, his jaw clenching in disbelief. "The flux is... completely stable. There is no static leakage whatsoever."

<br/>

"As reported in our research log, sir," Mark said, gesturing to his paper. "The grounding cable prevents the thermal accumulation that normally melts the silver traces. It's a field compliance patch."

<br/>

Valerius stood in silence for a long, uncomfortable minute. He couldn't cite them for a violation—the research paperwork was signed by an accredited Mage and a licensed Journeyman, and the hardware was operating with higher safety parameters than the Guild's own factory specifications.

<br/>

However, the Inspector's cold eyes dropped to Mark's unblemished face.

<br/>

"A fourteen-year-old who understands thermal dissipation better than the Master Artificers of the provincial Chapterhouse," Valerius said softly, his tone dripping with deep, suspicious scrutiny. He handed the parchment back to Mark.

<br/>

"You will not remain in this coal pit, Apprentice Vance," Valerius declared firmly. "If you possess this level of aptitude, your place is not hauling scrap for a dwarven smith. By Imperial decree, I am transferring your research sponsorship directly to the **Provincial Chapterhouse Academy** in the city center."

<br/>

Vespera and Lyra exchanged a quick, breathless glance.

<br/>

"Inspector," Vespera intervened, "his field research here is ongoing—"

<br/>

"His research will continue under proper Academy supervision, Mage Vespera," Valerius interrupted coldly. "He will report to the Advanced Artificer Track tomorrow morning at eight o'clock. If he is truly a prodigy, he will thrive in the lecture halls. If he is a fraudulent vandal, my instructors will expose him within a week."

<br/>

Valerius turned on his heel, his white robes swirling around his boots as he marched out of the forge yard, his enforcers falling in behind him.

---

Late that evening, the stone storage shed was quiet once more.

<br/>

Mark sat at his oak bench, packing his tools into a simple canvas satchel: his needle-files, his magnifying lens, his recharged mana-crystals, and Prototype 2.0 safely wrapped in cloth.

<br/>

Smith Master Torren leaned against the doorframe, holding two silver coins in his rough hand.

<br/>

"I suppose this is goodbye to my best scrap boy," Torren grunted, tossing the silver coins to Mark, who caught them smoothly. "The Academy's full of pompous fools in silk robes, kid. Don't let 'em break your brain."

<br/>

"They won't," Mark smiled faintly, pocketing the coins. "I've dealt with plenty of executives before, Torren. They all wear the same suit."

<br/>

After Torren left, Mark opened his leather-bound ledger under the light of his desktop capacitor and wrote in clean, encrypted English cursive:

<br/>

> *System Architecture Notes — Mark Vance*
> 
> <br/>
> 
> *Log 09: Audit Survived & Promotion Executed*
> 
> <br/>
>
> *Stakeholder Action:*
> 
> <br/>
> 
> Successfully deployed regulatory compliance workaround (Statute 402) during unannounced Imperial audit by High Inspector Valerius. Hardware optimizations reframed as 'static safety patches' to satisfy Guild safety standards.
> 
> <br/>
>
> *Status:*
> 
> <br/>
>
> Transferred to Provincial Chapterhouse Academy (Advanced Artificer Track). Field laboratory relocated to institutional grounds.
>
> <br/>
> 
> *Next Phase:*
> 
> <br/>
> 
> Infiltrate Academy archives, analyze First Era rune templates, and identify why Prototype 2.0's zero-impedance execution triggered the red alert on the ancient Aether Anchor.

<br/>

Mark closed the ledger, blew out the blue light of his capacitor, and tucked the notebook into his satchel.

<br/>

He stood up, slung the bag over his shoulder, and walked out into the cool night air. Tomorrow morning, he was going to magic school—and he couldn't wait to see what kind of legacy code they were teaching in the classroom.
