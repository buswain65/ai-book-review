### Chapter 16: The Chapterhouse Audit

<br/>

Dawn had barely broken over Oakhaven when a heavy, frantic pounding rattled the oak door of Mark's shed.

<br/>

Mark was already awake, seated at his workbench with Prototype 2.0 unbuckled and set beside his leather ledger. He hadn't slept much. Some part of his brain had spent the whole night refreshing an inbox that didn't exist, waiting for a reply to a message he had never meant to send.

<br/>

He stood up, unbolted the heavy latch, and pulled the door open.

<br/>

Vespera stood in the damp morning fog, her indigo cloak pulled tight around her shoulders. Her face was deathly pale, her sharp brown eyes wide with a panic Mark had never seen in her before. Beside her stood Lyra, her canvas apron smudged with soot, her knuckles white as she clutched her leather tool roll.

<br/>

"Pack your ledger, Mark," Vespera whispered, her voice trembling slightly. "Pack everything. Now."

<br/>

Mark stepped back into the shed, gesturing for them to enter. "What happened? What's the incident?"

<br/>

"Last night, just after ten o'clock, the Chapterhouse's ancient Aether Anchor flared red," Vespera hissed, stepping inside and pulling the heavy door shut behind them. "It hasn't registered an anomaly in five hundred years. The night wardens sent a pigeon to the capital within the hour. And the Regional Auditor, High Inspector Valerius, just happened to be on circuit at the Millbrook waystation, two hours down the road."

<br/>

"Of course he did," Mark said. "Auditors are always in the area. It's one of the fundamental laws of the universe."

<br/>

"He and a squad of Chapterhouse Enforcers arrived by fast carriage twenty minutes ago."

<br/>

"Red?" Lyra squeaked, her voice cracking. "Vespera, the Anchor is a First Era relic! The old texts say it only flares red for high-level unsanctioned casting or spatial ruptures!"

<br/>

"It logged an 'Aetheric Shift' originating from Sector 04," Vespera said, her eyes locking onto Mark. "Right here. In the south armory district. Valerius has sweepers walking the lanes with bronze detection rods, tracking the residual thermal vacuum and static frequency signature."

<br/>

Mark took a slow look around the shed. The two halves of the late Gerald lay against the back wall. A faint watermark of melted frost still stained the workbench. Four recharged crystals glowed cheerfully in a row beside his tools like tiny, incriminating nightlights. And half a wheel of cheese sat on the bench, looking as innocent as cheese could look.

<br/>

"When internal audit shows up unannounced," Mark said dryly, "you don't hide the files in the trash. You present them wrapped in a compliant regulatory framework."

<br/>

"Are you insane?" Lyra gasped. "If Valerius finds a fourteen-year-old scrap boy living in a shed filled with modified Guild regulators and recharged mana-crystals, he won't issue a fine! He'll seize Torren's forge and lock you in a dark cell for heresy and IP theft!"

<br/>

"Not if I'm not an unregistered scrap boy," Mark replied. He turned to Vespera. "Vespera, what is the exact legal status of a Chapterhouse apprentice?"

<br/>

Vespera blinked, thrown off by the question. "An apprentice is bound to a licensed master or workshop. They are exempt from individual equipment inspections provided their work is supervised under research protocols..." She paused, her brows knitting together as the bureaucratic logic clicked. "Imperial Statute 402."

<br/>

"The Prodigy Clause," Lyra gasped, her grey eyes going wide. "A licensed journeyman and an accredited Chapterhouse Mage can jointly nominate an unregistered assistant as a 'Sponsored Research Apprentice'!"

<br/>

"Exactly," Mark said, pulling a blank sheet of heavy parchment from his drawer and handing Vespera a quill. "Vespera, you provide the academic accreditation. Lyra, you provide the workshop affiliation. We backdate the registration to three weeks ago, citing an internal field study on furnace thermal efficiency. When Valerius walks through that door, he isn't finding a vandal—he's finding a Guild-sanctioned skunkworks project."

<br/>

Vespera stared at the parchment. "That is forgery."

<br/>

"It's *retroactive documentation*," Mark corrected. "Completely standard. Half the compliance paperwork I ever signed was written after the fact."

<br/>

"That doesn't make it better!"

<br/>

"It makes it *normal*," Mark said. "Which, in an audit, is better."

<br/>

Vespera looked at him for a long, agonized second. Then she took the quill.

<br/>

"Next," Mark said, already moving. "Lyra—crystals."

<br/>

Lyra looked around wildly. "Where am I supposed to hide four glowing crystals in a room with *nothing in it*?"

<br/>

Mark looked at the cheese.

<br/>

Lyra followed his gaze. "No."

<br/>

"It's dense, it's opaque, and nobody has ever in history searched a cheese."

<br/>

"*No.*"

<br/>

Ninety seconds later, four recharged mana-crystals had been pressed deep into the half-wheel of cheese, and Lyra was smoothing over the rind with the back of a graver, muttering under her breath about the indignity of it all.

<br/>

"Last thing," Mark said, rolling a thin brass backing plate across the bench toward her. "Can you bend this into a board with a clip on top?"

<br/>

"Why?"

<br/>

"Because," Mark said, "in forty years, I have never once seen anyone question a person holding a clipboard."

---

Ten minutes later, the main gates of the south armory forge yard burst open.

<br/>

Smith Master Torren stood by his central anvil, gripping Hammer, as a tall, imposing figure in crisp silver-and-white Guild robes swept into the yard. Flanking him were two heavily armored Chapterhouse Enforcers carrying long, brass-tipped detection rods that hummed with a low, vibrating resonance.

<br/>

High Inspector Valerius had cold, sunken eyes, a razor-thin mustache, and an aura of sharp, dogmatic authority. He held a glowing crystal pendulum in his gloved hand, its needle pointing directly toward the rear storage shed behind the coal pit.

<br/>

"By order of the Provincial Chapterhouse," Valerius announced, his voice ringing over the hum of the furnaces, "this forge is under immediate inspection for unsanctioned Aetheric manipulation. Stand aside, smith."

<br/>

Torren opened his mouth to bark a protest, but Vespera stepped out from the shadow of the storage shed, her indigo cloak fluttering in the morning breeze. Her wooden staff was held high, her posture erect and completely composed. Only Mark could see that there was still a smudge of quill ink on her thumb.

<br/>

"Inspector Valerius," Vespera called out, her tone smooth and formal. "An unexpected honor. What brings a senior auditor to Master Torren's humble armory?"

<br/>

Valerius stopped, his eyes narrowing as he recognized her. "Mage Vespera. I was not informed you had returned to Oakhaven from your travels. Last night, the town's Aether Anchor registered a severe, zero-impedance energy surge originating from this exact quadrant. My detection rods indicate a localized thermal drop of over twenty degrees occurred here."

<br/>

He stepped toward the stone shed, his enforcers trailing behind him. "Someone in this yard is executing unauthorized, high-throughput casting. Search the shed."

<br/>

"There is no need for force, Inspector," Mark's voice called out calmly.

<br/>

Mark stepped out of the shed, wearing a clean canvas apron smudged with charcoal. In his hands, he carried a brass clipboard, still faintly warm from Lyra's hammer, holding a freshly inked sheet of parchment bearing the official seals of both Vespera and Lyra.

<br/>

Valerius sneered down at the fourteen-year-old boy. "And who is this child?"

<br/>

"Mark Vance," Mark answered directly, stepping up to the auditor without a trace of flinching. "Sponsored Research Apprentice under Imperial Statute 402, assigned to the Oakhaven Chapterhouse Experimental Division."

<br/>

He handed over the clipboard.

<br/>

Valerius took it. His eyes went to the clipboard, and for a fraction of a second, Mark saw it happen: the subtle, instinctive deference that every bureaucrat in every universe extends to an official-looking object. Valerius's spine straightened a quarter-inch. His sneer softened by one degree.

<br/>

*"Universal,"* Mark thought. *"Absolutely universal."*

<br/>

Valerius read the text. His brow furrowed as he took in the signatures: *Mage Vespera, Academic Supervisor* and *Journeyman Lyra, Engraving Specialist*.

<br/>

"A research project?" Valerius scoffed. "An unregistered boy running experiments in a blacksmith's scrap yard?"

<br/>

"A *registered* boy, sir," Mark said politely. "Per the attached."

<br/>

"Search the shed anyway," Valerius snapped at his enforcers.

<br/>

The two armored men ducked through the low doorway. Mark and Lyra exchanged a look.

<br/>

From inside came the sounds of a cursory search: a tool roll unrolled, a drawer opened, a ledger flipped through and discarded as gibberish. Then one of the enforcers said, "Sir, there's a broken iron block back here."

<br/>

"Thermal stress testing," Mark called through the door. "Ballast fatigue study. We're assessing forge-adjacent iron for cold-fracture risk. It failed. That's the finding."

<br/>

Torren made a small, wounded sound. "Was that *Gerald*?"

<br/>

"He died doing important research," Mark said quietly. "I'm sorry, Torren."

<br/>

Torren removed his cap and held it over his heart.

<br/>

Inside the shed, the second enforcer had moved toward the corner of the doorframe to inspect it. There was a pause. Then a much longer pause.

<br/>

"Sir," the enforcer said, in a strained voice. "There's a spider."

<br/>

"So?" Valerius said.

<br/>

"A *big* one, sir. She's looking at me."

<br/>

"Then look somewhere else and finish your search!"

<br/>

"She's looking at me *very specifically*, sir."

<br/>

There was a scuffle of boots, and both enforcers emerged from the shed considerably faster than they had entered it. One of them was brushing frantically at the shoulder of his armor.

<br/>

"Nothing of note inside, sir," the first enforcer reported. "Tools. A cot. Broken iron. The spider. And some cheese."

<br/>

Valerius did not even glance at the cheese. Nobody ever does.

<br/>

"Then the source is elsewhere," Valerius said, watching his pendulum swing in a slow arc between the shed and the main forge. He frowned. "The resonance is diffuse."

<br/>

"That would be the grounding seam, sir," Mark offered. "Master Torren's furnace blowers all discharge into the same stone seam beneath the yard. It runs directly under the shed. If you step into the main forge, you can inspect the assembly yourself."

<br/>

Valerius marched into the forge, his detection rod humming softly. Torren followed closely behind him, looking extremely anxious.

<br/>

Valerius stopped directly in front of Torren's main air-blower. His eyes immediately locked onto the thin copper wire soldered to the bronze regulator housing, running straight down into a crack in the stone floor.

<br/>

"What is this vandalism?" Valerius demanded, pointing a gloved finger at the wire. "You have tampered directly with a Guild-sealed retention casing!"

<br/>

"Careful with her," Torren said.

<br/>

Valerius turned. "With *whom*?"

<br/>

"Nobody," Mark said quickly, stepping between them. "Master Torren is very attached to his equipment."

<br/>

"It's a static dish—" Torren began proudly.

<br/>

"It's a *static-discharge safety cable*, Inspector," Mark said, speaking over him at slightly higher volume. "Under heavy operational loads, Guild silver traces experience localized thermal expansion, causing short-circuit failures. The copper wire offloads excess thermal voltage directly into the earth foundation. It protects the Guild's valuable hardware from accidental degradation."

<br/>

"Noodle," Torren finished, quietly but with deep satisfaction.

<br/>

Valerius looked at Torren. Torren looked back with the serene innocence of a man who had no idea what he'd just said and was proud of it anyway.

<br/>

Valerius chose, wisely, to move on. He stared at the wire, then at Mark. "Demonstrate it."

<br/>

Mark stepped up to the blower, keeping his right arm tucked loosely at his side so his tunic sleeve completely hid the dual-crystal copper cuff beneath. He tapped the copper actuator lever.

<br/>

*Click.*

<br/>

Bessie hummed to life instantly, sending a clean, super-heated, perfectly regulated draft of air into the coal hearth. The fire turned white-hot in seconds, yet the bronze casing remained completely cool to the touch.

<br/>

Valerius raised his brass detection rod, holding it an inch from the housing.

<br/>

The rod's needle barely twitched. It registered **zero Aetheric drift, zero thermal bleed, and zero volatile leakage**. The flow was impossibly clean, completely grounded, and running at full efficiency.

<br/>

Valerius stared at the rod, his jaw clenching in disbelief. "Zero impedance," he said slowly. "This is the signature."

<br/>

"Last night we ran all four modified blowers in parallel for the first time," Mark said, which was technically true in the sense that two crystals had been in parallel and four blowers had existed. "A full-load stress test. It's in our research log, sir."

<br/>

He gestured to his clipboard, which said nothing of the sort. Valerius did not check. Nobody ever checks the clipboard.

<br/>

"The grounding cable prevents the thermal accumulation that normally melts the silver traces," Mark continued. "It's a field compliance patch."

<br/>

Valerius stood in silence for a long, uncomfortable minute. He couldn't cite them for a violation—the research paperwork was signed by an accredited Mage and a licensed Journeyman, and the hardware was operating with higher safety parameters than the Guild's own factory specifications. The Anchor's alert had a source, and the source was compliant.

<br/>

However, the Inspector's cold eyes dropped to Mark's young face.

<br/>

"A fourteen-year-old who understands thermal dissipation better than the Master Artificers of the provincial Chapterhouse," Valerius said softly, his tone dripping with deep, suspicious scrutiny. He handed the clipboard back to Mark.

<br/>

"You will not remain in this coal pit, Apprentice Vance," Valerius declared. "If you possess this level of aptitude, your place is not hauling scrap for a dwarf-blooded smith."

<br/>

"*Quarter*-dwarf," Torren said stiffly. "On my mother's side."

<br/>

Valerius ignored him. "By Imperial decree, I am transferring your research sponsorship directly to the **Provincial Chapterhouse Academy** in the city center."

<br/>

Vespera and Lyra exchanged a quick, breathless glance.

<br/>

"Inspector," Vespera intervened, "his field research here is ongoing—"

<br/>

"His research will continue under proper Academy supervision, Mage Vespera," Valerius interrupted coldly. "He will report to the Advanced Artificer Track tomorrow morning at eight o'clock. If he is truly a prodigy, he will thrive in the lecture halls. If he is a fraudulent vandal, my instructors will expose him within a week."

<br/>

Valerius turned on his heel, his white robes swirling around his boots as he marched out of the forge yard, his enforcers falling in behind him. The second enforcer glanced back over his shoulder toward the shed doorframe twice before they reached the gate.

<br/>

The moment the gate closed, Lyra slid down the forge wall until she was sitting on the floor.

<br/>

"We hid," she said faintly, "four contraband crystals. In a cheese."

<br/>

"And it worked," Mark said.

<br/>

"I will *never* be able to eat cheese again."

<br/>

"More for the power budget," Mark said.

<br/>

Torren, meanwhile, was standing beside the two halves of Gerald, which the enforcers had dragged out into the yard. He was patting the larger half gently.

<br/>

"Twelve years," he murmured. "Twelve years, holding down the back of my wagon. Never complained once."

<br/>

"He'd have wanted it this way," Mark said. "Contributing to science."

<br/>

Torren sniffed, wiped his eye with a soot-stained knuckle, and nodded. "Aye. Aye, he would."

---

Late that evening, the stone storage shed was quiet once more.

<br/>

Mark sat at his oak bench, packing his tools into a simple canvas satchel: his needle-files, his magnifying lens, the recharged mana-crystals (extracted from the cheese and wiped thoroughly clean), and Prototype 2.0 safely wrapped in cloth.

<br/>

Smith Master Torren leaned against the doorframe, holding two silver coins in his rough hand. He was careful not to lean against the *top* of the doorframe.

<br/>

"I suppose this is goodbye to my best scrap boy," Torren grunted, tossing the silver coins to Mark, who caught them neatly. "The Academy's full of pompous fools in silk robes, kid. Don't let 'em break your brain."

<br/>

"They won't," Mark smiled faintly, pocketing the coins. "I've dealt with plenty of executives before, Torren. They all wear the same suit."

<br/>

"And don't you worry about Bessie and the girls," Torren added. "I'll keep 'em running. Static dishcharge safety noodles and all."

<br/>

"I know you will."

<br/>

Torren nodded, then hesitated, looking up at the corner of the doorframe. "What about her?"

<br/>

Mark looked up at Legacy. Legacy looked down at Mark. She had rebuilt her web overnight, larger than before, as if to make a point.

<br/>

He had briefly considered bringing her. He suspected she had briefly considered letting him leave.

<br/>

"She stays," Mark said. "She was here before me. She'll be here after. That's how legacy systems work."

<br/>

"I'll look after her," Torren said solemnly.

<br/>

"Don't name her," Mark warned. "She already has a name."

<br/>

"Wouldn't dream of it," Torren said, and left—though Mark distinctly heard him say "Goodnight, Margaret" to someone on his way across the yard, and he was fairly certain it wasn't one of the blowers.

<br/>

After Torren left, Mark opened his leather-bound ledger under the light of his desktop capacitor and wrote in clean, encrypted English cursive:

<br/>

> *System Architecture Notes — Mark Vance*
> *Log 09: Audit Survived & Promotion Executed*
> *Stakeholder Action:*
> *Successfully deployed regulatory compliance workaround (Statute 402) during unannounced Imperial audit by High Inspector Valerius. Hardware optimizations reframed as 'static safety patches' to satisfy Guild safety standards. Anchor alert attributed to parallel blower stress test.*
> *Key Learnings:*
> *1. Nobody questions a clipboard.*
> *2. Nobody searches cheese.*
> *3. Enforcers are afraid of Legacy. Legacy should be considered a security asset.*
> *Casualties:*
> *Gerald (previously reported). Torren is grieving but stable.*
> *Status:*
> *Transferred to Provincial Chapterhouse Academy (Advanced Artificer Track). Field laboratory relocated to institutional grounds.*
> *Next Phase:*
> *Infiltrate Academy archives, analyze First Era rune templates, and identify why Prototype 2.0's zero-impedance execution triggered the red alert on the ancient Aether Anchor.*

<br/>

Mark closed the ledger, draped a cloth over the blue light of his capacitor, and tucked the notebook into his satchel.

<br/>

He stood up and slung the bag over his shoulder. At the door, he paused and looked up.

<br/>

"Thanks for the help today," he told Legacy.

<br/>

She did not respond. But she did not turn away either, which, from Legacy, was practically a hug.

<br/>

Mark walked out into the cool night air. Tomorrow morning, he was going to magic school—and he couldn't wait to see what kind of legacy code they were teaching in the classroom.
