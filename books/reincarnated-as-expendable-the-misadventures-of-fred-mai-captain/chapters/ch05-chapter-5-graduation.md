# Chapter 5: Graduation

By the end of the fifth week, Fred's latrines were famous.

It started small. A corporal from the next camp came by to borrow a ladder, used the latrine block while he was there, and came out looking like a man who had seen a vision. He told his sergeant. The sergeant told a lieutenant. By the third week, officers were riding in from camps two valleys away, their horses lathered and their faces set, just to use it. One elderly colonel asked, quite seriously, whether it would be possible to be buried nearby. Gruk told him to fill out a form.

Fred kept cleaning. He didn't know how not to.

The rest of Hut 9 settled into the weeks the way people settle into cold water, one gasp at a time. Recruit Thirty Minutes broke every record the camp had, then broke them again. Gruk stopped shouting THE FRONT'S GOING TO LOVE YOU and started saying it quietly, which was worse. Recruit Spreadsheet was put in charge of counting buckets, found eleven hundred that Bilge had sold to the camp twice, and was told to stop counting buckets. The Heroic Posing class added a second rock. Mook filled his first ledger and started a second, writing very small, so as not to waste paper.

Every Friday, with a cheerful chime, a small glowing window opened in front of Fred's eyes:

LATRINE SKILL INCREASED (PENDING APPROVAL)

And every Friday, his level stayed at 1.

The rumors about Gruk's name kept coming too. In the second week it was a cursed sword. In the fourth week it was a bet with a dragon. In the fifth week, a recruit from Hut 3 swore on his mother that Gruk had caught it from a love letter, and nobody could prove he hadn't.

Then it was the sixth week, and it was over.

On graduation morning, the parade ground had been transformed. Overnight, somebody had hung bunting from every hut in the gold and white of the Holy Kingdom, so the camp looked like a cheap cake. A wooden stage stood at one end of the mud, draped in gold cloth that didn't quite reach the ground; you could see the barrels holding it up. On either side rose bleachers full of minor nobles in feathered hats, fanning themselves and trying not to look at the mud. Behind the stage stood a huge painted backdrop of a cliff at sunset, with a single rock at its edge and a sky full of dramatic clouds. It was the most beautiful thing Fred had seen in Fekfosio. It was also, he noticed, held up by ropes tied to the latrine block.

The recruits had been up since four, scrubbing their swords. By seven, the swords were only slightly rusty. Fred had scrubbed his mop as well, out of habit, though nobody had told him to.

At eight, the officials from the Bureau of Destiny arrived. There were six of them, in identical gray robes, carrying identical clipboards, and they moved through the camp the way frost moves across a window, quietly, leaving everything colder. One of them carried a long, flat board under his arm, folded in half. Fred had seen one like it before. He knew, even folded, what it said.

The trumpets started at nine.

The Chosen One arrived the way weather arrives in stories: all at once, and from above. First came six trumpeters on white horses. Then came a carriage, gold from wheel to roof, pulled by four more white horses in plumes. Then came the petals, pink and white, falling from nowhere in particular and settling on the mud, where they sank. The nobles in the bleachers rose and applauded. A footman unrolled a gold carpet from the carriage step all the way to the stage, so that the Chosen One would not have to touch Fekfosio at all.

Kaito Reklamo stepped down.

He looked exactly as he had in the processing hall: impossibly handsome, impossibly neat, his black hair swept in a way that did not move. Now he wore white armor trimmed with gold, and the holy sword rode on his back in a jeweled scabbard. Fred noticed, because he noticed things, that the scabbard also bore a small crest that wasn't the Holy Kingdom's: a gold sun, with the words BLESSED BY RADIANCE TONICS underneath.

Behind Kaito came his companions: a mage in a midnight-blue robe, an elf archer with a bow taller than she was, a rogue with soft cat ears and a knife at each hip, and a priestess in white with her hands folded. They were all very beautiful, and they were all smiling the same smile. Behind each of them, exactly one step back, walked a Bureau official in gray, holding a clipboard and now and then murmuring something into a companion's ear.

Kaito climbed onto the stage, and an official handed him a stack of cards. Fred was standing near the front, and he saw Kaito's lips move as he read the first card to himself.

"Recruits of the Holy Kingdom!" said Kaito, in a strong, clear voice that didn't quite fit the words. "Six weeks ago, you were nobody!"

There was a pause while he turned to the next card.

"Today, you are still... nobody. But you are nobody in the service of DESTINY!" Card. "Destiny chose me. Destiny can choose you too!"

A herald in a tabard stepped forward at once and said, very fast and in one breath, "Destiny-is-not-guaranteed-past-heroes-are-no-guarantee-of-future-heroes-results-may-vary-harems-subject-to-availability-the-Bureau-accepts-no-liability-for-death-dismemberment-or-disappointment," and stepped back.

At the side of the stage, the official with the folded board opened it and raised it above his head. APPLAUSE, it said.

The nobles applauded. The recruits applauded. The Drill Assistant applauded and wiped her eyes. Fred clapped politely, because everyone else was.

Gangrene Gruk, standing at the end of the front row with his arms folded, did not. The official raised the sign a little higher and looked straight at him. Gruk looked back. After a while the official lowered the sign, and Gruk spat neatly into the mud and went on not clapping.

It was, Fred thought, the bravest thing he had seen in Fekfosio.

Kaito handed out the diplomas personally, which meant that each one was also a portrait. One by one, the recruits climbed onto the stage, took a rolled certificate from the Chosen One's hand, and turned to face a painter at an easel in the mud. Then they did the pose: one foot up on the rock, eyes on the middle distance, imagining the wind. The painter made a few quick strokes and said, "Next," and the recruit climbed down with a diploma and a sore calf.

Thirty Minutes got a dispatch bag and a pair of very light boots. Spreadsheet got an abacus. Mook got a new ledger with a black cloth cover and CASUALTIES stamped on the front in gold. He held it as if it were a puppy.

Fred was last.

He climbed the steps with his rusty sword at his hip and his mop over his shoulder, because nobody had told him to leave it behind. A Bureau official found his name on a clipboard, frowned at the hyphen, and handed Kaito the last kit. It was one rusty sword, slightly rustier than the one Fred already had, and one mop.

Kaito held it out. Fred took it. For a moment they stood there, the Chosen One and the Latrine Paladin, each holding one end of a mop, while the painter said, "Pose, please," and began to mix a new color.

Fred did the pose. He put his foot on the rock, gazed into the middle distance and imagined the wind. Beside him, Kaito did it too, perfectly, without seeming to think, the way a man breathes.

While the painter squinted at the sky, Kaito spoke. He didn't turn his head, and he barely moved his lips, and his voice was nothing like the voice he had used for the speech. It was quiet and flat and very tired.

"Do you ever get a day off?" he said.

Fred thought about it. He thought about eleven years of night shifts, and six weeks of latrines, and Dolores saying someday.

"I've never asked," he said.

Something moved in Kaito's face. Before Fred could tell what it was, a Bureau official was at Kaito's elbow, murmuring. The painter said, "Lovely, thank you," Kaito's smile came back, bright as the trumpets, and he turned away to wave at the nobles.

Fred climbed down with his diploma, his two rusty swords and his mop. At the bottom, a small glowing window opened with a chime:

ITEM ACQUIRED: MOP (BLESSED?)

He looked at the question mark for a while. Nobody, it seemed, was sure.

Gruk's speech came last, after the nobles had begun drifting back to their carriages and the Bureau had folded up its sign.

He climbed onto the stage, which brought him up to roughly the height of an average man, and looked out over Batch #4712 in the mud. For a while he didn't say anything at all.

"YOU WERE THE WORST BATCH I'VE EVER HAD," he said at last.

They waited.

"YOU MARCH LIKE CHICKENS. YOU FIGHT LIKE FURNITURE. HALF OF YOU WILL BE DEAD BY SPRING." He paused. "THE OTHER HALF, I'M PROUD OF. DON'T ASK ME WHICH. DISMISSED."

He climbed down. It was the shortest graduation speech in the history of the camp, and three people in the front row cried.

On his way past, Gruk stopped in front of Fred. He looked up at him the way he had on the first morning, with his small yellow eyes, and for once he didn't shout.

"Six weeks," he said. "Clean latrines. Six weeks. In thirty years, nobody's ever..."

He stopped. Something closed in his face, like a door.

"DEMONS STILL AIN'T FLYING, HYPHEN," he said, and walked off.

That evening, the last rumor of boot camp went round Hut 9. Gruk hadn't caught gangrene from a sword or a dragon or a love letter at all. He'd caught it from a library book. It was overdue, he had never returned it, and that was why.

Nobody believed it. Everybody repeated it.

The postings went up the next morning, nailed to a board outside the quartermaster's store. A crowd gathered at once, pushing and craning. Fred waited at the back, because there was always a back, and somebody had to be in it.

When the crowd thinned, he went up with Mook. The list was long and printed very small, and Mook ran his finger down it from long practice.

THIRTY MINUTES (RECRUIT): DISPATCH. THE FRONT.

SPREADSHEET (RECRUIT): CENTRAL SUPPLY. THE CAPITAL.

MOOREOVER, MOOK: CASUALTY OFFICE. FIELD ARMY.

"They've spelled it with two O's," said Mook. "Moreover, they've done it in ink." He sighed. "Well. It's still me."

Fred found his own name a little further down.

MAI-CAPTAIN, FRED (PRIVATE, SEE NAME): SANITATION SUPPORT. HERO'S PARTY (REKLAMO).

Fred read it three times. Then he read it a fourth time, in case it changed.

"The hero's party," said Mook slowly. "Fred. That's Kaito Reklamo's party. You'll be with the Chosen One."

"I'll be cleaning up after the Chosen One," said Fred.

"Still," said Mook. He looked at the list, and at Fred, and at the ledger under his arm with CASUALTIES on it in gold. Fred watched him do a sum in his head and not like the answer. "Still. It's something."

Behind them, Thirty Minutes was already packing his dispatch bag and singing to himself. Spreadsheet was reading the list for the fourth time, looking for an error he could report. Somewhere across the camp, Gruk was shouting at a new batch, just arrived and still wet.

Then, very close, came a cheerful chime, and a small glowing window opened in front of Fred's eyes.

CONGRATULATIONS! YOU HAVE JOINED A HERO'S PARTY.

ROLE: BACKGROUND.
