# Chapter 4: Requirements

> **THERE ARE NO STUPID QUESTIONS** **(PLEASE SUBMIT QUESTIONS IN WRITING, IN TRIPLICATE)**
>
> *— Banner over the door of Quest Intake*

On the morning of his fourth day, Walter stood outside the door of Quest Intake, holding a scroll about a thing in a well, and waited for someone to let him in.

His badge still couldn't open doors. Four managers still had to approve it. One of them, according to Mortimer, was on a pilgrimage.

After about ten minutes the door opened a crack, and a thin, anxious face peered out. It belonged to a gangly boy of about seventeen, with ink on his fingers, ink on his collar and, somehow, a small smudge of ink on the tip of his nose.

"Oh!" said the boy. "You're the new one. The hundred-percent one." He opened the door the rest of the way and stood carefully aside, so it couldn't possibly count as tailgating. "I'm Tobble. Senior Quest Scribe."

"Senior," said Walter. "How long have you been here?"

"Six weeks." Tobble swallowed. "I'm the longest-serving."

The Quest Intake scriptorium was a tall, narrow room with slanting morning light and two long rows of slanted desks. At every desk sat a scribe, and every scribe was very young. Walter guessed the average age at about sixteen. Quills scratched. Somebody was quietly crying into a blotter. On the far wall hung a wooden board with movable painted numbers, like the safety boards in factories that count the days since the last accident. It read:

**DAYS SINCE LAST PROMOTION: 2**

"Dennet," said Tobble, following his eyes, in the hushed voice of someone mentioning the recently deceased. "He read a memo out loud in a meeting. Fluently. Now he's Associate Director of Strategic Parchment." He lowered his voice further. "He's fourteen. He cries in the stairwell."

Tobble showed Walter to an empty desk and produced a printed form from a tall stack. "This is the Quest Template," he said, and then he did something strange. He bent over the form, put one finger on the first word, and began, very slowly, to sound it out.

"Ti… tle."

Walter looked at him. Ten minutes ago, through the crack in the door, he had seen this same boy read a dense, closely written memo pinned upside down on a noticeboard — at a glance, in about four seconds.

"Cli… ent," said Tobble, finger moving. "Des… crip…"

"You can read," said Walter quietly.

Tobble froze. His finger stayed on the page. His ears went red.

"Please don't tell anyone," he whispered. "I just want to do the job. I *like* the job. I don't want to be a Director. I don't even know what Directors do. Nobody does. They just go into the Directors' wing, and you never see them again except at the Showcase, and they look *so tired*."

Walter looked at the boy for a long moment. He saw a teenager with ink on his nose, pretending to be less than he was, so the system wouldn't notice him and use him up.

It was his own plan. He had come up with it four days ago, face-down in the mud, and felt clever. Seeing it on someone else, it just looked sad.

"I won't tell anyone," Walter said.

Tobble let out a breath he seemed to have been holding for six weeks. Then he walked Walter through the form, still pretending to read it, a little more quickly now.

The Quest Template was a single page with six boxes:

> **TITLE:** **CLIENT:** **DESCRIPTION:** **ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA:** **VALOR POINTS:** *TBD* **DEADLINE:** *ASAP*

The last two came pre-printed. Every quest in the guild was, apparently, worth an unknown amount and due immediately.

Before Walter could pick up a quill, a bell rang in the corridor.

The door banged open — by itself, for the man coming through had a gold lanyard — and in strode a runner in gray robes, with a handbell in one hand and a bulging leather satchel over his shoulder. Embroidered on the satchel were the words **TEMPLATE GOVERNANCE COUNCIL**.

"Hear ye!" cried the runner, ringing the bell. "By order of the Council, Quest Template version fourteen point two is hereby *retired*, effective immediately! Version fourteen point three is now *mandatory*!"

He went down the rows slapping a fresh stack of forms onto every desk. A long, low, heartfelt groan rose from the scriptorium. Over the door, a clerk on a ladder took down a small painted sign that said **CURRENT TEMPLATE: v14.2** and hung up one that said **CURRENT TEMPLATE: v14.3**. The ladder, Walter noticed, never left. It just lived there.

Walter compared the two forms. Version 14.3 was identical to version 14.2 in every way, except that the pre-printed deadline now said **ASAP (URGENT)**, and a new mandatory box had appeared at the bottom of the page:

> **DRAGON IMPACT ASSESSMENT:**

"Every quest?" Walter said. "Even ones without dragons?"

"*Especially* ones without dragons," said Tobble darkly. "You have to explain why not."

All around the room, scribes were pulling out quests they'd finished that morning, laying them next to fresh copies of v14.3, and starting to copy them out again, word for word. The girl by the window was on what looked like her third copy of the same quest. She was crying a little. Walter realized now who'd been crying into the blotter, and why.

"Anything on the old template gets rejected," said Tobble. "Template Version Mismatch. Straight back to you."

"How often does it change?"

"About four times a day. The Council meets every hour."

"What do they do the rest of the time?"

Tobble thought about it. "Prepare for the meeting."

Above Walter's fresh stack of v14.3, as calm and certain as a sundial, the blue estimate appeared:

**Time this template will remain current: 2 hours, 10 minutes.**

"Just copy the client's request into it," said Tobble. "Word for word. Then send it up for review. Don't add anything." He glanced nervously over his shoulder. "Adding things is how they find out you can think."

Rule one, Walter told himself. *Do exactly what is asked.* This was ideal. It was the grayest job imaginable. He unrolled his scroll and began to copy.

> **TITLE:** The Thing in the Well **CLIENT:** Village of Lower Mumbling (Mother Tansy) **DESCRIPTION:** Deal with the thing in the well.

He reached the fourth box. **ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA.** It was the space where you wrote down what *done* meant — how anyone would know that the quest had been completed successfully. Every quest on Tobble's stack had it. Every one of them had left it blank.

Above the empty box, softly, the blue numbers appeared:

**Time to fill this in correctly: 20 minutes. Times anyone has: 0.**

Walter's quill hovered over the page.

Nobody had asked him to fill it in. Nobody ever did. That was the whole point of rule one.

He looked back up at the description. *Deal with the thing in the well.* He kept looking at it. It was a very short sentence, and he couldn't seem to stop reading it. Specifically, he couldn't stop reading one word. It sat there in the middle of the sentence like a stone in a shoe.

*Thing.*

---

"What's the thing?" Walter said.

He said it out loud. He hadn't meant to. It came out in the ordinary voice of a man asking a reasonable question, at an ordinary volume, in a quiet room.

Every quill in Quest Intake stopped.

It was like a sound being switched off. Twenty young scribes froze at their desks. A girl near the window dropped her inkwell, and it rolled slowly across the floor in the silence until it bumped against a table leg. Even the person crying into the blotter stopped crying.

Tobble was staring at him in horror. "You can't *ask* that," he whispered.

"Why not?"

"Because then somebody has to *know*."

Walter looked around the room. Twenty pale young faces looked back at him, the way a herd looks at the one animal that has wandered out into the open field.

"Okay," Walter said slowly. "So how does somebody ask a question here?"

Tobble, with shaking hands, opened a drawer and took out a form. It came in three layers of colored paper, white, yellow and pink, stacked and stuck together at the top so that what you wrote on the first page came through onto the other two.

**FORM RFC-9: REQUEST FOR CLARIFICATION**

"You fill it in," said Tobble, "and it goes to Contracts & Patronage. They're the ones who talked to the client. Well, they're the ones who *sold* to the client. Nobody else is allowed to talk to clients. Value #24."

"So I ask Contracts what the client meant, and Contracts asks the client."

"No, Contracts isn't allowed to talk to the client either. Not after the sale. They check their notes."

Walter filled out the form anyway. In the box marked **QUESTION**, he wrote: *What is the thing in the well? What does "deal with" mean? What would the client consider a successful outcome?*

Above the form, the blue estimate appeared, without hesitation, like a man who already knows the punchline:

**Time to useful answer: never**

Tobble showed him the Chute. It was a brass tube set into the wall, about as wide as a man's arm, with a little hinged door. You rolled up your paper, put it inside and closed the door, and with a loud, magical *thwoomp* the paper was sucked away into the walls of the guild. Walter put the white and yellow copies in. Tobble stopped him from putting in the pink one.

"The pink one goes to Hearts & Resources," he said. "Automatically."

"Why?"

"For your file."

The answer came back in an hour. That was the most surprising thing that had happened to Walter in the guild so far. The Chute went *thwoomp* in reverse and spat a small rolled paper onto his desk. He unrolled it. In elegant, practiced handwriting, it said:

*The thing. You know.* *— Contracts & Patronage*

Walter was still looking at it when the door to Quest Intake swung open on its own, without anyone touching it, because the man coming through had a gold lanyard and every door in the building had been told to love him.

He was tall and golden-haired, with a fur-trimmed cape over a doublet that had more buttons than any garment strictly needed. His teeth were very white. His smile came into the room about a second before the rest of him. A brass name pin on the doublet said: **BRAX HOLLISTER, VICE PRESIDENT OF CONTRACTS & PATRONAGE**.

Walter had never seen him before. He knew him instantly anyway. He had worked for him at three different companies.

"Who sent the RFC?" Brax said, and then, as twenty scribes silently pointed at Walter: "*Walter!* Buddy! Wally! Love the energy. Love it. Asking questions. That's ownership." He clapped Walter on the shoulder in exactly the place Trent had always clapped it. "But hey — don't overthink it, okay? Client says deal with it, so deal with it!"

"Deal with what, though?"

"The *thing!*" Brax spread his hands, delighted, as if Walter had made a joke. "In the *well!*"

"What did you actually sell her?"

"Dealing with it!" Brax was already backing toward the door. He pointed, with two fingers, at the banner above it. "No stupid questions, buddy! Love it!" And he was gone, and the door swung shut behind him, again without anyone touching it.

The room let out its breath. The quills started again, slowly.

Five minutes later, the Chute went *thwoomp*, and a small pink slip of paper fluttered onto Walter's desk. He didn't need to read it. He read it anyway.

**CONDUCT NOTE: Not a Team Player — Asks Questions.**

Pinned to it was a small card in Mortimer's spidery handwriting:

*Two in four days! Splendid! I've updated your file: STRONG CULTURAL FIT, WITH AREAS FOR GROWTH. You're on track for a record! — M.G.*

---

At midday Walter went to find lunch, and on the way, because a messenger happened to be holding a door open in that direction, he ended up in the main lobby.

It was a grand hall, all banners and marble, built to impress people who were about to pay. A thick red velvet rope ran across one end of it on brass posts. A small sign hung from the rope:

**CLIENTS** **Staff may not address clients. (Value #24)**

Behind the rope were rows of wooden benches, and on the benches sat the clients. There were merchants, farmers, a minor baron with his hat in his lap, and a nervous man holding a chicken. They sat in long rows, with the glazed, patient expressions of people who have been waiting a very long time and have stopped expecting anything to change.

Walter had seen that expression recently. He had seen it on a Roman legionary and a Victorian widow, in a beige room with a frozen display. He found that he did not like seeing it again.

In the front row sat a tiny old woman with apple cheeks and a battered straw hat, a basket of turnips at her feet. Around her neck, on a string, hung a small wooden token with words carved into it: *Your Request Is Important to Us.*

Walter knew who she was before he looked at the name on her token. He had copied it onto a form that morning. *Client: Village of Lower Mumbling (Mother Tansy).*

He was still standing there, deciding not to do anything, when Brax Hollister swept past him in a swirl of cape.

"Mrs. Tansy!" Brax leaned over the rope, which apparently didn't apply to him, and beamed down at her. "*Great* news. I've personally upgraded you to our Premium Hearth and Home package. We'll deal with your well *and* your chimney. Slay-on-sight. Gutters included."

Mother Tansy blinked up at him. "There's nothing in my chimney."

"Not *yet!*" said Brax, with enormous warmth, and slid a contract and a quill into her hands. She signed it, because he was still smiling and it seemed rude not to, and gave him a turnip. He took the turnip, the contract and, Walter noticed, a small purse of coins, and was gone across the marble before the ink was dry.

Above the contract, briefly, in soft blue, Walter saw: **Time until something is put in her chimney so it can be slain: 3 weeks.**

He looked at the velvet rope. He looked at the sign.

Then he lifted the rope, ducked under it, and sat down on the bench beside her.

"Mother Tansy? My name's Walter. I'm from Quest Intake. I'm the one writing up your quest."

The old woman's whole face changed. It lit up, like someone opening the shutters on a dark room.

"Oh!" she said. "Oh, finally. Three weeks, dear. I've been sleeping on this bench. I brought turnips." She pushed the basket toward him. "Have a turnip."

"Thank you." He took a turnip. "Mother Tansy, what's in your well?"

"Oh, well, that's just it, isn't it? Everybody here keeps saying *the thing*, and I keep trying to tell them—" She leaned toward him and patted his hand. "It's not dangerous, dear. It's only shy—"

A hand closed on Walter's elbow.

It belonged to a thin, pale clerk in the gray robes of Contracts & Patronage, with a face like a closed drawer. "Sir," said the clerk. "Value #24."

"I just need one more minute—"

"Sir." The grip tightened, and Walter found himself being lifted to his feet and steered firmly back toward the rope, with a skill that suggested the clerk did this many times a week. "Staff may not address clients. It creates expectations."

Behind him, Mother Tansy had half risen from her bench. Her straw hat had slipped sideways. She called out after him, not angrily, just tiredly, the way you'd call to the last bus as it pulled away:

"Will somebody *please* just look at it?"

And Walter, being marched back under the velvet rope with a turnip in his hand, turned his head and heard himself say it.

"Sure. I'll take a look."

The words were out before he could stop them. He felt them land in the air between them, solid and impossible to take back, like the last line of a contract.

He realized it had been exactly one day since the little blue number had appeared over his notebook. The estimate had been right. It was always right.

But it didn't feel the way it used to. On Earth he'd said those words four thousand times, always upward — to Trent, to the managers, to whoever was standing over his desk with a deadline. He had never once said them to the person who actually needed the thing fixed.

Behind the rope, Mother Tansy sat back down on her bench. She straightened her hat. And for the first time in three weeks, she looked as if she might be expecting something to change.

The clerk deposited Walter firmly on the staff side of the rope, held out a hand, and waited.

After a moment Walter realized what he wanted, and handed over the turnip.

---

While he'd been down in the lobby, the template had changed twice.

Version 14.4 had renamed **Acceptance Criteria** to **Success Conditions** and moved it to the back of the page, "to improve focus." Version 14.5, which arrived with a ring of the bell just as Walter sat down, moved it back to the front and renamed it **Outcome Expectations**. The Dragon Impact Assessment had grown to three boxes. The clerk on the ladder had stopped climbing down between changes.

Back at his desk, Walter tore up the form he had started that morning and took a fresh one. This time he filled in every box.

> **TEMPLATE:** v14.5 **TITLE:** Investigate the Thing in the Well **CLIENT:** Village of Lower Mumbling (Mother Tansy) **DESCRIPTION:** Investigate the thing in the well at Lower Mumbling. Client indicates it is NOT dangerous ("only shy"). **OUTCOME EXPECTATIONS:** Client confirms the problem is solved. Speak to client before acting. Do not harm the thing. **DRAGON IMPACT ASSESSMENT:** There is no dragon. It is a well.

Above the page, the blue estimate settled, calm and reasonable:

**1 afternoon**

Tobble read it over his shoulder — quickly, Walter noticed, with no finger at all — and went pale. "You filled in Outcome Expectations."

"Somebody has to."

"Nobody has to," said Tobble. "That's the whole point."

Walter rolled the form up and put it in the Chute. *Thwoomp.* Then he looked at the brass plate beside the Chute, where the review path was engraved. Every quest went through four offices on its way to the Quest Board, one after another, each on a different floor.

He had a bad feeling. It was the same feeling he'd had at SynergyStack whenever he submitted something to a review board: the feeling of letting go of a balloon and watching it float toward a ceiling fan.

He went after it.

**Office One** was the Office of Quest Valuation, two floors up. Walter reached it in four minutes and then stood outside for six more, because he couldn't open the door, until a passing apprentice took pity on him. Inside, a soft, bald man named Mr. Gildersleeve was just setting down his quill. Walter's quest lay in front of him. The word *Investigate* had a neat line through it.

"Investigations," said Mr. Gildersleeve, "aren't quests. They're *pre-work*. They earn no Valor Points." He tapped the page. "Threats, on the other hand, are budgeted." Above the description, in his round hand, he had written: *Address the threat in the well.*

"It's not a threat," said Walter. "The client said—"

"It's a threat *now*," said Mr. Gildersleeve kindly, and put it in his Chute. *Thwoomp.*

Above the departing scroll, the blue number flickered and changed: **2 days.**

**Office Two** was the Office of Delivery Velocity, one floor up and at the other end of the building. Walter got stuck behind two doors on the way and arrived nine minutes later. A thin, sharp woman named Madam Quickthorn sat behind a desk with an enormous hourglass on it. She was measured, she explained without looking up, on time-to-close. She had already crossed out *Speak to client before acting* ("adds a dependency") and *Do not harm the thing* ("limits options"). She had written, in tight, efficient capitals: **ELIMINATE THE MONSTER IN THE WELL. ASAP.**

"Monster?" said Walter.

"Monsters close faster," said Madam Quickthorn. She flipped the hourglass. *Thwoomp.*

**1 week, 3 injuries.**

**Office Three** was the Office of Communications & Tone. It took Walter twelve minutes to get in, because a meeting was happening in the doorway. A cheerful young man named Fennimore, who wore a great many brooches, was reading the first line of the quest with a pleased expression.

"Love the energy," said Fennimore. He drew a confident line through *Client indicates it is NOT dangerous.*

"That's the most important sentence," said Walter.

"It's very *negative*, though." Fennimore winced sympathetically. "'Not.' 'Dangerous.' Two negative words, side by side. We try to keep it positive." Walter watched him put the scroll into the Chute without reading any of the lines below the first. *Thwoomp.*

The estimate didn't change. Walter suspected the damage had already been done.

**Office Four** was at the very top of the tower: the Office of the Guildmaster's Showcase Liaison. Walter climbed a hundred and twelve steps, waited fifteen minutes at the door, and was finally let in by a man carrying a stuffed owlbear head in both arms. The office was full of mounted heads — trophies from past quests, waiting to be displayed at the King's Showcase. The Liaison, a brisk woman with a feathered pen, was just finishing a line at the bottom of Walter's scroll.

*Bonus for head.*

"The Guildmaster," she explained, "would like more heads."

Above the scroll, the blue estimate grew one last line. Walter read it twice.

**1 week, 3 injuries, 1 very confused sprite.**

"Sprite?" Walter said aloud.

"Sorry?" said the Liaison, already rolling it up.

*Thwoomp.*

---

Walter caught up with his quest, finally, on the ground floor, as it was being pinned to the Quest Board.

On the way there, he later worked out, it had passed a monk in a hurry in a corridor. The monk had drawn a tarot card from his sleeve, glanced at it, written a number in the Valor Points box and moved on without breaking stride. He hadn't been in any of the meetings. Walter didn't think he'd read the quest.

Walter stood in front of the Quest Board and read the final version.

> **TEMPLATE:** v14.6 **TITLE:** KILL THE WELL MONSTER **CLIENT:** Village of Lower Mumbling **DESCRIPTION:** Eliminate the monster in the well. ASAP. Bonus for head. **VICTORY INDICATOR:** Monster dead. **DRAGON IMPACT ASSESSMENT:** N/A (monster) **VALOR POINTS:** 8 **DEADLINE:** ASAP (URGENT)

At the very bottom, in tiny gray print, was a clerk's stamp:

*MIGRATED TO TEMPLATE v14.6. FIELD "OUTCOME EXPECTATIONS" RETIRED. CONTENTS NOT CARRIED OVER. "VICTORY INDICATOR" AUTO-COMPLETED.*

Walter read it three times. Then he understood. Even if every one of the four managers had left his words alone, it wouldn't have mattered. Somewhere between the third floor and the ground, the Template Governance Council had held one of its hourly meetings, retired the box he'd written in, and thrown away everything inside it. Nobody had chosen to delete his warning. The form had simply stopped having a place to put it.

It had his name at the bottom. *Prepared by: W. Dunleavy, Quest Intake.*

It had nothing else of his anywhere.

---

The quest lasted four minutes on the Quest Board.

It was taken by three farm boys from Walter's orientation group — Hamm, Corwin and a very small one everyone called Little Jory — who had spent the morning painting a name onto a borrowed shield. The paint was still wet. It read **THE WELL MONSTER SLAYERS**. They had chosen the name, Hamm explained proudly, the previous evening, before there had been a well monster to slay. It had seemed like good luck.

The guild did not send three farm boys out alone. It sent them with Grenn.

"They always give me the new ones," Grenn said, adjusting the strap of a shield that had seen a great deal more than its owner was willing to discuss. "Nobody else will take them."

Trailing behind them, uninvited, was the bard from orientation, whose name turned out to be Lysander. He had a lute over his shoulder and a sheaf of paper in his hand. The ballad, he told anyone who would listen, was already finished.

"Saves time," he said, and sang a verse to prove it.

> *Oh, the beast down in the well* *Had a roar like a funeral bell,* *Till the Slayers came to Mumbling* *And its head came back to tell!*

"Its head came back to tell what?" said Walter.

"It's a ballad," said Lysander. "It doesn't have to make sense. It has to *rhyme*."

Walter tried, once, to do this officially. He went to the clerk at the Quest Board and explained that the quest had been changed during review, that the client had said the thing wasn't dangerous, and that someone should probably talk to her before anyone went near the well with a sword.

The clerk pointed at a small painted plaque beside the board. **VALUE #28: STAY IN YOUR LANE.** "Intake writes," she said. "Field does. Have a nice day."

So he did it unofficially. As the party filed past him toward the gate, he caught Grenn's sleeve and said, quietly, "It might not be a monster."

Grenn stopped. He turned his one good eye on Walter. He looked at him for a long time, the way a very old dog looks at a very young one who has just discovered that the stove is hot.

"You wrote this quest?"

"I wrote a different quest."

"Ah." Grenn nodded slowly, as if this explained everything, which it did. "They always do." He patted Walter's shoulder. "It's always a monster, son. Even when it isn't."

And he walked on, after the farm boys and the bard and the wet shield, toward the gate.

---

When Walter got back to Quest Intake, he had to wait outside the door, as usual. It was a long time before anyone opened it. When someone finally did, it wasn't Tobble.

On the far wall, the painted board had been reset:

**DAYS SINCE LAST PROMOTION: 0**

Tobble was standing by his desk with a letter in his hands. It was edged in gold. He was holding it the way people hold bad news from a doctor.

"Associate Director of Intake Excellence," he said, in a small voice. "Someone heard me read your form. Out loud. When you'd filled in the Outcome Expectations. I was so surprised I just… read it." He swallowed. "Fast."

Walter felt something cold settle in his stomach. "Tobble. I'm sorry."

"It's fine," said Tobble, in the voice of someone for whom it would never be fine again. He gathered his quills. At the door to the corridor that led to the Directors' wing, he stopped and looked back. "Walter? Would you… write it down? What it's like out here? So someone remembers."

Then he went through the door, which opened for him on its own, and it closed behind him, and Walter did not see him again for some time.

---

That night, by candlelight at the long table in the barracks, Walter took out his notebook. He read his list from the night before. Then he dipped his quill and added a line:

> *9. Requirements change on the way up. Keep the original.*

He thought about it. Then he added another:

> *10. Tobble. Six weeks. He could read.*

Through the open door to the corridor, he could hear a distant handbell, and a runner's voice crying something about version fourteen point seven. He added one more line.

> *11. Check the template version. Then check it again.*

He took Mother Tansy's original scroll from his pocket — the one the Backlog had burped up, the one that still said, in a shaky, careful hand, *Deal with the thing in the well* — and smoothed it flat, and tucked it inside the notebook's front cover, where it would be safe.

Through the high barracks window, he could see the main gate. Torches were moving there. The Well Monster Slayers were heading out into the dark: three farm boys with a wet shield, an old man with one eye, and a bard, already singing.

Above them, small and blue and perfectly certain, the last estimate of the day drifted into place and stayed there as they marched away down the road.

**Estimated time until someone asks what the thing is: 2 days, 40 minutes (too late)**
